The Los Angeles car market right now

On November 15, 2026, dealers within 50 miles of downtown Los Angeles listed about 150,000 new cars and 99,000 used ones, spread across roughly 650 franchised dealerships. About one new car in six was a Toyota; Honda and Ford came next. The median asking price was about $48,400 new and $24,200 used.

Fifty miles from downtown reaches Orange County, the San Fernando Valley and the western Inland Empire. On a map those dealers are neighbors. In traffic, a store in Ontario and a store in Torrance might as well be in different cities, which is why most people only ever get a price from the two or three dealers closest to home. We don’t have to drive anywhere. For a common car like a RAV4, dozens of stores in that circle hold the same thing, and each one gets asked for a written out-the-door price.

Buying a car around Los Angeles

Tell us how long you’d drive

A 50-mile circle on a map says little about the drive. The same trip can take far longer on a weekday afternoon than on a Sunday morning, whether you’re on the 405, the 10, the 5 or the 101. Tell us where you’d start, when you’d go and the longest drive you’d make, and we ask the dealers inside that window.

Look at what the sun did to a used car

Los Angeles sun wears a car from above. Look for a dashboard top that has cracked or gone chalky, clear coat peeling on the roof and hood, headlight lenses turned yellow or cloudy, and dry, cracked rubber trim and tire sidewalls. A car parked outside for years shows more of it than one kept in a garage. On a used search we can ask the dealer for photos of those spots. Have a mechanic you choose look the car over before you sign.

How it works from Los Angeles

  1. You pay the flat fee and tell us the car. Model, trims and colors you’d accept, budget, and how far you’d go to pick it up. Mason replies within one business day to confirm the search and when it starts.
  2. We find the cars. Your advisor searches new, used and CPO dealer inventory for the model.
  3. We get written offers and negotiate. Each dealer is asked for an out-the-door price in writing. Offers go against each other, and add-ons and padded fees get questioned before you see a contract.
  4. You pick. We send written recommendations and our pick of the best deal. You make the final call, sign the paperwork and pick up the car from the dealer.

Most searches take one to two weeks from payment to a signed deal. Rare specs and factory orders take longer, and we tell you that up front. We can’t promise a specific discount: the market for a given car sets what a dealer will do. The full process is on how it works.

What stays with you

You decide which car, if any, to buy. You arrange the inspection, pick the financing and insurance, read the contract and sign it. Our fee does not include the car, taxes, registration, inspection, financing, insurance or delivery. We don’t give tax or registration advice for your state; the dealer’s paperwork and your state’s motor vehicle agency are the sources for that.

Common questions

Do you have an office in Los Angeles?

No. We’re based in Austin, Texas, and we work with Los Angeles-area dealers by phone and email. You deal with one advisor the whole way, and you pick up the car from the dealer.

Will you contact dealers in Orange County and the Inland Empire too?

Yes. Tell us how far you’d go to pick up the car and we ask dealers across that area. They are all well within 200 miles of you, so every plan covers them.

Can you limit the search to dealers within a certain drive time?

Yes. Tell us where you’d start and the longest drive you’d make, and we ask the dealers inside that window. If a dealer farther out sends a lower written offer, you decide whether it’s worth the trip.

Can you tell if a used car has sun damage?

Only from photos. We can ask the dealer for pictures of the dashboard, the paint, the headlights and the tires, and send them to you. Have a mechanic you choose look the car over before you sign.
