The bottom line

New, as a 2026

The Corolla is the sensible small car: a Hybrid LE is rated 50 mpg combined, and it loses 9.3% of its value in three years by iSeeCars’ count. It’s slow and short on trunk space, and Edmunds ranks it ninth of 10 compact sedans. Price a 2026 Hybrid LE and aim for 3–4% under MSRP.

CPO (Toyota Certified Gold)

Best when: You want a 2021 to 2025 with a warranty, and you’re paying cash.

Check: That it’s Gold Certified, not Silver. Gold adds a 12-month/12,000-mile comprehensive warranty and 7-year/100,000-mile powertrain coverage; Silver gives 12 months of powertrain cover only. A hybrid’s battery stays covered for 10 years/150,000 miles from first sale.

Used, not certified

Best when: Your budget is under about $20,000.

Check: Open recalls by VIN, an independent inspection, and on a 2019 or 2020, that the fuel pump and the stall software were done (Toyota covers that pump for 15 years).

If you buy used, skip the 2020. It drew 316 NHTSA owner complaints, more than any Corolla since, and needed a stalling fix and a fuel pump recall. Check any used Corolla’s open recalls by VIN.

What to watch for at the dealer

  • Dealer add-ons. Toyota dealers put them on 37.7% of the quotes CarWhere studied, averaging $1,637. Paint protection, nitrogen and etched VINs are the dealer’s products: refuse or negotiate them.
  • The model year. A 2026 and a 2027 look alike on the lot. The 2027 costs $200 more, and its ToyotaCare covers one year or 10,000 miles against two.
  • Toyota financing. The 4.99% and the $500 rebates need Toyota Financial Services. Compare the total cost with your bank’s rate.
  • The wrong hybrid. An AWD Hybrid SE is rated 44 mpg combined against 50 for a front-drive Hybrid LE. Check the window sticker if fuel economy is why you’re buying.
  • The headlight recall. NHTSA recall 26V110000 covers 2026 Corollas with incorrectly aimed headlights. Ask the dealer to show it’s done before you take delivery.

Is the Toyota Corolla a good car?

Reviewers split. U.S. News scores it 8.6 out of 10 and KBB 4.5 out of 5, while Edmunds rates it 6.0 and ninth of 10 compact sedans. Consumer Reports expects it to be more reliable than the average new car.

How much under MSRP can you get a Toyota Corolla?

On a 2026 gas LE or SE, 5–6% under MSRP is fair and 7% or more strong. Hybrids sell closer to sticker, so 3–4% is fair. That’s our read of TrueCar and CarWhere figures, so a dealer may land a point either side.

Should I buy a 2026 or a 2027 Corolla?

The 2026, for most buyers. It costs $200 less with the same engines and keeps two years or 25,000 miles of ToyotaCare maintenance, which Toyota cut to one year or 10,000 miles for the 2027.

Is the Corolla Hybrid worth it?

For most buyers, yes. A Hybrid LE costs $1,850 more than a gas LE and is rated 50 mpg combined against 35, about 100 fewer gallons a year at 12,000 miles. Toyota covers the hybrid battery for 10 years or 150,000 miles.

What is the destination charge on a Corolla?

$1,295 on the 2026 and the 2027, sedan, Hybrid, Hatchback or GR Corolla. Toyota’s advertised starting prices leave it out, and Toyota says it’s higher in AL, AR, FL, GA, LA, MS, NC, OK, SC and TX.

Does Toyota offer cash back on the Corolla?

No. On November 16, 2026, Toyota offered 4.99% financing for 48 months and lease deals, but no customer cash. The only money off is a $500 military or college rebate when you finance or lease through Toyota Financial Services.

How much is a GR Corolla?

The 2026 starts at $40,520 with the manual and $42,520 with the automatic, and the Premium Plus is $46,565 and $48,565, all before the $1,295 destination charge. Edmunds found it selling about $1,500 under sticker in November.

Is a used Corolla a better deal than new?

Rarely. A used 2025 asks a median $22,016 at about 34,000 miles, and TrueCar’s average paid for a new 2026 LE is $22,665 with destination. Used loans averaged 11.19% against Toyota’s 4.99%. A Gold Certified 2021 to 2025 is the used route we’d take.
