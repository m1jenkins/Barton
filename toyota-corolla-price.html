The Corolla is the sensible small car most people expect: rated 50 mpg combined as a Hybrid LE, five stars overall from NHTSA, and it loses value slowly (9.3% in three years by iSeeCars’ count, against 24.8% for the average vehicle). It isn’t quick or roomy, though. Edmunds rates it 6.0 out of 10 and ninth of 10 compact sedans, faulting its 8.8-second run to 60 mph, highway noise and a 13.1-cubic-foot trunk. The trim we’d buy

A 2026 Hybrid LE. At $24,975 it costs $1,850 more than a gas LE with the same equipment, and it’s rated 50 mpg combined against 35. Over 12,000 miles a year, that’s about 100 fewer gallons of gas. You keep the wireless CarPlay, blind-spot monitor and 7-inch digital gauges that every 2026 Corolla gets. AWD adds $1,400 and costs 2 mpg (48 combined), which only makes sense if you see snow.

Step up to the Hybrid XLE ($29,340) if you want heated SofTex seats, a moonroof and the 10.5-inch screen. Skip the Hybrid SE: its 18-inch wheels drop it to 47 mpg combined for $2,440 more than the LE. If you need a hatch, the Hatchback SE ($24,580) is gas-only and rated 35 mpg combined.

GR Corolla: the 300-hp one

The GR Corolla shares the hatchback’s body and little else: a 300-hp turbocharged 1.6-liter three-cylinder, all-wheel drive that can send up to 70% of the power to the rear, and Torsen limited-slip differentials front and rear. Car and Driver gives the 2026 a 9.5 out of 10 and an Editors’ Choice; Edmunds rates it 6.9 and fourth of five hot hatches, marking it down for a plain Corolla cabin, highway noise and the smallest cargo area of its rivals (17.8 cubic feet).

What to watch for at the dealer

  • Dealer add-ons. Toyota dealers put add-ons on 37.7% of the quotes CarWhere studied, averaging $1,637. That’s a big share of a $25,000 car’s discount. Paint protection, nitrogen and etched VINs are the dealer’s products. Refuse or negotiate them.
  • The model year. A 2026 and a 2027 look alike on the lot. The 2027 costs $200 more, and its ToyotaCare covers one year or 10,000 miles against two years or 25,000.
  • The Toyota Financial Services requirement. The 4.99% and the $500 rebates need Toyota’s own financing. Compare the total cost with your bank’s rate.
  • Destination. Toyota’s $1,295 charge is the same at every dealer, except in AL, AR, FL, GA, LA, MS, NC, OK, SC and TX, where Toyota says it’s higher. Compare out-the-door prices.
  • The wrong hybrid. An AWD Hybrid SE is rated 44 mpg combined against 50 for a front-drive Hybrid LE. Check the window sticker if fuel economy is why you’re buying.
  • The headlight recall. NHTSA recall 26V110000 covers 2026 Corollas with incorrectly aimed headlights. Ask the dealer to show it’s done before you take delivery.

Common questions

Is the Toyota Corolla a good car?

Reviewers split. U.S. News scores it 8.6 out of 10 and named it a finalist for 2026 Best Compact Car for the Money, and KBB gives it 4.5 out of 5. Edmunds rates it 6.0 and ninth of 10 compact sedans, and Car and Driver (7.5 out of 10) prefers the Civic and Mazda 3. Consumer Reports expects it to be more reliable than the average new car, and a three-year-old Corolla has lost 9.3% of its value by iSeeCars’ figures, against 24.8% for the average vehicle.

How much under MSRP can you get a Toyota Corolla?

On a 2026 gas LE or SE, 5–6% under MSRP is a fair deal and 7% or more is a strong one. Hybrids sell closer to sticker, so 3–4% is fair, and the XSE and Hatchback about 2–3%. Those ranges are our read of TrueCar’s November averages and CarWhere’s verified buyers (a median 4.6% under), so a given dealer may land a point either side. Toyota has no cash back to add on top.

Should I buy a 2026 or a 2027 Corolla?

The 2026, for most buyers. It costs $200 less with the same engines, and it keeps two years or 25,000 miles of ToyotaCare maintenance, which Toyota cut to one year or 10,000 miles for the 2027. Wait for the 2027 if you want the Hatchback XSE’s bigger screens or the 60th Anniversary Hybrid (2,500 built, due early 2027).

Is the Corolla Hybrid worth it?

For most buyers, yes. A Hybrid LE costs $1,850 more than a gas LE and is rated 50 mpg combined against 35, about 100 fewer gallons a year at 12,000 miles. Edmunds tells shoppers to look at the hybrid first. Toyota covers the hybrid battery for 10 years or 150,000 miles and the other hybrid parts for 8 years or 100,000.

What is the destination charge on a Corolla?

$1,295 on the 2026 and the 2027, sedan, Hybrid, Hatchback or GR Corolla. It was $1,195 when the 2026 launched. Toyota’s advertised starting prices leave it out, and Toyota says it’s higher in AL, AR, FL, GA, LA, MS, NC, OK, SC and TX.

Does Toyota offer cash back on the Corolla?

No. In Chicago, Dallas, Los Angeles and New York on November 16, 2026, Toyota offered 4.99% financing for 48 months and lease deals, but no customer cash. The only money off is a $500 military or college rebate when you finance or lease through Toyota Financial Services.

How much is a GR Corolla?

The 2026 starts at $40,520 with the manual and $42,520 with the automatic; the Premium Plus is $46,565 and $48,565, all before the $1,295 destination charge. Dealers no longer mark it up: Edmunds found it selling about $1,500 under sticker in November. The two-seat GRMN Corolla is $64,360, with 730 built worldwide.

Is a used Corolla a better deal than new?

Rarely. CarWhere’s median asking price for a used 2025 is $22,016 at about 34,000 miles, and TrueCar’s average paid for a new 2026 LE is $22,665 with destination. Used loans averaged 11.19% in the second quarter of 2026, against Toyota’s 4.99% on a new one. A Gold Certified 2021 to 2025 is the used route we’d take.
