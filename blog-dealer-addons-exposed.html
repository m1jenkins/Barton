Dealer Add-ons Exposed: 5 Fees You Should Never Pay

By Mason • Finance Tactics

After negotiating the vehicle price, you may have reduced it by $1,800. You may then be sent to the Finance and Insurance (F&I) office, where the dealer presents financing terms, temporary-tag paperwork, and optional products. Review each charge separately before signing.

This room does more than print temporary tags. It is where dealers often present add-ons and financing products after the vehicle price has been negotiated. Those products may be presented as essential and folded into the monthly payment. Review each one as a separate line item.

The Phantom Five

Unless you requested them, treat the following five line items as optional; some carry margins above 800%. Ask for each charge on the buyer's order and remove anything you do not want before signing.

1. VIN Etching ($299 - $399)

This process etches the Vehicle Identification Number onto the corner of the vehicle's glass. Ask what insurance benefit applies and compare the charge with an inexpensive DIY kit. If the etching is already installed, decide whether you want to pay for it before accepting the vehicle.

2. Nitrogen-Filled Tires ($199)

Nitrogen may offer small pressure-stability benefits, but standard compressed air is already about 78% nitrogen and works for ordinary driving. Decline the charge unless the written terms give you a reason to choose it.

3. Fabric Protection / Paint Sealant ($800 - $1,500)

Often branded as "Environmental Protection Packages," these products may combine a basic paint sealant with fabric treatment. Ask what work is performed, which products are used, and what the warranty covers before comparing the price with an independent detailer.

4. Pinstriping ($150 - $400)

Pinstriping is usually vinyl tape applied along the vehicle's shoulder line. It does not add a functional safety or maintenance benefit, so treat it as an optional appearance item and compare its price with doing without it.

5. Documentation / "Prep" Fees Variations ($500 - $1,200)

While a state-regulated Documentation Fee is unavoidable in regions like Texas (though heavily capped), dealers regularly layer "Prep" or "Handling" fees masquerading as administrative necessity. The factory has already compensated the dealership to prepare the car under the invoice holdback column. You are paying twice for a standard car wash and the removal of plastic seat covers.

"The finance officer's primary objective isn't structuring your loan; it is maximizing the collateral attached to it."

Reviewing these charges means asking for an itemized buyer's order and declining products you do not want. If you want help reviewing the F&I paperwork, Drive Right can compare the line items before you sign. We serve buyers across Austin, Dallas, Houston, and all of Texas.
