Best Cars Under $30K in 2026 (Expert Picks)

By Mason • Vehicle Picks • Updated November 21, 2026

Most new cars under $30,000 are compact sedans and small crossovers, plus one hybrid pickup and one EV. We count destination in every price below, because it’s on every window sticker, and that moves several popular cars over the line: a Toyota Camry LE is $29,600 before a $1,295 destination charge, or $30,895.

The picks come from price with destination, published scores and fuel economy. Prices were checked on November 21, 2026; they come from the makers’ price lists, the price guides linked below and U.S. News.

Best Overall Value: Mazda3

The Mazda3 (sedan or hatchback) starts at $24,650 before destination for the 2.5 S, and U.S. News ranks it second of eight compact cars. The Preferred, at $27,290 before a $1,235 destination charge, is $28,525 and still leaves $1,475 for options. The naturally aspirated 2.5-liter engine is the only one that fits the budget: the Turbo Premium Plus starts at $36,940.

The catch is options: the upper trims, such as the Carbon Edition ($29,815 before destination), cross $30,000 once destination is added. See our analysis of why Mazda deserves a closer look. If you want one recommendation under $30K, this is ours.

Best Compact SUV: Mazda CX-30 or Honda HR-V

U.S. News ranks the 2026 Mazda CX-30 first of 16 subcompact SUVs. The 2.5 S starts at $26,375 and the Select Sport at $28,060, both before destination, so the Select Sport leaves roughly $1,900 for the fee. The Preferred, at $29,690, doesn’t fit.

The 2027 Honda HR-V costs $26,600 for the LX, or $28,050 with its $1,450 destination charge, and $29,850 for the Sport. U.S. News ranks it tenth of 16 in the class,. All-wheel drive adds $1,500. Pick the CX-30 on the scores, and price the HR-V against it. If you want more room than either, our ranking of compact SUVs starts at the Toyota RAV4, which costs $33,495 for the cheapest hybrid LE.

Best Sedan: Honda Civic or Toyota Camry

The Civic is a compact, and it’s the sedan that fits. An LX is $24,695, or $25,990 with destination, and the Sport is $27,990. Honda’s Sport Hybrid, our pick in the Civic price guide, is $30,690, so it’s $690 over. For the hybrid-versus-gas question against the Corolla, see Civic versus Corolla.

A Camry LE is $30,895 with destination, $895 over the budget, so you need a discount to fit it. Our Camry price guide has the trim prices and the discount we think is fair. The Camry versus Accord page covers the midsize decision.

Best for Texas Commuters: Toyota Corolla Hybrid

If your daily drive includes 45 minutes on I-35 in August with the A/C running, the Corolla Hybrid is worth considering. The 2026 Hybrid LE costs $24,975, or $26,270 with destination, and it’s rated 50 mpg combined. All-wheel drive adds $1,400, which still leaves it under $28,000. Ask for current battery coverage and maintenance terms rather than assuming every hybrid ages the same way.

The price leaves $3,730 under the line for the higher trims that add safety and comfort features: the Hybrid SE is $27,415, or $28,710 with destination. Our Corolla price guide lists every trim.

Best Truck Under $30K: Ford Maverick

A reasonably equipped F-150 or Tacoma now routinely exceeds $40,000: an F-150 XLT is $47,490 with destination, and a Tacoma TRD Sport is $42,160. Ford’s 2027 Maverick XL is the one new pickup that fits under $30,000, at $29,990 with a $1,845 destination charge. It’s a front-drive hybrid with a four-door cab, rated 38 mpg combined, and it tows 2,000 pounds as standard. AWD adds $3,395 and puts it over.

The Hyundai Santa Cruz no longer fits: the 2027 SE is $30,300 before a $1,650 destination charge, or $31,950, and it’s the model’s last year. Neither will tow a 10,000-pound boat. If your honest truck needs involve hauling mulch and moving furniture, the Maverick covers them for a lot less than a body-on-frame truck. See our ranking of the cheapest new pickups for the rest of the field.

Best EV Under $30K: Chevrolet Bolt

The $7,500 federal EV tax credit is gone, so the EV prices below are sticker prices. The 2027 Chevrolet Bolt LT is $27,600 before a $1,395 destination charge, or $28,995, and U.S. News lists 262 miles of range. The Nissan Leaf S+ is $29,990 before a $1,495 destination charge, or $31,485, and the Chevrolet Equinox EV starts at $35,395 before destination.

That makes the Bolt the only new EV we found under the line. It makes sense if your daily drive is short and you can charge at home: U.S. News puts a 120-volt household outlet at 4 miles of range per hour, so a 240-volt charger is the realistic plan. If you regularly drive Austin to Dallas, a hybrid is the more practical choice. We recommend reading our breakdown of the real pros, cons, and risks of buying an EV before committing.

Compare ownership costs

The window-sticker price is only the start of a five-year budget. Insurance premiums vary even when purchase prices are similar, and depreciation matters most: iSeeCars’ figures put the average new vehicle at a 24.8% loss over three years, against 13.8% for a Toyota RAV4 Hybrid.

A car that costs $28,000 to buy and $6,000 a year to own costs more than one that costs $30,000 and $4,500 a year, so the price on the sheet isn’t the whole comparison. See our guide to comparing reliability by model before making the calculation.
