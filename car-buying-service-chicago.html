The Chicago car market right now

On November 15, 2026, dealers within 50 miles of the Loop listed about 99,000 new cars and 89,000 used ones, across roughly 590 franchised dealerships. Ford and Chevrolet had the most new cars listed, then Toyota. The median asking price was about $46,300 new, the lowest of the six metros we checked, and $24,000 used.

Buying a car around Chicago

Look under a used car before you look at its price

Illinois and Indiana salt their roads every winter, and the salt works on a car from below. Brake lines, the exhaust and the rocker panels under the doors go first, often on a car whose paint still looks clean. On a used search we can ask the dealer for photos of the underside. Before you sign, have a mechanic you choose put the car on a lift.

You may not need all-wheel drive

We think front-wheel drive on a set of winter tires gets most city drivers through a Chicago winter, and it usually costs less than the all-wheel-drive version of the same car. All-wheel drive helps a car pull away on snow. It does little for stopping or turning, which is where winter tires earn their keep. If your street is the last one plowed, the extra traction may be worth paying for. If you’d take either, tell us: that brings more cars into the search and more offers to price against each other.

Count the trip to pick it up

You pick up the car at the dealer, so if you live in the city and don’t own a car yet, the trip out is part of the cost. A store in Schaumburg or Merrillville might beat a city dealer by a few hundred dollars and still take most of an afternoon to reach. Tell us how far you’d go, and we ask the dealers you would actually get to.

How it works from Chicago

  1. You pay the flat fee and tell us the car. Model, trims and colors you’d accept, budget, and how far you’d go to pick it up. Mason replies within one business day to confirm the search and when it starts.
  2. We find the cars. Your advisor searches new, used and CPO dealer inventory for the model.
  3. We get written offers and negotiate. Each dealer is asked for an out-the-door price in writing. Offers go against each other, and add-ons and padded fees get questioned before you see a contract.
  4. You pick. We send written recommendations and our pick of the best deal. You make the final call, sign the paperwork and pick up the car from the dealer.

Most searches take one to two weeks from payment to a signed deal. Rare specs and factory orders take longer, and we tell you that up front. We can’t promise a specific discount: the market for a given car sets what a dealer will do. The full process is on how it works.

What stays with you

You decide which car, if any, to buy. You arrange the inspection, pick the financing and insurance, read the contract and sign it. Our fee does not include the car, taxes, registration, inspection, financing, insurance or delivery. We don’t give tax or registration advice for your state; the dealer’s paperwork and your state’s motor vehicle agency are the sources for that.

Common questions

Do you have an office in Chicago?

No. We’re based in Austin, Texas, and we work with Chicago-area dealers by phone and email. You deal with one advisor the whole way, and you pick up the car from the dealer.

Can you include dealers in northwest Indiana?

Yes. For an Illinois buyer they are out-of-state dealers, which Steer Me Right Pro includes. If you live in Indiana, they are covered on either plan.

Do you check used cars for rust?

From photos. We can ask the dealer for pictures of the underside and tell you what we see in them. Photos miss things, so before you sign, have a mechanic you choose put the car on a lift. On a car that has spent a few Chicago winters outside, that hour is worth it.

I live in the city and don’t have a car. How do I pick it up?

At the dealer, like every buyer. Say so when you start, and we weigh how easy each dealer is to reach alongside its price. Some dealers will deliver the car to you; whether they do, and what they charge, is up to them.

Steer Me Right charges the same flat fee everywhere: $395 for one model or $695 for up to three. Most services where a person negotiates for you charge $999 or more, by their own published prices; see car buying services compared.

Do dealers pay you?

No. Dealers and lenders pay us nothing. You pay the flat fee, and you’re the only one we work for.
