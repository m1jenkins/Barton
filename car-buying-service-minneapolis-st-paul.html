The Minneapolis–St. Paul car market right now

The Census Bureau counted 216 new-car dealer locations across the Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington metro area in 2023. That area is fifteen counties, thirteen in Minnesota and two in Wisconsin (Pierce and St. Croix). We found no public price, inventory or registration figure for the Twin Cities that we could cite, so the Census count is the only market figure on this page.

Buying a car around Minneapolis–St. Paul

Look under a used car for road-salt rust

The Minnesota Pollution Control Agency lists vehicles among the things road salt damages. On a car that has spent its winters in the Twin Cities, look for rust on the brake lines, the exhaust and the rocker panels under the doors. On a used search we can ask the dealer for photos of the underside. Before you sign, have a mechanic you choose put the car on a lift.

Ask for a photo of the tread

AAA’s Minneapolis club says tread depth is critical to traction and suggests a quarter test: put the quarter in the groove with Washington’s head down, and if you can see the top of his head, it’s time for new tires. A used car on worn tires means a tire bill right after you buy it. Tell us which cars you’re weighing, and we ask each dealer for a photo of each tire’s tread with a quarter in the groove.

Plan the drive around the express lanes

Driving alone at rush hour without an E-ZPass account, you stay in the general lanes, so a dealer that looks close on the map can be a slow trip. MnDOT runs E-ZPass express lanes on I-394, I-35W and I-35E. Carpools, buses and motorcycles use them free at peak hours, and solo drivers pay a fee from an E-ZPass account. Tell us where you start and when you’d drive, and we weigh dealers by how far you would go.

How it works from Minneapolis–St. Paul

  1. You pay the flat fee and tell us the car. Model, trims and colors you’d accept, budget, and how far you’d go to pick it up. Mason replies within one business day to confirm the search and when it starts.
  2. We find the cars. Your advisor searches new, used and CPO dealer inventory for the model.
  3. We get written offers and negotiate. Each dealer is asked for an out-the-door price in writing. Offers go against each other, and add-ons and padded fees get questioned before you see a contract.
  4. You pick. We send written recommendations and our pick of the best deal. You make the final call, sign the paperwork and pick up the car from the dealer.

Most searches take one to two weeks from payment to a signed deal. Rare specs and factory orders take longer, and we tell you that up front. We can’t promise a specific discount: the market for a given car sets what a dealer will do. The full process is on how it works.

Common questions

Can you include dealers in Wisconsin?

Yes, on any plan. The Steer Me Right plan covers dealers within 200 miles of your ZIP code, and Hudson, River Falls and the other Wisconsin dealers in the circle are well inside that. A Wisconsin buyer gets the Minnesota dealers the same way.

Can you tell if a used car has road-salt rust?

Only from photos. We can ask the dealer for pictures of the underside and send them to you. Photos miss things, so have a mechanic you choose put the car on a lift before you sign, especially on a car that spent its winters outside.

Can you find out how worn a used car’s tires are?

From photos. We can ask the dealer for a picture of each tire’s tread with a quarter in the groove and send them to you. Have a mechanic you choose check the tires before you sign.
