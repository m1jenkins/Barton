# Locking in the deal

Use this once a dealer's OTD is agreed, or when financing, a trade-in, a deposit or the buyer's details come up. Every request to the dealer is a draft for Mason, written by the rules in SKILL.md step 5.

## Buyer's order

Before the buyer visits, ask the dealer to email the buyer's order (purchase order) for the agreed car. Check it line by line against the agreed quote:
- the 17-character VIN of the agreed car
- the sale price, each fee, each add-on (removed or at $0 where agreed), each rebate with its condition, tax, title and registration, and the OTD total
- how long the price holds
- a manager's signature or written acceptance

If the order says "not binding until approved" or "subject to change", the dealer can still change the price. Ask for it to be accepted in writing [1, 2]. When any line differs from the agreed quote, show Mason the difference and draft a request to fix it.

The buyer's name goes on the order. Mason gives it to the dealer once the buyer is ready to buy at this price.

An accepted order is strong evidence of the deal, but it does not guarantee the dealer will honor it [2]. Tell Mason to promise the buyer only this: bring the emailed order and the thread, compare the contract with them line by line at signing, and leave if the numbers change.

## Deposit

The buyer pays a deposit only against a VIN-specific order with the final price on it. If the car is not on the lot yet, the order also needs an arrival date. Never pay a deposit just to have a dealer start looking for a car. Pay by credit card and get the refund terms in writing [1]. A factory order, where no VIN exists yet, is a separate decision for Mason.

## Used cars

Before any deposit or signing:
- Ask the dealer by email to allow an independent pre-purchase inspection at a shop near the dealership, at the buyer's cost. A dealer that refuses is a reason to move on. The buyer-facing checklist is `blog-used-car-inspection-checklist.html`.
- Ask for the FTC Buyers Guide posted on the car. It says whether the car is sold as-is or with a warranty, and Texas allows as-is sales [3].
- Get a history report from an NMVTIS-approved provider, plus Carfax or AutoCheck [4]. A history report does not replace the inspection [3].

## Financing and trade-in

Settle these only after the price is agreed:
- **Financing.** The buyer compares the dealer's APR with their own pre-approval, on the loan's total cost rather than the monthly payment. A dealer can mark up the rate on a loan it arranges [5, 6]. If the agreed price depended on dealer financing, confirm the price still holds with the buyer's own loan. If it does not, the tied rebate must be worth more than the difference in loan cost.
- **Trade-in.** Get the trade-in quoted as its own number, and compare it with an outside offer, such as a CarMax or Carvana appraisal, before the buyer accepts. The agreed sale price and fees stay the same. Only the trade allowance and any tax that depends on it change.

## Finance office

Before the visit, Mason tells the buyer what the finance office will offer: an extended warranty, GAP, paint protection and similar products. Every one is optional. The buyer can decline them all, or buy one later on its own terms [7]. Any product the buyer does want is priced separately, and its price is negotiable.
