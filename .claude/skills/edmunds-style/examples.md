# Examples

Each pair is a weak line and its fix. The cars and figures are made up for illustration ("the Ridgeback" is a full-size pickup, "the Alder" a small hybrid SUV); never reuse these numbers in real copy.

## Verdict first

Weak: Since its introduction in 2021, the fourth-generation Alder has been an important model in the brand's growing lineup of electrified crossovers.

Better: The Alder is the small SUV to buy if fuel economy and cargo room top your list. It's quick for a hybrid and easy to see out of. The seats are the catch: they're firm enough to notice after two hours.

## Claim, proof, payoff

Weak: The Ridgeback features a quick-ratio electric power steering system.

Better: The Ridgeback's steering is quick and direct. That makes a 19-foot truck feel smaller in a parking lot and steadier on the freeway.

## No number travels alone

Weak: The Alder offers 37.2 cubic feet of cargo space.

Better: The Alder holds 37.2 cubic feet behind the rear seats, near the top of the class and about 2 cubic feet shy of the CR-V.

Weak: It gets 24 mpg.

Better: The hybrid is rated at 24 mpg combined. That's below average for a new vehicle but about as good as a full-size truck gets, and it matches the diesel Silverado.

## The fault you could go and find

Weak: Interior storage is somewhat limited and materials could be better.

Better: Even in a truck this size, the door pockets won't take a large water bottle, and the center console is one big bin with nothing to divide it.

## Praise, then the catch

Weak: The infotainment system is excellent and packed with features.

Better: The 14-inch screen is crisp, quick and easy to find your way around. Be ready to pay a subscription for navigation once the trial ends, though.

## Explain it in a parenthesis

Weak: Standard ADAS includes ACC, LKA and BSM with RCTA.

Better: Every Alder comes with adaptive cruise control (holds a set gap to the car ahead), lane keeping assistance (nudges you back if you drift over the line) and blind-spot warning.

## Calibrate

Weak: The Ridgeback delivers absolutely incredible, best-in-class acceleration that will blow you away.

Better: Our test truck reached 60 mph in 5.8 seconds. That's pretty good for a pickup, though the Ram's turbo six is a bit quicker.

## Rivals in one clause each

Weak: Competitors include the Ram 1500, Toyota Tundra and Chevrolet Silverado, which are all strong alternatives worth considering.

Better: The Ram 1500 is still the one to beat for ride comfort. The Tundra brings a hybrid of its own and Toyota's reliability record, and the Silverado makes the strongest case on price.

## Make the call

Weak: The best trim depends on your individual needs and budget, so consider your options carefully.

Better: We'd buy the XLT. It's about $900 more than the STX and adds a power driver's seat, heated front seats and blind-spot warning. If you tow often, the tow package is worth its price; if you don't, skip it.

## Fair to the loser

Weak: The Alder finished last and is clearly outclassed here.

Better: Third place is a surprise for the Alder. It's louder at highway speed than the other two and its rear seat is too firm for a long trip. We'd still recommend it to anyone who puts fuel economy first, since it beat both rivals by 6 mpg on our loop.

## When we haven't driven it

Weak: The Ridgeback rides smoothly and its cabin is impressively quiet at speed.

Better: Car and Driver measured 66 decibels at 70 mph, which is quiet for a truck, and Consumer Reports rates its ride above the Silverado's.

## Pros and cons

Weak pros: Great performance · Nice interior · Lots of technology and features and options
Better pros: Hybrid returns 44 mpg and is quicker than most rivals · Wide, square cargo area · Easy to see out of

Weak cons: A few drawbacks · Could be better in some areas
Better cons: Seats are firm on long drives · Doesn't ride as smoothly over bumps as rival SUVs

## Buying tips

Weak: Now is a great time to buy, with many attractive offers available at dealers.

Better: Alders are selling in about 6 days, against a market average near 34, so dealers have little reason to move. Buyers are paying roughly $600 under sticker. The LE and XLE Premium have the most room, at about 2.9% off (our estimate, November 2026).

## Labelling an estimate

Weak: These are estimates from published data, not a promise, and every figure here was checked on November 15, 2026.

Better: The discount ranges come from TrueCar's November averages, so your dealer may land a point or two either side.

## Comparison close

Weak: In conclusion, both trucks have their strengths and weaknesses, and the right choice comes down to personal preference.

Better: The Ridgeback keeps its place at the top for storage, seat comfort and a softer ride. If you care more about how a truck steers and how good its screen is, drive the Silverado before you sign.
