The Miami car market right now

Buying a car around Miami

Ask whether a used car was in a flood

Miami sits low, and hurricanes push water into streets and parking garages. A flooded car can look fine on the lot and fail months later, because water gets into the wiring and electronics. Look for a musty smell, silt or a water line under the carpet, rust on the seat bolts and under the dashboard, and moisture inside the headlights. A history report only shows floods that were reported, so on a used search we can ask the dealer to say in writing whether the car has been in one, and to send photos of the trunk floor and the carpet edges. Have a mechanic you choose look the car over before you sign.

Look at what sun and salt air did

South Florida sun cracks dashboards, clouds headlight lenses and dries out tires and rubber seals. Salt air from the coast adds rust on the underside, the exposed bolts and the brake parts, even on a car that never drove on a salted road. Ask for photos of the dashboard, the headlights, the tires and the underside. On the test drive, run the air conditioning at full blast the whole way, and price the repair if it blows warm.

How it works from Miami

  1. You pay the flat fee and tell us the car. Model, trims and colors you’d accept, budget, and how far you’d go to pick it up. Mason replies within one business day to confirm the search and when it starts.
  2. We find the cars. Your advisor searches new, used and CPO dealer inventory for the model.
  3. We get written offers and negotiate. Each dealer is asked for an out-the-door price in writing. Offers go against each other, and add-ons and padded fees get questioned before you see a contract.
  4. You pick. We send written recommendations and our pick of the best deal. You make the final call, sign the paperwork and pick up the car from the dealer.

Most searches take one to two weeks from payment to a signed deal. Rare specs and factory orders take longer, and we tell you that up front. We can’t promise a specific discount: the market for a given car sets what a dealer will do. The full process is on how it works.

Yes. Broward dealers are inside the same 50-mile circle, well within 200 miles of a Miami buyer, so every plan covers them.

How can I tell if a used car in Miami was flooded?

Check the history report, get the dealer’s answer in writing, then look at the car: a musty smell, silt or a water line under the carpet, rust on the seat bolts, moisture inside the lights. A report only shows floods that were reported. We can ask the dealer for photos and a written answer. Have a mechanic you choose look the car over before you sign.

Is a used car from South Florida a bad idea?

No. A garaged car with service records can be a good buy. Sun and salt-air wear is visible, so ask for photos of the dashboard, the headlights, the tires and the underside, and have a mechanic you choose look at the car before you sign.
