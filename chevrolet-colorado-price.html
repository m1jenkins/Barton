The bottom line

New, as a 2026

The Colorado is the midsize pickup to price if you tow: Chevrolet rates it to pull 7,700 pounds, and Edmunds scored its towing and hauling 10 out of 10. Reviewers fault the back seat, and Consumer Reports expects below-average reliability. Price a 2026 LT, aim for 6–7% under MSRP, and take Chevrolet’s 4.9% financing if your bank can’t beat it.

What owners praise

  • Price and value. Buyers report paying below sticker on new trucks, especially leftover model years with incentives
  • Power and driving. Owners say the 2.7 turbo has strong low-rpm torque and tows boats and campers easily
  • Reliability. Owners report older Colorados and 2.8 Duramax diesels running past 200,000 miles with routine maintenance
  • Interior and tech. Owners like the styling, especially Z71 wheels and bright colors

What owners complain about

  • Reliability. Owners report transmission shudder and hard shifts, most often on eight-speed trucks

How we set these discount targets

Colorados sell well under sticker, and there are plenty of them. Edmunds counts about 18,900 new trucks nationwide and says they take about 40 days to sell against 34 for the average new vehicle; Cox put Chevrolet’s supply at 94 days against 87 for the industry at the end of October. Edmunds’ suggested prices, based on Texas sales, run 7–10% under sticker with destination, its buyers are paying about 6.7% under, Kelley Blue Book’s fair purchase prices are about 5% under, and iSeeCars puts dealer invoice 4.6% under MSRP. CarWhere has only seven verified 2026 buyers (a median 4.5% under), too few to lean on.

  • WT, Custom, LT. Edmunds’ Texas prices run 9.7% under on the WT and 10.0% on the LT, and Kelley Blue Book’s fair price is about 5% under. Edmunds says the WT has the most stock.

  • ZR2. Edmunds’ Texas price is 7.1% under, Kelley Blue Book’s fair price 5.5% under, and Edmunds says ZR2 stock is tighter.

New 2026

Best when: Most buyers. Chevrolet’s financing and cash are on the 2026, and it costs $100 to $1,200 less than a 2027.

Check: That the truck is a 2026 and not a 2027, and that the offer runs in your region.

New 2027

Best when: You want the 350-hp engine or plan to tow near the limit. Chevrolet lists 350 hp and 450 lb-ft against 310 and 430 on the 2026.

Check: The price rise, and that the only 2027 offer we found is the $2,000 conquest cash.

CPO (GM CarBravo)

Best when: You want a 2023 to 2025 truck with some cover, and you’re paying cash.

Check: CarBravo’s certified warranty is 12 months/12,000 miles bumper to bumper on trucks under 10 model years and 100,000 miles. Chevrolet’s own powertrain warranty runs 5 years/60,000 miles from first sale, by Edmunds’ account.

Used, not certified

Best when: Your budget is under about $34,000: a 2023 asks a median $33,399.

Check: Open recalls by VIN and an independent inspection. A 2021 or 2022 (median asks $24,830 and $24,995) is the previous generation; the Colorado was redesigned for 2023.

Buying used? Look at a certified 2023 to 2025 and ask for the recall paperwork. The 2023 and 2024 were recalled for a camera fault that could trigger automatic emergency braking by mistake, overtightened front wheel hub bolts and a loose seat belt buckle bolt.

What to watch for at the dealer

  • Brakes. NHTSA is running an engineering analysis (EA26006) into loss of brake assist, and the 2023 to 2026 Colorado is on its list. No recall as of November 18, 2026.
  • Cash or rate. Chevrolet doesn’t say whether the $1,000 cash and the 4.9% rate combine. Ask for a quote each way and compare the total cost.
  • The model year. A 2026 and a 2027 look alike on the lot, and the cash and financing are on the 2026.
  • The back seat and storage. U.S. News calls the back seat tiny, and Edmunds scores storage 3 out of 10. Try both on the lot.
  • Fees and add-ons. Dealers add fees and extras to the price you see online. Compare out-the-door prices, and refuse or negotiate anything you didn’t ask for.

Is the Chevrolet Colorado a good truck?

Good, though short of the top. Kelley Blue Book gives it 4.6 out of 5 and U.S. News 8.7. Edmunds is cooler at 6.5 and faults the rear seat and storage. Consumer Reports expects it to be less reliable than the average new car.

How much under MSRP can you get a Chevrolet Colorado?

On a 2026 WT, Custom or LT, 6–7% under MSRP is a fair deal and 9–10% a strong one. The ZR2 has less room, around 5–6%. That’s our read of Edmunds, Kelley Blue Book and iSeeCars figures, so dealers may land a point either side.

Should I buy a 2026 or a 2027 Colorado?

The 2026, for most buyers. It costs $100 to $1,200 less, and Chevrolet’s 4.9% financing and customer cash are on the 2026. The 2027 has a 350-hp engine against 310, which matters if you tow near the 7,700-pound limit.

What is the destination charge on a Colorado?

$2,095 on both model years. Chevrolet’s advertised starting prices leave it out, so a $32,400 WT is $34,495 on the window sticker.

Does Chevrolet offer cash back on the Colorado?

Yes, to November 30: $1,000 customer cash on a 2026 ($500 on the Trail Boss and ZR2), $1,000 for eligible military members and $2,000 conquest cash for owners of a Tacoma, Ranger or five other rival models. Chevrolet’s page doesn’t say which combine.

Should I buy a Colorado or a Tacoma?

The Colorado if you tow: Edmunds says its towing, payload and screen lead the class. The Tacoma if resale matters most. It loses 19.5% of its value in five years against the Colorado’s 36.9%, iSeeCars says.

Is a used Colorado a better deal than new?

Rarely. A used 2024 asks a median $36,087, while a new 2026 LT starts at $36,000 and sells 6–7% under that. Chevrolet’s 4.9% financing compares with 11.19% for the average used loan. A certified 2023 to 2025 is the used route we’d take.
