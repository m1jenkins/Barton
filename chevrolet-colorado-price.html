The Colorado is the midsize pickup to price if you tow: Chevrolet rates it to pull 7,700 pounds against the Ford Ranger’s 7,500, and Edmunds scored its towing and hauling 10 out of 10. It isn’t the roomiest or the most reliable truck in the class. Edmunds and U.S. News fault the back seat, and Consumer Reports expects it to be less reliable than the average new car. It does sell well under sticker: Edmunds’ Texas suggested prices run 7–10% under, with about 18,900 trucks in stock and 40 days to sell. Price a 2026 LT, aim for 6–7% under MSRP, and take Chevrolet’s 4.9% financing if your bank can’t beat it. Prices and offers were checked on November 18, 2026.

What to pay: how far under MSRP

Colorados sell well under sticker, and there are plenty of them. Edmunds counts about 18,900 new trucks nationwide and says they take about 40 days to sell against 34 for the average new vehicle; Cox put Chevrolet’s supply at 94 days against 87 for the industry at the end of October. Edmunds’ suggested prices, based on Texas sales, run 7–10% under sticker with destination, its buyers are paying about 6.7% under, Kelley Blue Book’s fair purchase prices are about 5% under, and iSeeCars puts dealer invoice 4.6% under MSRP. CarWhere has only seven verified 2026 buyers (a median 4.5% under), too few to lean on.

What people praise

  • Reliability. Owners report older Colorados and 2.8 Duramax diesels running past 200,000 miles with routine maintenance
  • Price and value. Buyers report paying below sticker on new trucks, especially leftover model years with incentives
  • Power and driving. Owners say the 2.7 turbo has strong low-rpm torque and tows boats and campers easily
  • Interior and tech. Owners like the styling, especially Z71 wheels and bright colors

What owners and shoppers complain about

  • Reliability. Owners report transmission shudder and hard shifts, most often on eight-speed trucks
  • Interior and tech. Owners report over-the-air software updates failing or looping and needing a dealer visit
  • Dealers and markups. Owners report dealers dismissing rattles as not reproducible and waiting weeks on backordered parts
  • Price and value. Used mid-trim trucks priced near a new one are seen as poor value

The trim we’d buy

A 2026 LT crew cab, with 4WD if you leave pavement. At $36,000 for the 2WD (4WD adds $3,300) it adds the EZ Lift and Lower tailgate with built-in storage and remote start to the WT, and Edmunds names it the editors’ pick of the lineup. Chevrolet’s $1,000 customer cash applies to it (the Trail Boss and ZR2 get $500), and Edmunds’ Texas prices show it selling 10.0% under sticker, the deepest of any trim.

If you’ll use trails, a Trail Boss costs $1,200 more than an LT 4WD ($40,500 against $39,300) and adds a 2-inch lift, 32-inch all-terrain tires and a two-speed automatic transfer case, which Chevrolet lists on the Trail Boss and which U.S. News says some other trims lack. Skip the ZR2 unless you drive rough ground often: it starts at $50,700 and sells the least under sticker. A 2027 LT adds 40 hp for $900 but gets none of Chevrolet’s current offers.

What to watch for at the dealer

  • Brakes. NHTSA is running an engineering analysis (EA26006) into loss of brake assist on GM vehicles with the eBoost brake system, and the 2023 to 2026 Colorado is on its list. No recall had been issued as of November 18, 2026. Ask the dealer to check the VIN for open recalls and read the brake complaints on NHTSA before you buy.
  • Cash or rate. Chevrolet lists the $1,000 cash and the 4.9% rate as separate offers and doesn’t say whether they combine. Ask for a quote each way and compare the total cost.
  • The model year. A 2026 and a 2027 look alike on the lot, and the cash and financing are on the 2026.
  • The back seat. Edmunds says the rear seats lack comfort and taller riders may lack knee room, and U.S. News calls the back seat tiny and says loading a child seat is a pain. Sit in it with your own seat.
  • Storage and controls. Edmunds scores storage 3 out of 10 (small door pockets) and says the headlight controls sit in the touchscreen. Try both on the lot.
  • Fees and add-ons. Dealers add fees and extras to the price you see online. Compare out-the-door prices, and refuse or negotiate anything you didn’t ask for.

Common questions

Is the Chevrolet Colorado a good truck?

Reviewers rate it well but not at the top. Kelley Blue Book gives it 4.6 out of 5 (third among midsize trucks), U.S. News 8.7 and third of seven, and J.D. Power’s owner score is 83, second behind the Tacoma’s 84. Edmunds is cooler at 6.5, fourth of seven, and faults the rear seat and storage. Consumer Reports expects it to be less reliable than the average new car, and iSeeCars puts its five-year value loss at 36.9% against 30.2% for midsize pickups.

How much under MSRP can you get a Chevrolet Colorado?

On a 2026 WT, Custom or LT, 6–7% under MSRP is a fair deal and 9–10% a strong one. The Trail Boss and Z71 run 6–7% fair and 8–9% strong, and the ZR2 has less room, around 5–6%. Those ranges are our read of Edmunds’ Texas suggested prices, Kelley Blue Book’s fair purchase prices and iSeeCars’ invoice figures, so a given dealer may land a point either side, and Chevrolet’s cash comes on top.

Should I buy a 2026 or a 2027 Colorado?

The 2026, for most buyers. It costs $100 to $1,200 less, and Chevrolet’s 4.9% financing and customer cash are on the 2026. The 2027 has a 350-hp engine against 310, which matters if you tow near the 7,700-pound limit. The only 2027 offer we found is the $2,000 conquest cash.

What is the destination charge on a Colorado?

$2,095 on both model years. Chevrolet’s advertised starting prices leave it out, so a $32,400 WT is $34,495 on the window sticker.

Does Chevrolet offer cash back on the Colorado?

Yes. On November 18, 2026 it listed $1,000 customer cash on a 2026 (Trail Boss and ZR2 get $500), $1,000 for eligible military members, and $2,000 conquest cash for owners of a 2012 or newer Tacoma, Ranger, Ridgeline, Frontier, Bronco, Gladiator or Wrangler, per GM Authority. All run to November 30. Chevrolet’s page doesn’t say which combine.

Should I buy a Colorado or a Tacoma?

Buy the Colorado if you tow or want more standard power and a bigger screen: Edmunds says its towing, payload and screen lead the class. Buy the Tacoma if resale matters most. It loses 19.5% of its value in five years against the Colorado’s 36.9% (iSeeCars), owners score it 84 against 83 at J.D. Power, and Edmunds calls it the safe choice in the segment. If you own a Tacoma, Chevrolet’s $2,000 conquest cash lowers the Colorado’s price.

Is a used Colorado a better deal than new?

Rarely. CarWhere’s median asking price for a used 2024 is $36,087 and for a 2025 $38,636, while a new 2026 LT starts at $36,000 and sells 6–7% under that, with 4.9% financing against 11.19% for the average used loan. A certified 2023 to 2025 with its recalls done is the used route we’d take.
