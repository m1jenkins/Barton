The Boston car market right now

The Census Bureau counted 336 new-car dealer locations across the Boston metro area in 2023, which covers eastern Massachusetts and southeastern New Hampshire. S&P Global Mobility ranked the Boston-Manchester market eighth in the country for new-vehicle registrations in the first half of 2025. Of the ten markets with the most registrations, it had the most days’ supply, at 78, and the second-lowest average monthly payment, $636, after Detroit’s.

Buying a car around Boston

Ask where a used car spent its winters

Massachusetts and New Hampshire salt their roads every winter, and a car that lived outside takes the worst of it from below. The brake lines, the exhaust and the rocker panels under the doors rust first, often on a car whose paint still looks fine. Ask the dealer where the car has lived. On a used search we can ask for photos of the underside, and you can have a mechanic you choose put the car on a lift before you sign.

Check the car against your street

Many Boston streets are narrow, and a lot of cars park on them. A big SUV that wins on price can lose the first night you try to park it. Tell us the longest and widest car you can live with and whether you have a garage or a driveway, and we check each model against those limits before we ask any dealer for a price.

Work out how you’ll reach the dealer by train

You pick up the car at the dealer. A store that looks close on the map can still take a train and a ride to reach, and you need a way to drive home afterward. Tell us which line you’d use and how long a trip you’d make, and we ask the dealers you can actually reach.

How it works from Boston

  1. You pay the flat fee and tell us the car. Model, trims and colors you’d accept, budget, and how far you’d go to pick it up. Mason replies within one business day to confirm the search and when it starts.
  2. We find the cars. Your advisor searches new, used and CPO dealer inventory for the model.
  3. We get written offers and negotiate. Each dealer is asked for an out-the-door price in writing. Offers go against each other, and add-ons and padded fees get questioned before you see a contract.
  4. You pick. We send written recommendations and our pick of the best deal. You make the final call, sign the paperwork and pick up the car from the dealer.

Most searches take one to two weeks from payment to a signed deal. Rare specs and factory orders take longer, and we tell you that up front. We can’t promise a specific discount: the market for a given car sets what a dealer will do. The full process is on how it works.

What stays with you

You decide which car, if any, to buy. You arrange the inspection, pick the financing and insurance, read the contract and sign it. Our fee does not include the car, taxes, registration, inspection, financing, insurance or delivery. We don’t give tax or registration advice for your state; the dealer’s paperwork and your state’s motor vehicle agency are the sources for that.

Common questions

Do you have an office in Boston?

No. We’re based in Austin, Texas, and we work with Boston-area dealers by phone and email. You deal with one advisor the whole way, and you pick up the car from the dealer.

Can you get offers from New Hampshire and Rhode Island dealers as well as Massachusetts ones?

Yes, on any plan. The Steer Me Right plan ($395) covers dealers within 200 miles of your ZIP code, and the Massachusetts, New Hampshire and Rhode Island dealers in the circle are all inside that.

Do you check used cars for road-salt rust?

From photos. We can ask the dealer for pictures of the underside and tell you what we see in them. Photos miss things, so before you sign, have a mechanic you choose put the car on a lift. On a car that has spent a few Boston winters outside, that hour is worth it.

I take the train to work. Can I still use a car buying service?

Yes. You pick up the car at the dealer like every buyer. Tell us which line you’d use and how long a trip you’d make, and we weigh how easy each dealer is to reach alongside its price. Some dealers will deliver the car to you; whether they do, and what they charge, is up to them.
