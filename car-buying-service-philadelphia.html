The Philadelphia car market right now

The Census Bureau counted 381 new-car dealer locations across the Philadelphia metro area in 2023, an area that spans Pennsylvania, New Jersey, Delaware and Maryland. S&P Global Mobility ranked the Philadelphia market seventh in the country for new-vehicle registrations in the first half of 2025, with an average monthly payment of $651 and 69 days’ supply. Among the ten markets with the most registrations, only Detroit and Boston had lower monthly payments.

Buying a car around Philadelphia

Check which side of the river you’re buying on

The Delaware River divides Pennsylvania from New Jersey here, and a dealer on the far bank can sit minutes from a Center City address. Both plans cover it, because what counts is the distance from your ZIP code and not the state. Tell us where you live and which bridges you’d cross, and we ask the dealers you’d actually drive to.

Ask where a used car spent its winters

Pennsylvania treats winter roads with salt and brine, and a car that lived outside takes it from below. The rust shows first on the brake lines, the exhaust and the rocker panels under the doors, often before the paint gives anything away. On a used search we can ask the dealer for photos of the underside. Before you sign, have a mechanic you choose put the car on a lift.

Plan how you’ll get to the dealer

You pick up the car at the dealer, so a buyer without a car needs a route there and a way to drive home. A store in the suburbs or across the river can look close on the map and still take a train and a ride to reach. Tell us the train line or bridge you’d use, and we ask the dealers you can actually get to.

Tell us where the car will park

Many Philadelphia blocks are lined with rowhouses and street parking, and a car that fits a driveway may not fit a curb. Tell us the longest and widest car you can park, and whether it will live on the street or in a garage. We check your model against those limits before we ask any dealer for a price, so a car that won’t fit never reaches your shortlist.

How it works from Philadelphia

  1. You pay the flat fee and tell us the car. Model, trims and colors you’d accept, budget, and how far you’d go to pick it up. Mason replies within one business day to confirm the search and when it starts.
  2. We find the cars. Your advisor searches new, used and CPO dealer inventory for the model.
  3. We get written offers and negotiate. Each dealer is asked for an out-the-door price in writing. Offers go against each other, and add-ons and padded fees get questioned before you see a contract.
  4. You pick. We send written recommendations and our pick of the best deal. You make the final call, sign the paperwork and pick up the car from the dealer.

Most searches take one to two weeks from payment to a signed deal. Rare specs and factory orders take longer, and we tell you that up front. We can’t promise a specific discount: the market for a given car sets what a dealer will do. The full process is on how it works.

What stays with you

You decide which car, if any, to buy. You arrange the inspection, pick the financing and insurance, read the contract and sign it. Our fee does not include the car, taxes, registration, inspection, financing, insurance or delivery. We don’t give tax or registration advice for your state; the dealer’s paperwork and your state’s motor vehicle agency are the sources for that.

Common questions

Do you have an office in Philadelphia?

No. We’re based in Austin, Texas, and we work with Philadelphia-area dealers by phone and email. You deal with one advisor the whole way, and you pick up the car from the dealer.

Can you get offers from New Jersey and Delaware dealers as well as Pennsylvania ones?

Yes, on either plan. The Steer Me Right plan ($395) covers dealers within 200 miles of your ZIP code, whichever state they are in, so a New Jersey buyer on that plan gets the Philadelphia and Wilmington stores as well as the New Jersey ones.

Can you tell if a used car has road-salt rust?

Only from photos. We can ask the dealer for pictures of the underside and send them to you. Photos miss things, so have a mechanic you choose put the car on a lift before you sign, especially on a car that spent its winters parked outside.

I don’t own a car. Can I still use a car buying service?

Yes. You pick up the car at the dealer like every buyer. Tell us which train line or bridge you’d use and how long a trip you’d make, and we weigh how easy each dealer is to reach alongside its price. Some dealers will deliver the car to you; whether they do, and what they charge, is up to them.

Do dealers pay you?

No. Dealers and lenders pay us nothing. You pay the flat fee, and you’re the only one we work for.
