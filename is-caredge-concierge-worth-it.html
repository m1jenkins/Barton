Is CarEdge Concierge worth it? What $999 buys, and when to pay less

By Mason · Updated November 10, 2026

Short answer: CarEdge Concierge costs $999 per car, paid at search start, and a person handles the research, dealer outreach, negotiation and paperwork review for you. It is worth it if you want a large, well-known company and the fee is small next to the car. It is hard to justify if you already know the car you want, because Steer Me Right does the same finding and negotiating for $395 (one model) or $695 (up to three), and Automatch Consulting lists $499. Prices checked November 10, 2026 on each company’s own pages.

We run Steer Me Right, so this is a competitor’s view. Every fact about CarEdge below comes from CarEdge’s own site, with the link beside it.

What CarEdge Concierge costs

CarEdge’s pricing page lists Concierge at $999, “flat, per car,” paid at search start. One fee covers the whole search, for buying or leasing, new or used. Its cheaper option, the AI Buying Agent, is listed at $49.99 in beta (normally $149); software runs that deal and a person steps in only for unusual problems.

What you get for the $999

CarEdge’s service page describes the Concierge scope as research, dealer outreach, test drive coordination, price negotiation, paperwork review and delivery logistics. CarEdge says it has closed deals in all 50 states. That is a full-service search, the same shape of work as the Steer Me Right plan.

Refund and timing rules to read first

  • Refund window. When we read CarEdge’s pricing FAQ on October 18, 2026, it said the Concierge fee is non-refundable 24 hours after the initial consultation or once you agree to proceed, “regardless of the outcome.” Steer Me Right’s line is simpler: full refund if you cancel before negotiations begin.
  • Timing. The same FAQ said a typical search runs 7 to 10 days from sign-up to possession. At Steer Me Right, most searches take one to two weeks from payment to a signed deal. Rare specs and factory orders take longer, and we tell you that up front.
  • Per car. The $999 is per car. If you are torn between two or three models, that can mean paying more than once, or picking before you have offers. Steer Me Right Pro prices up to three models side by side for $695.

How CarEdge is paid

CarEdge’s pricing page says of the Concierge fee: “One-time. No commissions, no dealer kickbacks.” CarEdge as a company also earns money from dealers and other businesses, and it says so on its own site. Its dealer program page says enrollment “bills a card” and that certified dealers “show up first” when a CarEdge shopper is looking for a car they have (dealers cannot pay to change their grade). Section 6 of its terms says CarEdge “may receive compensation or other consideration in connection with referrals, sponsored placements, marketplace activity or Consumer-requested services.” None of that means your Concierge is steering you; it does mean dealers are also CarEdge customers. At Steer Me Right, dealers and lenders pay us nothing. You pay the flat fee, and you’re the only one we work for.

When CarEdge Concierge is worth it

  • You want a large company with a long public track record, and $999 is small next to the car.
  • You already use CarEdge’s free tools and want the same brand handling the deal.

When it isn’t

  • You know the car. The work is finding stock, getting written offers and negotiating price and fees. Steer Me Right does that for $395, which is $604 less than Concierge.
  • You are deciding between a few models. Steer Me Right Pro covers up to three models for $695, $304 less than one CarEdge search.
  • You want a clean refund line. Full refund if you cancel before negotiations begin, at Steer Me Right.
  • You only need data. CarEdge’s own free tools, or its $49.99 AI Buying Agent, may be enough if you are happy to do the final haggling yourself.
