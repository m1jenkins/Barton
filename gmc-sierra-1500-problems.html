We rate every Sierra 1500 year Avoid when it has the 5.3-liter or 6.2-liter V8, so there is no best used year for those engines. Engine is the top complaint part on every Sierra from 2019 to 2025, and NHTSA’s open EA25007 covers 6.2-liter V8 failures on 2019 to 2024 trucks. By the numbers the 2019 and the 2022 draw the highest rates, 386 and 430 NHTSA owner complaints per 100,000 sold, with 339 and 271 of their complaints naming the engine, and the 2022 adds 111 complaints on structure, where NHTSA’s open recall query RQ26003 covers grille deflectors that detach. The lowest rates are the 2025, at 57, and the 2017, at 268. The 2.7-liter turbo, 4.3-liter V6 and 3.0-liter diesel trucks aren’t covered by that call, but NHTSA counts complaints by part, not by engine, so this table can’t rate them on their own.

The rates divide each model year’s NHTSA complaints by U.S. light-duty Sierra sales. They compare years with each other; they don’t predict one truck. The 2016 count is only 54 complaints, which looks incomplete next to the 433 for the 2017, so we don’t trust its rate. The 2026 is too new to rate; our Sierra price guide covers what it costs new.

Complaints are owner reports to NHTSA, counted once each across every GMC Sierra 1500 listing (SIERRA 1500, SIERRA 1500 LIMITED, SIERRA) and read on November 21, 2026. Model years and calendar years don’t line up, since a model year’s cars sell across two calendar years, so the rate compares years with each other. Sales for 2019 to 2025 are GMC’s light-duty Sierra figures from Best-Selling-Cars’ GM tables. GM didn’t split light-duty from heavy-duty before 2019, so 2016 to 2018 are our estimate: Good Car Bad Car’s all-Sierra totals times 74%, the 2019 light-duty share (172,452 of 232,325). Our verdicts use this car’s own yardstick, the median of 275 complaints per 100,000 across its 2019 to 2025 model years: Buy up to 1.25 times it, Check first up to twice it, Avoid above that. A recall, free or not, never moves a verdict. A year can move when NHTSA has an open investigation on it or one part carries most of its complaints,; the reason column says so.

Sierra generations: what changed

Fourth generation (K2XX) 2014–2018

The 2016 to 2018 model years are this truck, with a 4.3-liter V6, 5.3- and 6.2-liter V8s and six- or eight-speed automatics; the 5.3 also came as an eAssist mild hybrid from 2016 to 2018 (Wikipedia). GM renamed it the Sierra Limited for 2019 and ended it in December 2019; it returned for 2022 as a stop-gap.

Fifth generation (T1XX) 2019–

Unveiled March 1, 2018 and on sale that September as a 2019 model. Engines were a 2.7-liter turbo four (2019–2021, High Output from 2022), a 4.3-liter V6 (2019–2021), 5.3- and 6.2-liter V8s, and a 3.0-liter Duramax diesel from 2020, with a 10-speed automatic standard on gas trucks (Wikipedia). The 2022 refresh brought a new front end and interior and the AT4X and Denali Ultimate trims.

Common Sierra problems by component

NHTSA files each complaint under the parts the owner names, and one complaint can name more than one. Across all 4,310 Sierra complaints for the 2016 to 2026 model years, these parts come up most:

  • Engine 2019–2025 model years1,784 complaints naming engine, 137 per 100,000 sold. Engine tops every year from 2019 to 2025: 339 on the 2019, 380 on the 2021. Recall 25V-274 covers 6.2-liter V8s from 2021 to 2024, where a connecting rod or crankshaft defect can lead to engine failure; dealers inspect and repair or replace the engine, free. NHTSA’s EA25007 counted 1,157 reports of 6.2-liter engine failure across GM trucks and SUVs, 173 of them outside the recall. Owners on Reddit also report 5.3 lifter and cylinder deactivation trouble on used trucks, and early problems on the 3.0 Duramax.
  • Transmission and drivetrain 2017–2019 model years418 complaints naming transmission and drivetrain, 84 per 100,000 sold. Power train leads the 2017 (145) and the 2018 (131), with no recall on NHTSA’s list for it. NHTSA’s label spans the transmission, driveshaft and axles, so it doesn’t say which. Recall 20V-650 covers a driveshaft joint weld on 2019–2020 trucks that can separate the shaft and cut propulsion.
  • Structure 2022–2023 model years209 complaints naming body and structure, 63 per 100,000 sold. Structure draws 111 complaints on the 2022 and 98 on the 2023. Recall 25V-060 covers the chrome front grille deflector on 2022 trucks, whose attachments can fracture and let it detach. NHTSA’s open recall query RQ26003 has 118 complaints of detachment on 2022 to 2024 Sierras, 21 of them after the recall repair.
  • Brakes 2016–2017 model years140 complaints naming brakes, 43 per 100,000 sold. Brakes are second on the 2017 (128). Recall 19V-645 (2014–2018 trucks) has dealers reprogram the brake control module because the vacuum pump’s output can fall over time and lengthen stopping distances.

What owners report on Reddit

Reliability is the topic Reddit raises most about the Sierra: 405 mentions in 301 threads, 96 positive and 258 negative, a net of −37 on a scale from −100 to +100. Our Reddit Sentiment Index for the Sierra is 49 out of 100 (50 is even). The threads run from January 2025 to November 2026 and come from r/gmcsierra and the rest of Reddit, so they say little about a car that owners stopped discussing before 2025. The GMC Sierra 1500 price guide has the full topic breakdown.

Owners on Reddit report many trouble-free miles on 3.0 Duramax and V8 trucks and call a well-kept 5.3 good for 200,000 miles, but they raise the same engine worries as Silverado owners: 6.2 failures and anxiety about the recall repair, and 5.3 lifter and cylinder deactivation trouble on used trucks. The Sierra threads add the 3.0 Duramax (emissions faults, oil leaks and early engine issues), which NHTSA’s engine count can’t separate from the gas engines.

Recalls worth knowing about

  • Engine connecting rod and crankshaft 2021–2024 6.2-literRecall 25V-274. Manufacturing defects in the connecting rod or crankshaft can lead to engine damage and failure. Dealers inspect the engine and repair or replace it as necessary, free.
  • Brake vacuum pump 2016–2018Recall 19V-645. The vacuum pump’s output can drop over time, reducing brake assist and lengthening stopping distances. Dealers reprogram the brake control module, free.
  • Driveshaft joint weld 2019–2020Recall 20V-650. A driveshaft joint may not have been welded properly and can separate while moving, cutting propulsion. Dealers replace the driveshaft, free.
  • ESC and ABS software 2019Recall 19V-889. A software error can disable stability control and ABS without lighting their warning lamps. Dealers reprogram the brake control module, free.
  • Chrome grille deflector 2022Recall 25V-060. The attachments that hold the chrome front grille deflector can fracture and let it detach, leaving a road hazard. Dealers reinforce or repair the attachment points, free.
  • Roof-rail air bag inflators 2018Recall 24V-756. An inflator end cap can detach or the inflator can rupture and throw fragments into the cabin. Dealers replace both roof-rail modules, free.
  • Roof-rail air bag inflators, second campaign 2016–2017Recall 26V-611. The same inflator fault on 2016–2017 trucks, filed September 24, 2026. Dealers replace both modules, free; owner letters were mailed October 21, 2026.
  • Transfer case 2026 4WDRecall 26V-289. A missing component in the transfer case can lock the front or rear wheels without warning. GM advises owners not to drive an affected truck until a dealer inspects and, if needed, replaces the transfer case, free.

Open and recent NHTSA investigations

  • Loss of power from 6.2-liter engine failure 2019–2024EA25007, open. NHTSA opened the engineering analysis on October 23, 2025 after 1,157 reports of L87 6.2-liter engine bearing failure, 173 of them outside recall 25V-274’s scope. Its opening summary names 2019 to 2024 Sierra 1500s and Chevrolet Silverado 1500s, among other GM trucks and SUVs.
  • Upper grille deflector detachment 2022–2024RQ26003, open. NHTSA opened a recall query on October 9, 2026 after 118 complaints that the upper grille deflector detaches on 2022 to 2024 Sierras, some leading to a cracked windshield or evasive maneuvers. Recall 25V-060 covered only 2022 trucks with the chrome deflector.
  • Air bag warning light 2020–2021PE21010, closed. NHTSA closed it in August 2023 after finding the failure rate low and the warning easy to spot, on trucks built during a seven-month pandemic production gap.
  • Brake vacuum pump 2014–2016PE18012, closed. NHTSA closed it in April 2022 after GM’s special coverage for the vacuum pump and the later recall 19V-645.

What to check before you buy a used Sierra

  • Open recalls by VIN.Enter the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. A recall fix is free at a GMC dealer, and the lookup shows which ones are still open on that exact car.
  • The 6.2-liter V8 on a 2021 to 2024.Ask for the dealer’s record showing the 25V-274 inspection was done and how the truck passed. EA25007 is open on engines the recall doesn’t cover.
  • The grille deflector on a 2022 to 2024.Recall 25V-060 covers only 2022 trucks with the chrome deflector, and RQ26003 reports detachments after the repair and on 2023 and 2024 trucks. Look at how the deflector is attached.
  • The 5.3 and 3.0 Duramax.Owners on Reddit report 5.3 lifter and cylinder deactivation trouble and 3.0 Duramax emissions faults and oil leaks. Ask for service records and have a shop scan for codes.
  • The driveshaft and brake module on a 2019 or 2020.Confirm 20V-650 (driveshaft weld) and, on a 2019, 19V-889 (stability control and ABS software).
  • The roof-rail air bags on a 2016 to 2018.Recall 26V-611 (2016–2017) was filed in September 2026 and 24V-756 covers the 2018; the VIN lookup shows whether they are open.
  • An independent inspection.Have a shop that isn’t the seller’s check the engine for codes, the transmission for shift faults and the front suspension before you sign.

Common questions

What are the worst GMC Sierra years?

With a 5.3-liter or 6.2-liter V8, every one: we rate 2016 to 2026 Avoid. By the numbers the 2019 and 2022 are the worst, drawing 386 and 430 NHTSA complaints per 100,000 sold against 57 for the 2025, with engine named in 339 and 271 complaints, and the 2022 adds the grille deflector recall query.

What is the best used Sierra year?

With a 5.3-liter or 6.2-liter V8 we don’t have one. The 2025 draws the lowest rate, 57 complaints per 100,000, and the 2017 draws 268, but we rate them Avoid like the rest because engine is the top complaint part. The 2.7-liter turbo, 4.3-liter V6 and 3.0-liter diesel trucks aren’t covered by that call, but NHTSA counts complaints by part, not by engine, so this table can’t rate them on their own.

Why is the 2016 Sierra count so low?

NHTSA lists only 54 complaints for the 2016 against 433 for the 2017. We checked other listing names for the 2016 and found nothing more, so the count looks incomplete and its low rate can’t be trusted. It rates Avoid with the other V8 years.

Does the Sierra have the same engine problems as the Silverado?

Their 6.2-liter V8s share recall 25V-274 and investigation EA25007, and engine is the top complaint part on both trucks from 2019 to 2025. NHTSA counts complaints by part, not by engine, so these figures can’t say which engine.

How do I check a used Sierra for recalls?

Enter the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It lists open recalls for that truck; recall repairs are free at a GMC dealer.
