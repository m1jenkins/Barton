The best used CR-Vs are the 2024 and 2025 model years, which draw 121 and 75 NHTSA owner complaints per 100,000 sold against a yardstick of 136 for this car. Skip the 2017, 2018 and 2019: they draw 473, 573 and 290, with the 2017 and 2018 led by engine complaints (543 and 545) and the 2019 by automatic emergency braking (299). The 2020, 2021 and 2022 rate low enough to buy (136, 125 and 149), but NHTSA’s open engineering analysis EA24002 covers unexpected automatic braking on 2017 to 2022 CR-Vs, so we call them Check first. The 2023 (176) is Check first for its steering count: 398 of 637 complaints, which Honda’s free recall 24V-744 covers.

The rates divide each model year’s NHTSA complaints, counted across the gas and Hybrid listings, by U.S. sales. Honda’s sales table doesn’t say in words that its CR-V total includes the Hybrid, and 2022 sales of 238,155 were low next to 361,457 in 2023, which lifts the 2022 rate. The 2016, the last of the previous generation, rates Buy at 111, though it is ten years old. The 2026 is too new to rate. Our CR-V price guide covers what a new or used one costs.

Complaints are owner reports to NHTSA, counted once each across every Honda CR-V listing (CR-V, CR-V HYBRID) and read on November 21, 2026. Each rate divides a model year’s complaints by the U.S. sales of the same calendar year. Model years and calendar years don’t line up, since a model year’s cars sell across two calendar years, so the rate compares years with each other. Sales come from Good Car Bad Car’s CR-V table, whose 2019 to 2025 figures reproduce the rates on our CR-V comparison pages built from Honda’s FactBook. Our verdicts use this car’s own yardstick, the median of 136 complaints per 100,000 across its 2019 to 2025 model years: Buy up to 1.25 times it, Check first up to twice it, Avoid above that. A recall, free or not, never moves a verdict. A year can move when NHTSA has an open investigation on it or one part carries most of its complaints,; the reason column says so.

CR-V generations: what changed

Fourth generation 2012–2016

Honda’s fourth-generation CR-V went on sale in December 2011 as a 2012 with a 2.4-liter engine and was facelifted for 2015 (Wikipedia). The 2016 is its last year.

Fifth generation 2017–2022

The fifth generation went on sale in December 2016 as a 2017 and was updated for 2020 (Wikipedia). It carried a turbo engine in its first years, and NHTSA files a CR-V Hybrid recall from 2020, so the Hybrid arrived with the update.

Sixth generation 2023–

The redesigned CR-V went on sale in September 2022 as a 2023 (Wikipedia). Hybrids made 54% of 2025 sales, from the Honda FactBook figures our CR-V versus Mazda CX-5 page uses.

Common CR-V problems by component

NHTSA files each complaint under the parts the owner names, and one complaint can name more than one. Across all 8,365 CR-V complaints for the 2016 to 2026 model years, these parts come up most:

  • Fuel and hybrid system1,612
  • Engine1,604
  • Steering1,579
  • Automatic emergency braking1,420
  • Electrical system1,217
  • Brakes719
  • Transmission and drivetrain421
  • Wipers and visibility330
  • Engine and fuel in the 2017 and 2018 turbo 2017–2018 model years1,088 complaints naming engine, 144 per 100,000 sold. These two years have the most engine complaints of any CR-V (543 and 545), and in the complaint text we read most mention oil or a fuel smell. CarComplaints reports Honda extended warranties for fuel mixing into the turbo’s oil and that the extension has run out; it was never a recall, so a VIN lookup won’t show it.
  • Unexpected automatic braking 2017–2022 model years1,239 complaints naming automatic emergency braking, 60 per 100,000 sold. NHTSA’s EA24002 covers 2017 to 2022 CR-Vs and 2018 to 2022 Accords after owners reported the emergency braking system activating with nothing in the car’s path. There is no recall yet. The 2019 alone has 299 complaints naming the system.
  • Steering, 2023 on 2023–2025 model years619 complaints naming steering, 53 per 100,000 sold. Recall 24V-744 covers a steering gearbox with excess internal friction on 2023 to 2025 CR-Vs and the Hybrid; dealers replace the worm gear spring and add grease, free. NHTSA’s analysis EA23003, which covered the 2023 CR-V, closed in January 2025. The 2023 has 398 steering complaints, the 2024 151 and the 2025 70.
  • Fuel pumps and tanks 2018–2020 model years938 complaints naming fuel and hybrid system, 86 per 100,000 sold. Recalls 21V-215 and 23V-858 cover an in-tank fuel pump that can fail and stall the engine on 2018 and later cars, and 19V-569 a fuel tank weld on 2019 cars. Dealers repair each one free. NHTSA’s field doesn’t say what failed, so the 609 complaints on the 2018 may describe more than one problem.
  • The Hybrid’s power and electrical system 2020–2023 model years199 complaints naming electrical system, 15 per 100,000 sold. Hybrid recalls cover a DC-DC converter that can shut down (20V-798), a battery cable missing a fuse (23V-844), battery modules that can spark (24V-745) and a transmission (23V-588). NHTSA’s open PE25016 covers 2020 to 2022 Hybrids that lose power at highway speed without a fault code.

What owners report on Reddit

Reliability is the topic Reddit raises most about the CR-V: 635 mentions in 542 threads, 269 positive and 300 negative, a net of −4 on a scale from −100 to +100. Our Reddit Sentiment Index for the CR-V is 57 out of 100 (50 is even). The threads run from January 2025 to November 2026 and come from r/crv and the rest of Reddit, so they say little about a car that owners stopped discussing before 2025. The Honda CR-V price guide has the full topic breakdown.

The turbo-engine worries in those threads line up with the 2017 and 2018 engine counts in the table. The steering and braking stories that fill the 2019 and 2023 rows barely appear, so the complaint counts here carry that part of the record.

What owners praise about reliability

  • Owners report older CR-Vs passing 200,000 miles with only routine maintenance
  • Owners of newer CR-Vs report no major trouble past 100,000 miles
  • Hybrid system and battery are seen as proven and unlikely to fail early

What owners report going wrong

  • Owners report fuel injector faults and dashboard warning lights on recent models
  • Owners report head gasket and oil dilution worries on the 1.5-liter turbo
  • Owners report the windshield chipping and cracking easily

Other complaints that come up

  • Dealers and markups. Dealers push add-on packages and charge high documentation fees on new CR-Vs
  • Price and value. Used and new CR-V asking prices are often seen as too high

Recalls worth knowing about

  • Steering gearbox friction 2023–2025Recall 24V-744. A steering gearbox built with excess friction can make the car hard to steer. Dealers replace the worm gear spring and redistribute or add grease, free. It covers the Hybrid and the 2025 fuel cell model too.
  • High-pressure fuel pump 2023–2025 HybridRecall 24V-763. The pump may crack and leak fuel, which raises the risk of a fire. Dealers inspect and replace it as needed, free.
  • Fuel feed hose 2025 HybridRecall 25V-378. A hose that was installed wrong can let fuel leak. Dealers fit a new hose and connector cover, free.
  • In-tank fuel pump 2018–2023Recall 23V-858 and 21V-215. The pump inside the tank may fail and stall the engine. The recalls cover the CR-V in several model years; dealers replace the pump module, free, and the VIN lookup shows which one applies.
  • Rear subframe bolts 2019–2020Recall 19V-865. Bolts that were made wrong can loosen and let the rear subframe separate. Dealers retorque or replace them, free.
  • Fuel tank weld 2019Recall 19V-569. The weld between the tank and the vapor return line may fail and leak fuel. Dealers replace the tank, free.
  • Steering sensor magnet 2017–2018Recall 18V-663. A magnet in the torque sensor can come loose and apply steering assist the wrong way. Dealers replace the gearbox, free.
  • Transmission and battery modules 2023 HybridRecall 23V-588 and 24V-745. A transmission built wrong can cost drive power, and battery modules built wrong can spark and break a busbar. Dealers replace the e-CVT or the module, free.
  • Tire repair kit bottle cap 2023–2026 HybridRecall 26V-366 and 26V-663. Pressure can build in the sealant bottle and blow its cap off. Dealers replace the nozzle or bottle, and the second recall covers cars already fixed under the first.

Open and recent NHTSA investigations

  • Inadvertent automatic emergency braking 2017–2022EA24002, open. NHTSA opened the engineering analysis on April 16, 2024, upgrading PE22003, after 1,294 owner complaints of the braking system activating with no obstruction ahead, 31 of them alleging a crash. It covers 2017 to 2022 CR-Vs and 2018 to 2022 Accords. Honda has not recalled anything for it.
  • Loss of motive power 2020–2022 HybridPE25016, open. NHTSA opened the preliminary evaluation on October 29, 2025 after 48 owner reports of intermittent power loss at highway speed, with a flashing check engine light, in 2020 to 2022 CR-V Hybrids. Turning the car off and on restores power but also clears the fault codes, so repairs often find nothing.
  • Momentary increased steering effort 2023EA23003, closed. NHTSA’s analysis of steering effort covered the 2023 CR-V along with the 2022 and 2023 Civic and the Acura Integra. It closed in January 2025, after Honda’s recall 24V-744.

What to check before you buy a used CR-V

  • Open recalls by VIN.Enter the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. A recall fix is free at a Honda dealer, and the lookup shows which ones are still open on that exact car.
  • Steering recall on any 2023 to 2025.Ask the dealer for proof that 24V-744 is done.
  • Braking on a 2017 to 2022.Ask whether the seller has had automatic emergency braking activate by itself. No recall covers EA24002, so a VIN lookup won’t show it; check that the system works as described on the test drive.
  • Oil on a 2017 or 2018 turbo.Owners and CarComplaints report fuel mixing into the oil in cold weather. Check the dipstick for a high level or a fuel smell, and ask for service records of oil changes.
  • Fuel pump and subframe recalls.Confirm 21V-215 and 23V-858 on a 2018 to 2020, 19V-569 and 19V-865 on a 2019, and 24V-763 on a 2023 to 2025 Hybrid.
  • Hybrid power loss.On a 2020 to 2022 Hybrid, ask whether the car has ever lost power with a flashing check engine light, and have a shop read stored codes and service history, since the fault clears itself.
  • An independent inspection.Owners report head gasket leaks on turbo engines and a windshield that cracks easily. Have a shop that isn’t the seller’s check coolant, the windshield and the all-wheel-drive rear differential before you sign.

Is the 2026 better?

The 2026 CR-V has 199 NHTSA complaints and 2 recalls so far, both for the Hybrid’s tire repair kit, so it is too new to rate. The steering recall 24V-744 applies to used 2023 to 2025 cars and not to a new one, and Honda’s open braking analysis covers cars through 2022 only.

Common questions

What is the worst Honda CR-V year?

The 2018 has the most complaints (2,172) and the highest rate of any year, 573 NHTSA complaints per 100,000 sold. The 2017 (473) and 2019 (290) are close behind, and the 2017 and 2018 are the years CarComplaints’ oil story covers.

What is the best used CR-V year?

The 2024 and 2025, at 121 and 75 complaints per 100,000 sold. Ask for proof of steering recall 24V-744 on the 2024 and fuel pump recall 24V-763 on a Hybrid.

Does the CR-V have an oil dilution problem?

CarComplaints reports fuel mixing into the oil on 2017 and 2018 CR-Vs with the 1.5-liter turbo in cold weather, and says Honda extended warranties that have since run out. It isn’t a recall, so a VIN lookup won’t show it.

Is the 2023 Honda CR-V reliable?

It drew 637 NHTSA complaints, 398 of them about steering. Honda’s free recall 24V-744 covers the gearbox, and the Hybrid has two more recalls, so we’d buy one only with proof they are done.

How do I check a used CR-V for recalls?

Enter the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It lists open recalls for that car, and recall repairs are free at a Honda dealer.
