Yes. A Hybrid SEL costs $1,700 more than a gas SEL and is rated 38 mpg combined against 28 with front-wheel drive, so it burns about 140 fewer gallons at 15,000 miles. At the national average of $3.805 a gallon that is $536 a year, and the premium is back in 3.2 years (4.0 years at 12,000 miles).

The one thing that clouds it is Hyundai’s own money. This month the gas Tucson gets 0% financing for up to 60 months and up to $3,000 in bonus cash, while the hybrid gets 2.9% and $1,750. On a $33,000 loan over 60 months, 2.9% costs $2,490 in interest. Take Hyundai’s offers on each and the hybrid’s payback stretches to about 8 years, or 10 if the gas truck gets the full extra $1,250 in cash. Pay cash or finance elsewhere and the 3.2 years stands.

The hybrid is also the quicker Tucson: 231 hp against 187, and Edmunds timed the gas engine at 10.2 seconds to 60 mph, one of the slowest crossovers it has tested. Hyundai’s plug-in costs $6,425 more again and only pays off if you charge it every day (see below).

Buy the hybrid if…

  • You pay cash or have your own financing. The Hybrid SEL pays back its $1,700 in about 3 years at 15,000 miles.
  • You want all-wheel drive. The hybrid gives up 2 mpg for AWD (36 combined) and the gas Tucson 2 (26), so the hybrid keeps a 10-mpg lead.
  • You want a quicker Tucson. The gas engine takes 10.2 seconds to 60 mph in Edmunds’ test, and the hybrid has 44 more hp.
  • You do a lot of city driving. EPA rates the front-drive hybrid at 40 mpg in the city against 25 for the gas Tucson.

Buy the gas version if…

  • You are taking Hyundai’s financing. Gas gets 0% for 60 months and up to $3,000 in bonus cash; the hybrid gets 2.9% and $1,750.
  • You want the cheapest Tucson. A gas SE starts at $29,700, and the cheapest hybrid is $31,300.
  • You will resell soon. iSeeCars has a three-year-old gas Tucson down 24.8% and a hybrid down 29.2% (from the Tucson price guide).
  • You want fewer parts to worry about after the warranty. Hyundai covers the hybrid battery for 10 years or 100,000 miles, and the gas Tucson has no battery pack to replace.

EPA fuel economy, gas against hybrid

EPA rates the front-drive Hybrid at 38 mpg combined (40 city, 35 highway) and the front-drive gas Tucson at 28 (25 city, 33 highway). With AWD it is 36 against 26. The gain is in town: on the highway a front-drive hybrid is rated 2 mpg ahead of the gas Tucson, so a mostly highway commute narrows the case.

Hyundai’s offers change the answer. The 0% against 2.9% difference on a $33,000, 60-month loan is $2,490 of interest (about $42 a month). Add that to the $1,700 and the Hybrid SEL’s payback at 15,000 miles and $3.805 gas becomes 7.8 years. Add the extra $1,250 of bonus cash the gas truck can carry and it is about 10 years. That arithmetic is ours, from the rates and cash in the price guide, and it only applies if you finance through Hyundai Motor Finance.

What about the plug-in?

The Plug-in SEL costs $6,425 more than a Hybrid SEL with AWD, the trim it matches closest, and EPA rates it 35 mpg combined once the battery is empty, a point worse than the 36 mpg of the AWD Hybrid. Its pitch is 32 miles of electric range. We worked out the best case: a full charge used every day, 365 days a year.

Do this month’s incentives favor one version?

This month Hyundai’s offers favor the gas Tucson. The gas Tucson and the Plug-in get 0% APR for up to 60 months plus no payments for 90 days, while the Hybrid gets 2.9% for up to 60 months. Bonus cash runs up to $3,000 on the gas Tucson, $1,750 on the Hybrid and $4,000 on the Plug-in, and on the gas SEL Premium and Limited it requires Hyundai Motor Finance and goes toward the down payment. Everything ends November 30, 2026. Offers are from the Tucson price guide, read at four ZIP codes on Hyundai’s site on November 18, 2026 for well-qualified buyers; the plug-in’s were read in Los Angeles only.

Resale: does the hybrid hold its value?

iSeeCars’ figures from the Tucson price guide put three-year depreciation at 24.8% for the gas Tucson and 29.2% for the Hybrid, against 22.7% for compact SUVs. Its comparison page has five-year depreciation at 45.9% and 47.4%. The hybrid loses a little more, which lengthens the payback a little, but not by as much as Hyundai’s gas-side financing does.

What owners on Reddit report about real-world mpg

Owners say the hybrid reaches 35 to 40 mpg with gentle driving and the gas Tucson 27 to 30, so both land near their ratings; the complaints are fast highway speeds and short trips. We counted 128 opinions about the Tucson’s fuel economy in 503 Reddit threads about the car, posted from January 2025 to November 2026: 66 positive, 39 negative and 23 mixed. That is a net score of +21 on the same −100 to +100 scale as the Tucson price guide, and it covers every version of the car, not only the hybrid.

Common questions

How much more does the Tucson Hybrid cost than the gas Tucson?

$1,600 on an SE ($31,300 against $29,700), $1,700 on an SEL ($32,750 against $31,050) and $1,250 on a Limited once you add AWD to the gas truck ($42,425 against $41,175). Prices are 2026 starting MSRPs without destination, from the Tucson price guide.

How much better is the Tucson Hybrid’s mpg?

EPA rates a front-drive Hybrid at 38 mpg combined against 28 for the gas Tucson, and 36 against 26 with AWD. That is 10 mpg either way, about 140 fewer gallons a year at 15,000 miles.

Is the Tucson Plug-in Hybrid worth the extra money?

Only if you can charge it at home every night. EPA rates it 32 miles of electric range and 35 mpg after that, and it costs $6,425 more than a Hybrid SEL with AWD. In the best case we could work out, that takes about 14 years to earn back.

Does Hyundai cover the hybrid battery?

Yes. Hyundai’s U.S. warranty page lists a 10-year/100,000-mile warranty on hybrid and electric batteries and hybrid system components, including plug-in hybrids.
