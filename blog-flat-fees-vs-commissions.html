Why Your Car Broker Might Be Costing You More

By Mason • Service Comparison

Some auto consultants charge a percentage of the savings they negotiate. Others charge a flat fee. The compensation model can affect what the consultant is rewarded for, so compare the terms before hiring one.

A percentage-of-savings offer may sound risk-free, but it can reward a larger starting price or a larger stated discount. Ask how the savings are calculated and what you will pay if the final price is not the lowest available.

How the starting price affects the fee

If an advisor is earning a commission based entirely on the gap between Manufacturer's Suggested Retail Price (MSRP) and the final deal, the model rewards a high starting price rather than the lowest final price. They may hunt for vehicles at dealerships that inflate the original asking cost with large "Market Adjustments."

Imagine a truck legitimately worth $50,000 on the open market. A rogue dealer lists it at $58,000 with an arbitrary markup. Your broker negotiates the truck down to $52,000 and presents you with a "$6,000 discount," of which they quietly pocket $1,500. You still overpaid by two thousand dollars, and the broker earned more from the larger discount.

Compare the incentives: A percentage-of-discount fee increases when the stated discount increases. A flat fee does not change with the vehicle price or discount.

What a flat fee changes

For help with vehicle research, price negotiation and fee review, compare Full Service at $295 USD one time with Ultimate Concierge at $895 USD. You make the final purchase decision. Vehicle and other transaction costs are separate.

  • Compare the final price: We compare the dealer invoice, factory holdbacks, and final out-the-door price rather than only the stated discount.
  • Same fee structure: An $85,000 luxury SUV and a $25,000 sedan may require different research, but the flat service fee does not rise with the vehicle price.
"When the fee is fixed, there is no financial mechanism left for the consultant to exploit. The only option remaining is doing an excellent job."

Ask any car-buying service to explain its fee, what counts as savings, and whether the dealer pays anything. The terms should be clear before you begin. Drive Right uses a flat service fee.
