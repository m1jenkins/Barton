The Forester is the compact SUV for buyers who want standard all-wheel drive, 8.7 inches of ground clearance and a clear view out, and who’ll pay for the hybrid. The gas engine is the weak spot: Edmunds timed a Forester Sport at 9.2 seconds to 60 mph and scores the car 5.7 out of 10, well below U.S. News’ 8.6 and J.D. Power’s 84. The hybrid (194 hp, 35 mpg combined) is quicker by Edmunds’ account, but it trails an AWD Toyota RAV4 at 41 mpg and an AWD Honda CR-V hybrid at 37. IIHS names the Forester a 2026 Top Safety Pick+, and iSeeCars counts 13.3% of its value lost in three years against 24.8% for the average vehicle. It’s also easy to discount: Edmunds’ Texas prices run about 7% under sticker on every trim, and Cox counted 62 days of Subaru supply at the end of October, against 24 for the RAV4. What to pay: how far under MSRP

Foresters sell about 7% under sticker. Edmunds’ Texas suggested prices run 6.8–7.4% under with destination on every trim, its November tips say buyers are paying about $2,600 below MSRP on average, and CarWhere’s five verified buyers paid a median 7.3% under MSRP. iSeeCars puts dealer invoice 6.1–6.9% under MSRP. Supply is ample: Edmunds counted about 27,100 new Foresters in stock and Cox 62 days of Subaru supply at the end of October, against 87 for the industry. Even so, Edmunds says Foresters sell in a median 16 days, so the scarcer trims may not move much. Kelley Blue Book’s fair purchase price for a Base is $1,845 under its $31,445 MSRP with destination, about 5.9%.

The trim we’d buy

A 2026 Premium Hybrid. At $34,730 it costs $2,735 more than a gas Premium, and the step buys the hybrid system (194 hp against 180, and 35 mpg combined against 29), 12.3-inch digital gauges, a power rear gate and blind-spot detection. A gas Premium sells the last two together as a $1,200 package, so the real gap is $1,535, and the EPA’s fuel-cost estimate ($1,700 a year against $2,050) pays it back in about four years. Edmunds calls the gas Forester slow, 9.2 seconds to 60 mph in its tested Sport, and says the hybrid is noticeably quicker. That alone is worth the money to us.

If you tow or drive unpaved roads, take the Wilderness ($36,995, Edmunds’ editors’ pick). It’s rated to tow 3,500 pounds against 1,500 for most Foresters and sits 9.3 inches off the ground, but it’s rated 26 mpg combined. Edmunds likes the gas Sport for value, but at $34,795 it costs $65 more than a Premium Hybrid and is rated 28 mpg combined. Skip it for the hybrid.

What to watch for at the dealer

  • The ball-joint recall. NHTSA recall 26V685, filed October 19, 2026, covers some 2026 Foresters whose left-front lower control arm ball joint may separate. Subaru tells owners not to drive an affected car until the dealer has fixed it. Ask whether the car you’re buying is on it, and get the repair in writing.
  • Which engine you test-drive. Edmunds finds the gas Forester slow and its engine coarse and noisy when you push it. Drive the hybrid and a gas car back to back on a highway on-ramp before you pick.
  • Dealer add-ons. Paint protection, nitrogen and etched VINs are the dealer’s products. Refuse or negotiate them, and ask for a written quote that lists MSRP, your discount and each fee on its own line.
  • The option package on a gas Premium. The power rear gate and blind-spot detection that come standard on the Premium Hybrid are a $1,200 package on a Premium. Add it before you compare the two.
  • The 2027’s changes. A 2026 and a 2027 look alike on the lot. The 2027 has a $1,525 destination charge, costs more on the gas Limited and Wilderness, and loses the gas Sport and Touring.
  • The credit conditions. Subaru’s 1.99% and 3.99% are for well-qualified buyers, need delivery from retailer stock by November 30, and the 3.99% covers only the Premium, Sport, Limited and Touring Hybrids. Compare the total cost with your bank’s rate.

Common questions

Is the Subaru Forester a good car?

Reviewers like it but don’t agree how much. U.S. News scores the 2026 an 8.6 out of 10, eighth of 14 compact SUVs, and J.D. Power owners give it 84 out of 100, second in the class. Edmunds rates it 5.7 out of 10 and calls the gas engine slow, Kelley Blue Book gives it 4.4 out of 5, and IIHS names it a 2026 Top Safety Pick+. iSeeCars counts 13.3% of its value lost in three years, against 24.8% for the average vehicle.

How much under MSRP can you get a Subaru Forester?

About 5–6% on most 2026 trims, and 7% or more on a strong deal. Those ranges are our read of Edmunds’ Texas prices (6.8–7.4% under sticker on every trim), CarWhere’s five verified buyers (a median 7.3% under) and iSeeCars’ invoice figures, so a given dealer may land a point either side. The Base and Sport Hybrid are tighter on supply, so 4–5% is fair. Subaru has no cash back to add on top.

Is the Forester Hybrid worth it?

For most buyers, yes. A Premium Hybrid costs $2,735 more than a gas Premium, or $1,535 after counting the $1,200 package the gas car needs for a power rear gate and blind-spot detection. It’s rated 35 mpg combined against 29, and the EPA estimates $1,700 a year in fuel against $2,050. Edmunds says the hybrid is noticeably quicker. It still trails an AWD Toyota RAV4 hybrid (41 mpg combined) and an AWD Honda CR-V hybrid (37).

Should I buy a 2026 or a 2027 Forester?

The 2026, for most buyers. Subaru’s 1.99% and 3.99% financing is on the 2026, its destination charge is $75 less ($1,450 against $1,525), and the Base, Premium and hybrid trims cost the same either year. Buy the 2027 if you want the new Wilderness Hybrid ($40,495), or a Limited or Wilderness with more standard equipment, and don’t mind paying $1,600 or $2,500 more for it.

Is the Forester good off-road?

More than most small SUVs. Every Forester has all-wheel drive and at least 8.7 inches of ground clearance. The Wilderness adds 9.3 inches, all-terrain tires, underbody protection, a full-size spare and a 3,500-pound tow rating, which Edmunds calls the one to get if you tow. Other Foresters tow 1,500 pounds.

What is the destination charge on a Forester?

$1,450 on the 2026 ($1,600 in Alaska) and $1,525 on the 2027 ($1,675 in Alaska); Subaru says it may vary in CT, HI, MA, ME, NH, NJ, NY, RI and VT. Subaru’s advertised starting prices leave it out.

Does Subaru offer cash back on the Forester?

No. In Chicago, Dallas, Los Angeles and New York on November 18, 2026, Subaru listed 1.99% and 3.99% financing and two lease deals, but no customer cash. The only money off is a $500 military discount.

Is a used Forester a better deal than new?

Rarely. CarWhere’s median asking price for a 2025 is $31,476 at about 15,000 miles, and Edmunds’ Texas price for a new 2026 Premium is $31,124 with destination. Used loans averaged 11.19% in the second quarter of 2026, against Subaru’s 1.99% or 3.99% on a new one. A Subaru Certified Forester is the used route we’d take.
