The bottom line

New, as a 2026

The Forester is for buyers who want standard all-wheel drive, 8.7 inches of ground clearance and a clear view out. The gas engine is slow, so buy the hybrid (35 mpg combined), though an AWD RAV4 is rated 41. Price a 2026 Premium Hybrid, aim for 5–6% under MSRP, and take Subaru’s 3.99% financing if your bank can’t beat it.

What owners praise

  • Safety and driver aids. EyeSight driver aids and strong crash ratings give owners confidence
  • Power and driving. Standard AWD and X-Mode handle snow, gravel and light trails with confidence
  • Reliability. Owners report 150k to 250k miles with only routine maintenance on well-kept Foresters
  • Ride and comfort. Large windows and thin pillars give excellent outward visibility with almost no blind spots

What owners complain about

  • Reliability. Owners report head gasket leaks and oil consumption on older Forester engines

  • Price and value. Used Foresters are seen as priced high for their age and mileage

How we set these discount targets

Foresters sell about 7% under sticker. Edmunds’ Texas suggested prices run 6.8–7.4% under with destination on every trim, its November tips say buyers are paying about $2,600 below MSRP on average, and CarWhere’s five verified buyers paid a median 7.3% under MSRP. iSeeCars puts dealer invoice 6.1–6.9% under MSRP. Supply is ample: Edmunds counted about 27,100 new Foresters in stock and Cox 62 days of Subaru supply at the end of October, against 87 for the industry. Even so, Edmunds says Foresters sell in a median 16 days, so the scarcer trims may not move much. Kelley Blue Book’s fair purchase price for a Base is $1,845 under its $31,445 MSRP with destination, about 5.9%.

New 2026

Best when: Most buyers. Subaru’s rates and leases are on the 2026, destination is $75 less, and the gas Sport, Sport Onyx Edition and Touring exist only as 2026s.

Check: That the car is a 2026, and that recalls 26V685 (ball joint), 26V346 (moonroof glass) and 25V889 (rear hatch bracket) are done if it’s on them.

New 2027

Best when: You want the Wilderness Hybrid ($40,495), which exists only for 2027, or a Limited or Wilderness with the power rear gate and heated steering wheel standard. Subaru said the 2027 arrives at retailers later this fall.

Check: The higher gas Limited and Wilderness prices ($1,600 and $2,500 more), the $1,525 destination charge, and whether any 2027 offer exists yet (none was listed).

CPO (Subaru Certified)

Best when: You want a recent used Forester with a warranty. Subaru lists finance rates from 4.29%.

Check: That it’s Subaru Certified: a 152-point inspection, a 7-year/100,000-mile powertrain agreement with a $0 deductible, 24/7 roadside assistance and a history report. Subaru’s page doesn’t give age or mileage limits, so ask.

Used, not certified

Best when: Your budget is under about $27,000. CarWhere’s median 2023 asks $26,997 at about 35,000 miles.

Check: Open recalls by VIN, an independent inspection, and for a 2025 or 2026, that the recalls above are done.

Buying used? Look at a 2023 or 2024 and skip the 2019 and 2020. Those two drew 834 and 604 NHTSA owner complaints, against 77 for the 2023 and 32 for the 2024. Check any used Forester’s open recalls by VIN.

What to watch for at the dealer

  • The ball-joint recall. Recall 26V685 covers some 2026 Foresters. Subaru says not to drive an affected car until it’s fixed. Ask for the repair in writing.
  • Which engine you test-drive. Edmunds finds the gas Forester slow and its engine coarse and noisy when pushed. Drive the hybrid and a gas car back to back.
  • The gas Premium’s option package. The Premium Hybrid’s power rear gate and blind-spot detection are a $1,200 package on a gas Premium. Add it before you compare.
  • The 2027’s changes. The 2027 has a $1,525 destination charge, costs more as a gas Limited or Wilderness, and loses the gas Sport and Touring. Check which year you’re quoted.
  • The credit conditions. Subaru’s rates are for well-qualified buyers and need delivery from retailer stock by November 30. Compare the total cost with your bank’s rate.

Is the Subaru Forester a good car?

Reviewers like it but don’t agree how much. U.S. News scores the 2026 8.6 out of 10 and J.D. Power owners give it 84 out of 100. Edmunds rates it 5.7 and calls the gas engine slow. IIHS names it a 2026 Top Safety Pick+.

How much under MSRP can you get a Subaru Forester?

About 5–6% on most 2026 trims, and 7% or more on a strong deal. That’s our read of Edmunds, CarWhere and iSeeCars figures, so a dealer may land a point either side. On the Base and Sport Hybrid, 4–5% is fair. Subaru offers no cash.

Is the Forester Hybrid worth it?

For most buyers, yes. A Premium Hybrid costs $2,735 more than a gas Premium and is rated 35 mpg combined against 29. Edmunds says it’s noticeably quicker. It still trails an AWD Toyota RAV4 hybrid (41 mpg combined) and an AWD Honda CR-V hybrid (37).

Should I buy a 2026 or a 2027 Forester?

The 2026, for most buyers. Subaru’s financing is on the 2026, destination is $75 less, and the Base, Premium and hybrid trims cost the same either year. Buy the 2027 if you want the new Wilderness Hybrid ($40,495).

Is the Forester good off-road?

More than most small SUVs. Every Forester has all-wheel drive and at least 8.7 inches of ground clearance. The Wilderness sits 9.3 inches up, with all-terrain tires, a full-size spare and a 3,500-pound tow rating. Other Foresters tow 1,500 pounds.

What is the destination charge on a Forester?

$1,450 on the 2026 ($1,600 in Alaska) and $1,525 on the 2027 ($1,675 in Alaska). Subaru’s advertised starting prices leave it out.

Does Subaru offer cash back on the Forester?

No. On November 18, 2026, Subaru listed 1.99% and 3.99% financing and two lease deals in the four regions we checked, but no customer cash. The only money off is a $500 military discount.

Is a used Forester a better deal than new?

Rarely. A used 2025 asks a median $31,476 at about 15,000 miles, and Edmunds’ Texas price for a new 2026 Premium is $31,124 with destination. Used loans averaged 11.19% against Subaru’s 1.99% or 3.99%. A Subaru Certified Forester is the used route we’d take.
