What CarEdge offers

CarEdge sells three levels of help, from free research to a person who negotiates for you.

  • Concierge: $999 per car search, paid when the search starts, according to its pricing page. A dedicated expert handles research, dealer outreach, test-drive coordination, negotiation, paperwork review and delivery logistics, per its service page. It covers new or used cars, bought or leased, and CarEdge says it has closed deals in all 50 states. Its pricing page says a deal typically takes 7 to 10 days from sign-up to taking the car, and that the fee becomes non-refundable 24 hours after your first consultation, or once you agree to proceed.
  • AI Buying Agent: $49.99 during its beta (normally $149), per its checkout page. An AI agent runs the deal, and CarEdge says a person steps in when something unusual comes up, such as a dealer playing games or a strange financing wrinkle. You can get a full refund until it starts contacting dealers.
  • Free research tools, including dealer ratings, invoice prices and a depreciation calculator, plus a large library of guides.

CarEdge says the $999 covers the whole search, for any make or model, and that you can cross-shop as many cars as you want.

How CarEdge makes money

CarEdge’s pricing page says: “One-time. No commissions, no dealer kickbacks.” CarEdge also earns money from businesses other than buyers, and it says so on its own site.

  • CarEdge Certified is a paid program for dealerships. CarEdge’s dealer page says enrollment “bills a card,” that certified dealers “show up first” when a CarEdge shopper is looking for a car they have, and that a dealer cannot pay to change its CarEdge grade.
  • Its terms say CarEdge “may receive compensation or other consideration in connection with referrals, sponsored placements, marketplace activity or Consumer-requested services.”

None of that is hidden, and none of it means a CarEdge negotiator will steer you wrong. If you hire CarEdge, it is fair to ask whether certified dealers get priority in your search. At Drive Right, dealers and lenders pay us nothing. You pay the flat fee, and you’re the only one we work for.

Why people look for a CarEdge alternative

CarEdge is one of the best-known names in car buying help, and its free content is useful whether or not you hire anyone. People who keep looking usually have one of these reasons.

The fee for a person

$999 is a lot to spend before you have saved anything. The Drive Right plan costs $395 and Drive Right Pro costs $695. Both are one-time fees, and both come with one dedicated advisor who searches, contacts dealers and negotiates.

Wanting a person instead of software

The AI Buying Agent is cheap because software does most of the work. If you want someone who reads each quote, spots the add-ons and pushes back by phone, compare the human tiers only.

Wanting to be the only one paying

Some buyers would rather hire a service with no paid dealer program at all. Drive Right has none: dealers and lenders pay us nothing.

Refund terms

CarEdge’s pricing page says the Concierge fee becomes non-refundable “regardless of the outcome” 24 hours after your first consultation, or once you agree to proceed. At Drive Right, cancel before negotiations begin and you get a full refund. During negotiations, refund eligibility depends on the work already done.

Other CarEdge alternatives

Delivrd

A founder-led negotiation service for new cars. Its Insta-Pay page lists $1,000 per deal, paid up front, and its service agreement says there are no refunds after work has started. Our Delivrd alternatives page has the details, and our used car buying service page covers who still takes used cars.

Car Pal

Its pricing page lists full service at $1,195 for cars and SUVs and $1,695 for trucks, covering new and used cars, with inspections for used cars and home or office delivery arranged.

Automatch Consulting

Its payments page lists $499 if you already know the car and $599 if you want help choosing or want several deals compared, for new, used or leased cars.

Authority Auto

Handles new-car purchases and leases, with full-service pricing by phone. Its free deal review charges nothing unless it beats a quote you already have; then you split the savings.

Costco Auto Program

For Costco members, the program offers a price prearranged with a participating dealer. You go to the dealer and finish the purchase yourself.

Do it yourself

Ask several dealers for a written, itemized out-the-door quote on the same car, then compare them line by line in our free quote comparison worksheet. CarEdge’s free tools work well alongside it.

When CarEdge is the better pick

  • You want to do the research yourself first, with free data on dealers, invoice prices and depreciation.
  • You are happy to let software gather offers, and your budget is closer to $49.99 than $395.
  • You want a large company with a big team and a long list of published guides.
  • Speed matters most: CarEdge says a Concierge deal typically takes 7 to 10 days from sign-up to taking the car.

Common questions

How much does CarEdge cost?

On October 18, 2026, CarEdge’s pricing page listed the Concierge at $999 per car search and the AI Buying Agent at $49.99 during its beta (normally $149). Its research tools are free.

Is CarEdge worth it?

It can be. Its free tools are useful even if you never pay, and the AI agent costs little. For a person who negotiates, the Concierge is $999 per car search. The Drive Right plan, where one advisor also searches, contacts dealers and negotiates, is $395.

Does a person negotiate with the CarEdge AI Buying Agent?

Software runs the deal. CarEdge says a person steps in when the AI hits something unusual. The Concierge is the tier with a dedicated person.

Can I get a refund from CarEdge?

For the Concierge, CarEdge’s pricing page says the fee becomes non-refundable 24 hours after your first consultation, or once you agree to proceed, regardless of the outcome. The AI Buying Agent can be cancelled for a full refund until it starts contacting dealers.

Does CarEdge take money from dealers?

Its pricing page says “No commissions, no dealer kickbacks.” It also runs CarEdge Certified, a paid program for dealerships, and its terms say it may receive compensation for referrals and sponsored placements. The section on how CarEdge makes money has the details and links.
