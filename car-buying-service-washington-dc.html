The Washington, DC car market right now

On November 15, 2026, dealers within 50 miles of downtown Washington listed about 83,000 new cars and 71,000 used ones. Ford, Toyota and Honda had the most new cars on the lot. The median asking price was about $48,400 new and $23,600 used.

Fifty miles from downtown covers Northern Virginia, the Maryland suburbs and Baltimore. Very few of those dealers are in the District itself; nearly all of them are in Maryland or Virginia. So a Washington-area search almost always crosses a state line, and a Virginia store and a Maryland store selling the same car rarely see each other’s quotes unless we show them.

Buying a car around Washington, DC

Choose Maryland or Virginia before you choose a dealer

A dealer on each side of the Potomac can be the same distance from your door and a very different trip, depending on the bridge, the Beltway or the Metro line between you. Tell us which side you could reach and how you’d get there. If you live in the District, ask us which plan fits before you pay.

Look under a used car that has seen a few winters

Maryland and Virginia treat roads with salt and brine before and during winter storms, and the damage shows underneath before it shows in the paint. Look at the brake lines, the exhaust and the rocker panels below the doors. Ask the dealer where the car has lived: one that came down from the snowy northern states has had more salt than one that stayed in the Southeast. On a used search we can ask for photos of the underside. Have a mechanic you choose put the car on a lift before you sign.

How it works from Washington, DC

  1. You pay the flat fee and tell us the car. Model, trims and colors you’d accept, budget, and how far you’d go to pick it up. Mason replies within one business day to confirm the search and when it starts.
  2. We find the cars. Your advisor searches new, used and CPO dealer inventory for the model.
  3. We get written offers and negotiate. Each dealer is asked for an out-the-door price in writing. Offers go against each other, and add-ons and padded fees get questioned before you see a contract.
  4. You pick. We send written recommendations and our pick of the best deal. You make the final call, sign the paperwork and pick up the car from the dealer.

Most searches take one to two weeks from payment to a signed deal. Rare specs and factory orders take longer, and we tell you that up front. We can’t promise a specific discount: the market for a given car sets what a dealer will do. The full process is on how it works.

What stays with you
