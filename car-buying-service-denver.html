Buying a car around Denver

Ask whether a used car has been through a hailstorm

The National Weather Service says Colorado has always been known for hail storms, and it lists a summer of severe hail over the Denver metro area among the state’s top weather events of the decade. A car that sat outside through one can carry dents across the roof and hood. Dents show in reflections, so ask for daylight photos of the roof, hood and trunk lid taken from a low angle. On a used search we can ask the dealer for those photos and for a written answer on hail repair. Have a mechanic you choose look for filler and mismatched paint before you sign.

Check the tires before you drive I-70 into the mountains

If you’ll drive I-70 into the mountains, the tires decide whether the car meets Colorado’s traction law. CDOT says the law covers I-70 between Morrison and Dotsero from fall through spring. While it is in effect, a passenger car needs tires with the required tread depth that are rated all-weather or winter, marked with a mountain-snowflake, M+S or M/S, or it must carry chains or another approved traction device. CDOT also highly recommends all-wheel or four-wheel drive in the mountains, but a worn all-wheel-drive car can still fall short of the law. Tell us whether the mountains are in your plans, and we ask each dealer what tires are on the car and for a photo of the tread and sidewall.

Plan for E-470 on the way to a dealer

E-470 collects its tolls without cash. Without an ExpressToll account, cameras read your plates and a statement is mailed to the vehicle’s owner, at higher rates than ExpressToll customers pay. A dealer you reach on E-470 comes with that bill. Tell us how far you’d drive and whether you’d rather skip toll roads, and we ask the dealers inside that window.

How it works from Denver

  1. You pay the flat fee and tell us the car. Model, trims and colors you’d accept, budget, and how far you’d go to pick it up. Mason replies within one business day to confirm the search and when it starts.
  2. We find the cars. Your advisor searches new, used and CPO dealer inventory for the model.
  3. We get written offers and negotiate. Each dealer is asked for an out-the-door price in writing. Offers go against each other, and add-ons and padded fees get questioned before you see a contract.
  4. You pick. We send written recommendations and our pick of the best deal. You make the final call, sign the paperwork and pick up the car from the dealer.

Most searches take one to two weeks from payment to a signed deal. Rare specs and factory orders take longer, and we tell you that up front. We can’t promise a specific discount: the market for a given car sets what a dealer will do. The full process is on how it works.

Common questions

Do you have an office in Denver?

No. We’re based in Austin, Texas, and we work with Denver-area dealers by phone and email. You deal with one advisor the whole way, and you pick up the car from the dealer.

Can you tell if a used car has hail damage?

Only from photos and the dealer’s written answer. We can ask for pictures of the roof, hood and trunk lid and for a written answer on hail repair, and send them to you. Photos miss dents, so have a mechanic you choose look the car over before you sign.

Do I need all-wheel drive for I-70 in the mountains?

CDOT highly recommends it, and its traction law turns on the tires. While the law is in effect, a passenger car needs tires with the required tread that are rated all-weather or winter, or chains or another approved traction device. Tell us what you plan to drive, and we ask the dealer what tires are on the car and for a photo of the tread.

Will I pay a toll driving to a dealer on E-470?

Yes, if your route uses it. E-470 takes no cash, and without an ExpressToll account the toll is billed by plate at a higher rate. Tell us how far you’d drive and whether you’d rather avoid toll roads, and we weigh dealers by that drive.
