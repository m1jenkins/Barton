The Camry is the midsize sedan most buyers should start with, and since 2025 every one is a hybrid: a front-drive LE is rated 51 mpg combined, U.S. News ranks it first of nine hybrid cars, and IIHS names it a 2026 Top Safety Pick+, a grade above the Honda Accord’s Top Safety Pick. It holds its value, too, losing 22% in three years by iSeeCars’ count against 24.8% for the average vehicle. The catches: Edmunds and U.S. News both find the engine coarse when you push it, and Edmunds rates the 15.1-cubic-foot trunk smaller on paper than the Accord’s or Hyundai Sonata’s. It’s also easier to discount than its tight supply suggests: Edmunds’ Texas prices run about 7–8% under sticker, and CarWhere’s 90 verified buyers paid a median 6.2% under MSRP. Price a 2026 XLE, aim for 4–5% under MSRP, and take Toyota’s 4.99% financing if your bank can’t beat it. Prices and offers were checked on November 17, 2026.

What to pay: how far under MSRP

Camrys sell under sticker even though they’re scarce. Cox Automotive counted 28 days of supply at the end of October, against 39 for Toyota and 87 for the industry, and Edmunds says new Camrys sell in a median 10 days. Yet Edmunds’ Texas suggested prices sit about 7–8% under sticker with destination on every trim, CarWhere’s 90 verified 2026 buyers paid a median 6.2% under MSRP (the middle half 5.1–8.3%), and iSeeCars puts dealer invoice about 8% under MSRP. Kelley Blue Book’s fair purchase price, only $295 to $395 under MSRP, is far less generous.

The trim we’d buy

A 2026 XLE. At $34,800 it costs $2,700 more than an SE, and the step up buys a 12.3-inch touchscreen and 12.3-inch digital gauges in place of an 8-inch screen and 7-inch gauges, heated leather-and-microfiber front seats, a power passenger seat and acoustic glass in the front doors. It’s rated 46 mpg combined, the same as an SE, and it’s Edmunds’ pick of the line. AWD adds $1,525 and costs nothing in the EPA’s figures on the XLE (46 mpg combined either way), so take it if you see snow.

Pick the LE ($29,600) if price and mileage come first: it’s rated 51 mpg combined, about 25 fewer gallons a year than an XLE at 12,000 miles, but it has cloth seats and an 8-inch screen. Skip the XSE with AWD: its 19-inch wheels drop it to 43 mpg combined for $1,200 more than an AWD XLE.

If you buy used, look at a 2022 to 2024 gas Camry or a 2025 hybrid, and skip the 2019 and 2020. Those two drew 417 and 287 NHTSA owner complaints, more than any Camry since, and their fuel pumps were recalled. The 2022 to 2024 drew 53 to 69 a year. What to watch for at the dealer

  • Dealer add-ons. Toyota dealers put add-ons on 37.7% of the quotes CarWhere studied, averaging $1,637. On a $36,000 XLE, that’s most of a fair discount. Paint protection, nitrogen and etched VINs are the dealer’s products. Refuse or negotiate them.
  • The model year. A 2026 and a 2027 look alike on the lot. The 2027 costs $100 more, and its ToyotaCare covers one year or 10,000 miles against two years or 25,000.
  • The inverter recall. NHTSA recall 25V869 covers some 2025 and 2026 Camrys with an inverter bolt that may not be tight, which can cut power or cause a fire. Ask the dealer to show the inverter was replaced before you take delivery.
  • The Toyota Financial Services requirement. The 4.99% and the college rebate need Toyota’s own financing. Compare the total cost with your bank’s rate.
  • The wrong wheels. An AWD XSE is rated 43 mpg combined against 46 for an AWD XLE and 50 for an AWD LE. Check the window sticker if fuel economy is why you’re buying.
  • Which year the rate covers. Toyota’s 4.99% was on the 2027 only in Chicago and on the 2026 only in Los Angeles. Ask which model year the dealer’s quote assumes.

Common questions

Is the Toyota Camry a good car?

Most reviewers rank it at or near the top of its class. U.S. News scores the 2026 a 9.4 out of 10, first of nine hybrid cars, and named it the 2026 Best Midsize Hybrid Car for the Money. Edmunds rates it 8.4, Kelley Blue Book 4.8 out of 5 with its Best Buy Award, and J.D. Power owners 82 out of 100. Consumer Reports expects it to be more reliable than the average new car, and iSeeCars counts 22% of its value lost in three years, against 24.8% for the average vehicle.

How much under MSRP can you get a Toyota Camry?

More than on most Toyotas. On a 2026 LE, SE or XSE, 5–6% under MSRP is a fair deal and 7% or more a strong one; the Nightshade and XLE are harder to find, so 4–5% is fair. Those ranges are our read of Edmunds’ Texas prices (about 7–8% under sticker), CarWhere’s 90 verified buyers (a median 6.2% under) and iSeeCars’ invoice figures, so a given dealer may land a point either side. Toyota has no cash back to add on top.

Should I buy a 2026 or a 2027 Camry?

The 2026, for most buyers. It costs $100 less with the same engine and equipment, and it keeps two years or 25,000 miles of ToyotaCare maintenance, which Toyota cut to one year or 10,000 miles for the 2027. Buy the 2027 if Toyota’s 4.99% covers only the 2027 in your region, as it did in Chicago.

Is every Camry a hybrid?

Yes, since the 2025 redesign. Every Camry has a 2.5-liter four-cylinder hybrid with 225 hp, or 232 hp with AWD. The EPA rates the front-drive LE at 51 mpg combined and the other front-drive trims at 46. Toyota covers the hybrid battery for 10 years or 150,000 miles and the other hybrid parts for 8 years or 100,000.

Is AWD worth it on a Camry?

If you see snow, yes. It adds $1,525 on every trim and costs little in fuel: the EPA rates an AWD SE or XLE at 46 mpg combined, the same as front drive, and an AWD LE at 50 against 51. The AWD XSE drops to 43 because of its 19-inch wheels.

What is the destination charge on a Camry?

$1,295 on the 2026, up from $1,195 on the 2025. Toyota’s advertised starting prices leave it out.

Does Toyota offer cash back on the Camry?

No. In Chicago, Dallas, Los Angeles and New York on November 17, 2026, Toyota offered 4.99% financing for 48 months and lease deals, but no customer cash. The only money off is a $500 military or college rebate.

Is a used Camry a better deal than new?

Rarely. CarWhere’s median asking price for a used 2025 is $27,989 at about 40,000 miles, and Edmunds’ Texas price for a new 2026 LE is $28,450 with destination. Used loans averaged 11.19% in the second quarter of 2026, against Toyota’s 4.99% on a new one. A Gold Certified 2022 to 2025 is the used route we’d take.
