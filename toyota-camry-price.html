The bottom line

New, as a 2026

The Camry is the midsize sedan most buyers should start with, and every one is a hybrid: a front-drive LE is rated 51 mpg combined. Edmunds and U.S. News both find the engine coarse when you push it. Edmunds’ Texas prices run about 7–8% under sticker. Price a 2026 XLE and aim for 4–5% under MSRP.

How we set these discount targets

Camrys sell under sticker even though they’re scarce. Cox Automotive counted 28 days of supply at the end of October, against 39 for Toyota and 87 for the industry, and Edmunds says new Camrys sell in a median 10 days. Yet Edmunds’ Texas suggested prices sit about 7–8% under sticker with destination on every trim, CarWhere’s 90 verified 2026 buyers paid a median 6.2% under MSRP (the middle half 5.1–8.3%), and iSeeCars puts dealer invoice about 8% under MSRP. Kelley Blue Book’s fair purchase price, only $295 to $395 under MSRP, is far less generous.

New 2026

Best when: Most buyers. It costs $100 less than the 2027 and keeps two years or 25,000 miles of ToyotaCare.

Check: That the car is a 2026, that Toyota’s rate covers the 2026 in your region, and that inverter recall 25V869 is done if the car is on it.

New 2027

Best when: Toyota’s rate covers only the 2027 where you live (the Midwest, when we checked), or the color you want is only on a 2027.

Check: The $100 price rise, and ToyotaCare cut to one year or 10,000 miles.

CPO (Toyota Certified Gold)

Best when: You want a 2022 to 2025 with a warranty. In Southern California and Greater New York, Toyota’s 5.99% helps.

Check: That it’s Gold Certified, not Silver. Gold adds a 12-month/12,000-mile comprehensive warranty and 7-year/100,000-mile powertrain coverage; Silver gives 12 months of powertrain cover only. A 2025’s hybrid battery stays covered for 10 years/150,000 miles from first sale.

Used, not certified

Best when: Your budget is under about $23,000.

Check: Open recalls by VIN, an independent inspection, and on a 2025, that inverter recall 25V869 and display recall 26V511 are done.

Buying used? Look at a 2022 to 2024 gas Camry or a 2025 hybrid, and skip the 2019 and 2020. Those two drew 417 and 287 NHTSA owner complaints, and their fuel pumps were recalled. Check any used Camry’s open recalls by VIN.

What to watch for at the dealer

  • Dealer add-ons. Toyota dealers put add-ons on 37.7% of the quotes CarWhere studied, averaging $1,637. Paint protection, nitrogen and etched VINs are the dealer’s products: refuse or negotiate them.
  • The model year. A 2026 and a 2027 look alike. The 2027 costs $100 more, and its ToyotaCare covers one year or 10,000 miles against two years or 25,000.
  • The inverter recall. NHTSA recall 25V869 covers some 2025 and 2026 Camrys. A loose inverter bolt can cut power or cause a fire. Ask for proof the inverter was replaced.
  • Toyota financing. The 4.99% and the college rebate need Toyota Financial Services. Compare the total cost with your bank’s rate.
  • Which year the rate covers. Toyota’s 4.99% was on the 2027 only in Chicago and the 2026 only in Los Angeles. Ask which model year the dealer’s quote assumes.

Is the Toyota Camry a good car?

Most reviewers rank it at or near the top of its class. U.S. News scores the 2026 a 9.4 out of 10, first of nine hybrid cars. Edmunds rates it 8.4 and Kelley Blue Book 4.8 out of 5 with its Best Buy Award.

How much under MSRP can you get a Toyota Camry?

On a 2026 LE, SE or XSE, 5–6% under MSRP is a fair deal and 7% or more a strong one. The Nightshade and XLE are harder to find, so 4–5% is fair. That’s our read of Edmunds, CarWhere and iSeeCars figures.

Should I buy a 2026 or a 2027 Camry?

The 2026, for most buyers. It costs $100 less with the same engine and equipment, and it keeps two years or 25,000 miles of ToyotaCare. Buy the 2027 if Toyota’s 4.99% covers only the 2027 in your region, as it did in Chicago.

Is every Camry a hybrid?

Yes, since the 2025 redesign. Every Camry has a 2.5-liter four-cylinder hybrid with 225 hp, or 232 hp with AWD. The EPA rates the front-drive LE at 51 mpg combined and the other front-drive trims at 46.

Is AWD worth it on a Camry?

If you see snow, yes. It adds $1,525 on every trim and costs little in fuel: the EPA rates an AWD SE or XLE at 46 mpg combined, the same as front drive. The AWD XSE drops to 43.

What is the destination charge on a Camry?

$1,295 on the 2026, up from $1,195 on the 2025. Toyota’s advertised starting prices leave it out.

Does Toyota offer cash back on the Camry?

No. On November 17, 2026, Toyota offered 4.99% financing for 48 months and lease deals in the four regions we checked, but no customer cash. The only money off is a $500 military or college rebate.

Is a used Camry a better deal than new?

Rarely. A used 2025 asks a median $27,989 at about 40,000 miles, and Edmunds’ Texas price for a new 2026 LE is $28,450 with destination. Used loans averaged 11.19% against Toyota’s 4.99%. A Gold Certified 2022 to 2025 is the used route we’d take.
