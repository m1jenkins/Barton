New 2026

Best when: Most buyers. The runout discounts and GMC’s diesel offers are on the 2026, and the SLE and SLT exist only as 2026s.

Check: That the truck is a 2026, which engine it has, and that the offer runs in your region.

New 2027

Best when: You want the new 5.7L or 6.6L V8, the redesigned cabin, or the 5.7L’s 13,500-pound tow rating.

Check: The price rise ($4,800 on the Denali) and that GMC lists only one 2027 offer, a lease in Chicago.

CPO (GM CarBravo)

Best when: You want a 2023 to 2025 truck with some cover, and you’re paying cash.

Check: CarBravo’s certified warranty is 12 months/12,000 miles bumper to bumper, starting after any factory cover left. GMC’s powertrain warranty runs 5 years/60,000 miles from first sale.

Used, not certified

Best when: Your budget is under about $40,000.

Check: Open recalls by VIN, an independent inspection, and on a 6.2L V8, proof that recall 25V274’s engine inspection was done.

Buying used? Start at 2023. The 2019 and 2020 trucks carry 10 and 12 recalls, and 2021 to 2024 trucks with the 6.2L V8 were recalled in 2025 for defects that can wreck the engine. Owners are suing GM over lifter failures on its V8s.

What to watch for at the dealer

  • Which engine. GMC’s cash and 0% apply only to the 3.0L Duramax, and you get one or the other. Make sure a quote names the engine.
  • Dealer add-ons. GMC dealers put them on 36.4% of the quotes CarWhere studied, averaging $1,441. Paint protection, nitrogen and etched VINs are the dealer’s products: refuse or negotiate them.
  • Fees. Documentation fees run from about $85 in California to $999 in Florida, by CarEdge’s count. Compare out-the-door prices.
  • The 6.2L V8. On a 2021 to 2024 truck, ask for proof that recall 25V274’s inspection was done. NHTSA’s open investigation also covers 2025 and 2026 engines.
  • The transfer case recall. A few 2026 4WD trucks are under recall 26V289 and shouldn’t be driven until fixed. Have the dealer check the VIN before delivery.

Is the GMC Sierra 1500 a good truck?

Reviewers split on it. U.S. News scores the 2026 a 9.0 out of 10 and Kelley Blue Book 4.3 out of 5. Edmunds gives it 6.7, faulting the ride and fuel economy. Consumer Reports expects it to be less reliable than the average new car.

How much under MSRP can you get a GMC Sierra 1500?

On a 2026 SLE, Elevation or SLT, 5–7% under MSRP is a fair deal and 8–10% a strong one. The Pro has less room, about 2–3%. That’s our read of TrueCar’s averages and iSeeCars’ invoice figures. GMC’s diesel cash comes on top.

Should I buy a 2026 or a 2027 Sierra 1500?

The 2026, unless you want the new truck. It costs less (the Denali starts $4,800 lower), the SLE and SLT exist only as 2026s, and GMC’s cash and 0% are on the 2026 diesel. The 2027 brings new V8s and a redesigned cabin.

What is the destination charge on a Sierra 1500?

$2,795 on the 2026 and the 2027. GMC’s advertised starting prices leave it out.

Does GMC offer cash back on the Sierra 1500?

Only on the diesel. On November 16, 2026, GMC offered $8,250 on a 2026 Sierra 1500 with the 3.0L Duramax, or 0% for 60 months plus $2,000. Gas 2026 trucks got lease deals only. The offers end November 30.

Is the Sierra’s 6.2L V8 reliable?

It has a record. GM recalled nearly 600,000 2021 to 2024 trucks and SUVs with the 6.2L in 2025, and NHTSA has since opened a wider investigation covering 2021 to 2026 engines. Until that closes, we’d take the 5.3L V8 or the diesel.

Is the Sierra 1500 the same truck as the Silverado?

They’re closely related and offer the same four engines. The Sierra costs more (the 2026 Pro starts at $38,600, against $37,200 for a Silverado WT) and is finished a step higher inside. Price both and let the better deal decide.
