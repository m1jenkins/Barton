The New York car market right now

On November 15, 2026, dealers within 50 miles of Midtown Manhattan listed about 185,000 new cars and 122,000 used ones. That was the most of the six metros we checked. Honda, Toyota and Ford had the most new cars on the lot, and the median asking price was about $49,300 new and $24,900 used.

Buying a car around New York

Work out how you’ll get to the dealer

You pick up the car at the dealer, so a buyer without a car needs a route there and a plan to drive home. A store on Long Island, in Westchester or in northern New Jersey can sit close on the map and still take a train, a ride and a bridge or tunnel to reach. Tell us which train line or crossing you’d use, and we ask the dealers you can actually reach.

Ask where a used car spent its winters

New York, New Jersey and Connecticut salt their roads when it snows, and a car parked on the street all winter takes more of it than one kept in a garage. The rust starts underneath, on the brake lines, the exhaust, the subframe and the rocker panels below the doors. Ask the dealer where the car has lived. On a used search we can ask for photos of the underside, and you can have a mechanic you choose put the car on a lift before you sign.

Tell us where the car will park

Many New York buyers park on the street or rent a garage space, and a car’s length and width can rule it out before its price does. Tell us your limits when you start, and we’ll say whether your model fits before we ask any dealer for a price.

How it works from New York

  1. You pay the flat fee and tell us the car. Model, trims and colors you’d accept, budget, and how far you’d go to pick it up. Mason replies within one business day to confirm the search and when it starts.
  2. We find the cars. Your advisor searches new, used and CPO dealer inventory for the model.
  3. We get written offers and negotiate. Each dealer is asked for an out-the-door price in writing. Offers go against each other, and add-ons and padded fees get questioned before you see a contract.
  4. You pick. We send written recommendations and our pick of the best deal. You make the final call, sign the paperwork and pick up the car from the dealer.

Most searches take one to two weeks from payment to a signed deal. Rare specs and factory orders take longer, and we tell you that up front. We can’t promise a specific discount: the market for a given car sets what a dealer will do. The full process is on how it works.

What stays with you

You decide which car, if any, to buy. You arrange the inspection, pick the financing and insurance, read the contract and sign it. Our fee does not include the car, taxes, registration, inspection, financing, insurance or delivery. We don’t give tax or registration advice for your state; the dealer’s paperwork and your state’s motor vehicle agency are the sources for that.

Common questions

Do you have an office in New York?

No. We’re based in Austin, Texas, and we work with New York-area dealers by phone and email. You deal with one advisor the whole way, and you pick up the car from the dealer.

Can you get offers from New Jersey and Connecticut dealers as well as New York ones?

Yes, on any plan. The Steer Me Right plan ($395) covers dealers within 200 miles of your ZIP code, whichever state they are in, and the New York, New Jersey and Connecticut dealers in the circle are all inside that. Steer Me Right Pro ($695) covers dealers nationwide.

I don’t own a car. Can I still use a car buying service?

Yes. You pick up the car at the dealer like every buyer. Tell us which train line or crossing you’d use and how long a trip you’d make, and we weigh how easy each dealer is to reach alongside its price. Some dealers will deliver the car to you; whether they do, and what they charge, is up to them.

Can you tell if a used car has road-salt rust?

Only from photos. We can ask the dealer for pictures of the underside and send them to you. Photos miss things, so have a mechanic you choose put the car on a lift before you sign, especially on a car that spent winters parked on the street.

How much does a car buying service cost in New York?

Steer Me Right charges the same flat fee everywhere: $395 for one model or $695 for up to three. Most services where a person negotiates for you charge $999 or more, by their own published prices; see car buying services compared.

Do dealers pay you?

No. Dealers and lenders pay us nothing. You pay the flat fee, and you’re the only one we work for.
