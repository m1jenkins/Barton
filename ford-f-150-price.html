The trim we’d buy

A 2026 XLT SuperCrew 4x4. At MSRP it costs $2,840 more than an STX, but in most regions Ford’s $4,500 cash applies to the XLT alone, so it can come out cheaper. It also opens up the options most buyers want, such as a 360-degree camera, heated seats and BlueCruise (hands-free driving on mapped highways). And about half of all used F-150s for sale are XLTs, which makes it the easiest one to sell later.

If you want leather, cooled seats and the 360 camera as standard, the Lariat discounts about as well, though it starts $14,865 higher. We’d skip the PowerBoost hybrid unless you’ll use its onboard generator (outlets in the bed that run tools and appliances). It loses value faster than the gas truck, and Consumer Reports ranked it among its least reliable vehicles in 2022 and 2023.

What to pay: how far under MSRP

Ford dealers have more trucks than they want. At the end of September they held 101 days of supply against 82 for the industry, and the 2027s have arrived since, so the 2026s on the lot need to go. TrueCar’s November figures show the average 2026 XLT selling 5.6% under MSRP and the average Lariat 6.5% under.

What to watch for at the dealer

  • Dealer add-ons. Bedliners, tint and theft-recovery devices are the dealer’s products, not Ford’s. Refuse or negotiate them.
  • The model year. A 2026 and a 2027 look alike on the lot. The offers apply only to the 2026.
  • Rebates dressed up as a discount. Get the selling price first, then each incentive on its own line.
  • The Ford Credit requirement. Finance elsewhere and the bonus cash tied to 0% or 1.9% goes away.
  • Fees. Documentation fees differ by hundreds of dollars. Compare out-the-door prices.
  • Raptor R markups. Owner forums report $5,000 or more over sticker. MSRP is a good result.

Common questions

Is the Ford F-150 a good truck?

The reviewers think so. Edmunds rates the 2026 an 8.0 out of 10 and first of five large trucks, and U.S. News ranks it first of six full-size pickups. The weak spots are owner complaints about harsh shifts from the 10-speed automatic and a long recall list on 2021 and 2022 trucks.

How much under MSRP can you get a Ford F-150?

On a 2026 XLT or Lariat, 5–7% under MSRP is a fair deal before Ford’s cash and about 10% is a strong one. XL and STX have less room, around 2–3%. Raptors sell at about sticker. Those ranges are our read of TrueCar’s November sale prices, so a given dealer may land a point or two either side.

Should I buy a 2026 or a 2027 F-150?

The 2026, for most buyers. It carries Ford’s year-end offers and dealers want it gone now that 2027s are arriving. The 2027 makes sense if you want the new 3.0L engine or the lower starting price on Lariat, Tremor or Raptor.

What is the destination charge on an F-150?

$2,795 on most 2026 trucks and on the 2027. Ford’s advertised starting prices leave it out, and it is on every window sticker. A few early 2026 trucks show $2,595.

Can I combine 0% financing with the $4,500 cash back?

Plan on no. Ford’s 0% and 1.9% offers come with $500 or $1,500 in bonus cash, and the full $4,500 appears only as a cash offer. Ask the dealer to price the truck both ways in writing.

Is the F-150 hybrid worth it?

Only if you will use the onboard generator. It loses value faster than the gas truck by iSeeCars’ figures, and the early hybrids scored poorly for reliability with Consumer Reports. Consumer Reports now predicts about average reliability for the 2026.

Is a used F-150 a better deal than new?

Not by much right now. A three-year-old F-150 has lost about 19% of its value, against 25% for the average vehicle, and used loans averaged 11.19% in the second quarter of 2026 against 6.35% for new. The new 2026 also has 0% financing or cash back. A Gold Certified 2023 or 2024 is the used route we’d take.
