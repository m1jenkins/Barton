Best when: You are paying cash and want Lariat-level equipment for XLT money. A 2023 or 2024 is the sweet spot.

Check: That it is Gold Certified, not Blue. Gold adds a 12-month, 12,000-mile comprehensive warranty and 7-year, 100,000-mile powertrain coverage. Blue gives 90 days.

What to watch for at the dealer

  • Dealer add-ons. Bedliners, tint and theft-recovery devices are the dealer’s products. Refuse or negotiate them.
  • The model year. A 2026 and a 2027 look alike on the lot. The offers apply only to the 2026.
  • Rebates dressed up as a discount. Get the selling price first, then each incentive on its own line.
  • The Ford Credit requirement. Finance elsewhere and the bonus cash tied to 0% or 1.9% goes away.
  • Fees. Documentation fees differ by hundreds of dollars. Compare out-the-door prices.

Is the Ford F-150 a good truck?

Reviewers think so. Edmunds rates the 2026 an 8.0 out of 10 and first of five large trucks, and U.S. News ranks it first of six full-size pickups. Owners report harsh shifts from the 10-speed automatic, and 2021 and 2022 trucks have long recall lists.

How much under MSRP can you get a Ford F-150?

On a 2026 XLT or Lariat, 5–7% under MSRP is fair and about 10% strong, give or take a point or two. That’s our read of TrueCar’s November sale prices, which may include Ford’s cash. Count it as extra only if the quote itemizes it.

Should I buy a 2026 or a 2027 F-150?

The 2026, for most buyers. It carries Ford’s year-end offers and dealers want it gone now that 2027s are arriving. The 2027 makes sense if you want the new 3.0L engine or the lower starting price on Lariat, Tremor or Raptor.

What is the destination charge on an F-150?

$2,795 on most 2026 trucks and on the 2027. Ford’s advertised starting prices leave it out, and it’s on every window sticker. A few early 2026 trucks show $2,595.

Can I combine 0% financing with the $4,500 cash back?

Plan on no. Ford’s 0% and 1.9% offers come with $500 or $1,500 in bonus cash, and the full $4,500 appears only as a cash offer. Ask the dealer to price the truck both ways in writing.

Is the F-150 hybrid worth it?

Only if you’ll use the onboard generator. It loses value faster than the gas truck by iSeeCars’ figures, and the early hybrids scored poorly for reliability with Consumer Reports. Consumer Reports now predicts about average reliability for the 2026.

Is a used F-150 a better deal than new?

Not by much right now. A three-year-old F-150 has lost about 19% of its value, against 25% for the average vehicle, and used loans averaged 11.19% against 6.35% for new. A Gold Certified 2023 or 2024 is the used route we’d take.
