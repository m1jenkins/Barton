Dealers and lenders pay us nothing.

A used Model 3 is the cheapest way into a Tesla, and the 2023 is where the money is. A 2023 averaged $27,800 in dealer listings in September, against $37,300 for a 2024 and $38,630 for the cheapest new Model 3 with fees. The 2024 is the redesign, with a quieter cabin and Hardware 4; the 2023 is the last of the original body, with the longest EPA range of any version before 2024 (358 miles on the Long Range AWD). Shop a 2023 Long Range AWD, aim for about 1–3% under asking, and check every open recall by VIN. Prices were checked on November 18, 2026.

  • A 2023 lists for about $10,800 less than a new Standard with fees
  • 358 miles of EPA range on a 2023 Long Range AWD
  • Battery and drive-unit warranty transfers from any seller
  • Five-star NHTSA ratings on the 2019, 2022, 2023 and 2026 AWD

  • 18 to 22 recall campaigns each on 2019–2022 cars

  • No Apple CarPlay or Android Auto, Edmunds says
  • Tesla’s used warranty covers only cars Tesla sells

The used Model 3 we’d buy

A 2023 Long Range AWD. At an average asking price of $27,800 in September, a 2023 costs about $9,500 less than a 2024, and Kelley Blue Book puts the Long Range AWD’s fair price at $30,100. It has the longest EPA range of any original-body Model 3 (358 miles, against 342 for a 2024 Long Range AWD) and is quick: Edmunds timed the equivalent Long Range AWD at 4.2 seconds to 60 mph. You give up Hardware 3 (every 2017–2023 car has it, and the 2024 got Hardware 4), a quieter cabin and ventilated seats, and you take on 12 recall campaigns against the 2024’s 5.

If you’d rather have the newer car, a 2024 Long Range RWD is rated at 363 miles and costs about $9,500 more. We’d skip a 2019 or 2020 unless the price allows for it: they carry 21 to 22 recall campaigns each, and their battery cover ends in 2027 or 2028. The Performance gives up about 43 miles of EPA range to a Long Range AWD of the same year (315 against 358 on a 2023).

What to watch for at the dealer

  • Open recalls. Run the VIN at NHTSA. Tesla will replace the headlights on 2017–2023 cars (26V507, low beams too bright), but the repair isn’t available yet and owners get another notice when it is. 2021–2023 cars have a rearview-camera delay recall (26V283) fixed by software update, and 2025 cars a battery-pack contactor recall (25V690) that needs a service visit.
  • Hardware 3 or 4. Every 2017–2023 Model 3 has Hardware 3, and the redesigned 2024 has Hardware 4. Tesla says Hardware 3 can’t run unsupervised FSD. Look under Settings, Software, Additional Vehicle Information for the Autopilot computer.
  • FSD in the listing. Tesla now sells FSD only as a subscription, and says FSD bought outright stays with the car. If a listing charges extra for it, check that it shows on the car’s screen before you pay.
  • The “warranty” on the window. A dealer’s warranty on a used Model 3 is its own or a third party’s. Tesla’s used warranty covers only cars Tesla sells. Ask for the first delivery date to see how much battery cover is left.
  • Suspension, brakes and lights. Germany’s TÜV failed 13.1% of two- to three-year-old Model 3s at inspection, against about 6.5% for that age group, and Tesla’s problem areas were suspension parts, rusted brake discs and lights. Have an independent shop look the car over before you buy.
  • Add-ons and fees. Used-car dealers add paint protection, etching and service contracts. Compare out-the-door prices, and refuse what you didn’t ask for.

Common questions

How much is a used Tesla Model 3?

Dealers listed them at an average of $21,100 for a 2019 up to $38,200 for a 2025 in September 2026, by Recurrent’s count. Each 10,000 miles takes about $1,000 off a car of the same year. A new Model 3 starts at $36,990 before fees.

How much under asking can you get a used Model 3?

On a 2024 or 2025, 1–2% under asking is a fair deal and 3–4% a strong one; on a 2019 to 2023, about 1% is fair and 2–3% strong. Those ranges are our estimate, since nobody publishes sale prices for used Model 3s, and Tesla doesn’t negotiate on its own used cars.

What is the best year for a used Model 3?

The 2023 Long Range AWD, in our view: about $9,500 below the average 2024 asking price, with 358 miles of EPA range. Buy a 2024 or later if you want Hardware 4, the quieter cabin and ventilated front seats.

Does the Tesla battery warranty transfer to a used buyer?

Yes, at no cost, whoever sells you the car. It runs 8 years or 120,000 miles on a Long Range or Performance and 8 years or 100,000 miles on a Standard or Standard Range Plus, counted from first delivery, and covers capacity that falls below 70%.

Does a used Model 3 from a dealer come with Tesla’s warranty?

Not Tesla’s used warranty. Its 1-year/10,000-mile cover applies only to used cars Tesla sells and delivers itself. The remainder of the original 4-year/50,000-mile basic warranty and the battery warranty do carry over.

Does Full Self-Driving transfer with a used Model 3?

Tesla says FSD bought outright stays with the car, and it now sells FSD only as a subscription, which doesn’t carry over. Check that FSD shows on the car’s own screen before you pay extra for it, and check for Hardware 4: Tesla says Hardware 3 cars can’t run unsupervised FSD.

Is a used Model 3 reliable?

Mixed. U.S. News predicts 82 out of 100 for the 2026. Owners of three-year-old Teslas reported 226 problems per 100 vehicles in J.D. Power’s 2026 dependability study, against an industry average of 204, and Germany’s TÜV failed 13.1% of two- to three-year-old Model 3s at inspection.
