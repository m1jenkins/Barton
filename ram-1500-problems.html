The best used Ram 1500s are the 2023 and 2024 model years, at 47 and 42 NHTSA owner complaints per 100,000 sold, and the 2020 and 2021 at 115 and 122. Skip the 2016: it draws 322, with engine (356), steering (318) and brake (290) complaints each in the hundreds. The 2019 (237 and 30 recalls, the most of any year), the 2017, the 2022 and the 2025 (187, with 203 of 382 complaints naming the electrical system) are Check first.

These counts include the Ram 1500 Classic, the old-body truck Ram sold beside the new one from 2019 to 2024, because NHTSA files its complaints and recalls under the same 1500 name and we can’t separate them. The rates also rest on weak sales figures: Ram published no 1500-only total before 2024, so 2016 to 2023 divide by all Ram pickups (1500, Classic and heavy duty), which understates those rates, while 2024 and 2025 divide by Ram’s light-duty pickups. Treat the step from the 2023 to the 2024 as partly a change of denominator. The 2026 is too new to rate; our Ram 1500 price guide covers what it costs new.

Complaints are owner reports to NHTSA, counted once each across every Ram 1500 listing (all the trim and body-style listings) and read on November 21, 2026. Each rate divides a model year’s complaints by the U.S. sales of the same calendar year. Model years and calendar years don’t line up, since a model year’s cars sell across two calendar years, so the rate compares years with each other. Ram published no 1500-only sales before 2024. The 2016 to 2021 figures are Ram pickup totals (1500, Classic and heavy duty) from carfigures.com, 2022 is the Ram pickup total reported by Auto Dealer Today and 2023 is Motor1’s Ram figure; 2024 and 2025 are Ram’s light-duty pickups from the FCA US release. The first eight years therefore divide by more trucks than the 1500 and understate its rate. Our verdicts use this car’s own yardstick, the median of 122 complaints per 100,000 across its 2019 to 2025 model years: Buy up to 1.25 times it, Check first up to twice it, Avoid above that. A recall, free or not, never moves a verdict. A year can move when NHTSA has an open investigation on it or one part carries most of its complaints,; the reason column says so.

Ram 1500 generations: what changed

Fourth generation (DS) 2009–2018

The 2016 to 2018 model years are this truck. It got a new front end, a 3.6-liter Pentastar V6, an optional eight-speed automatic and electric power steering for 2013, and a 3.0-liter EcoDiesel for 2014 (Wikipedia).

Ram 1500 Classic 2019–2024

When the fifth generation arrived, Ram kept the old-body truck in production as a lower-priced, commercial-oriented model through the 2024 model year (Wikipedia). NHTSA files its complaints and recalls under the 1500 name, so the 2019 to 2024 counts here include it, and Ram’s sales totals include it too.

Fifth generation (DT) 2019–

It debuted at the January 2018 Detroit auto show as a 2019 model with an optional eTorque mild-hybrid system on the 5.7-liter Hemi, a 12-inch touchscreen and about 225 pounds less weight. The 2025 model year swapped the Hemi for the 3.0-liter Hurricane twin-turbo six, and the Hemi V8 returned for 2026 (Wikipedia).

  • Engine 2016–2022 model years1,658 complaints naming engine, 44 per 100,000 sold. Engine tops the 2016 (356) and the 2020 to 2022 trucks (168, 284 and 257). Recall 23V-265 covers 2021 5.7-liter eTorque trucks whose engine software can stall the engine, and NHTSA’s open PE24018 looks at stalls on 2022 trucks that FCA says may have an electrical cause. The 3.0-liter EcoDiesel (2014–2019) has its own recalls: 19V-757 (a cracking EGR cooler that can cause a fire in the intake manifold) and 22V-406 (a high-pressure fuel pump that can starve the engine).
  • Steering 2016–2019 model years981 complaints naming steering, 45 per 100,000 sold. Steering leads the 2016 (318), the 2017 (231) and the 2019 (342). Recall 16V-167 covers a short on the electric power steering board of 2015–2016 trucks, 19V-812 covers a contaminated steering gear on 2019 trucks, and NHTSA’s open RQ23003 looks at 380 reports of lost power steering assist on 2013–2016 trucks that may fall outside the 2016 recall.
  • Electrical system 2018–2026 model years1,475 complaints naming electrical system, 41 per 100,000 sold. Electrical is the top part on the 2018, the 2019 (384), the 2023 to 2026 trucks and the 2025 above all (203). NHTSA’s label names a part, not a failure. The 2025–2026 instrument cluster recalls (25V-826, 26V-225) and the 2019 cluster recall (19V-556) are electrical, and owners on Reddit report eTorque faults and dead batteries.
  • Transmission and drivetrain 2021–2022 model years213 complaints naming transmission and drivetrain, 21 per 100,000 sold. Power train draws 103 complaints on the 2021 and 110 on the 2022. Recall 24V-413 covers a weld in the transmission control unit on 2023 trucks, and 20V-080 covered a transfer case gear on 2019 four-wheel-drive trucks; neither explains the 2021 and 2022 counts.

What owners report on Reddit

Reliability is the topic Reddit raises most about the Ram 1500: 508 mentions in 265 threads, 166 positive and 263 negative, a net of −25 on a scale from −100 to +100. Our Reddit Sentiment Index for the Ram 1500 is 51 out of 100 (50 is even). The threads run from January 2025 to November 2026 and come from r/ram_trucks, r/Ram1500 and r/RamTRX and the rest of Reddit, so they say little about a car that owners stopped discussing before 2025. The Ram 1500 price guide has the full topic breakdown.

Owners on Reddit report more than 100,000 miles on the 5.7 Hemi without major repairs and trouble-free early miles on the Hurricane six. What they say goes wrong is the Hemi’s lifter and camshaft failures and cracked exhaust manifolds, eTorque mild-hybrid faults including dead batteries, and air suspension failures in cold weather. The threads rarely name a model year, so they agree with the engine and electrical counts without confirming any one year.

Recalls worth knowing about

  • Differential pin screw 2016–2017Recall 17V-198. The differential pin retaining screw can come loose while driving, which can break or lock the differential. Dealers inspect and replace the retaining pin, free.
  • Power steering contamination 2015–2016 and 2019Recall 16V-167 and 19V-812. Contamination in the electric power steering unit can short out and cut assist. Dealers replace the unit (2015–2016) or the gear assembly (2019), free.
  • Air bags disabled 2019–2020Recall 19V-407. Corrupted memory in the occupant restraint controller can disable the air bags and seat belt pretensioners. Dealers reprogram or replace the controller, free.
  • EGR cooler fire risk 2014–2019 EcoDieselRecall 19V-757. The EGR cooler on 3.0-liter diesels may crack and let coolant vapor combust in the intake manifold. Dealers replace the cooler and inspect the manifold, free.
  • Engine stall 2021 5.7-liter eTorqueRecall 23V-265. Powertrain control software can create an incorrect fuel mixture and stall the engine. Dealers update the calibration, free.
  • Stability control disabled 2019, 2021–2024Recall 24V-653. An ABS control module software fault may disable electronic stability control. Dealers update the software, free.
  • Blank instrument cluster 2025–2026Recall 25V-826 and 26V-225. A software error can leave the instrument cluster display blank or failed, hiding warning lights and gear selection. Dealers update the software or replace the cluster, free.
  • Rear seat belt anchors 2019–2026Recall 26V-495. The second-row center and left-side seat belt buckle anchors may have been installed wrong. Dealers inspect and attach them to the body, free; owner letters were expected beginning August 18, 2026.
  • ABS module and brake lights 2022–2024 ClassicRecall 26V-756. Filed November 19, 2026 for the Classic: ABS software may misread the brake pedal, which can light the brake lights wrongly, let the truck move unintentionally or disable stability control. Dealers update the software, free; owner letters are expected January 14, 2027.

Open and recent NHTSA investigations

  • Engine stalls on 5.7-liter eTorque trucks 2022PE24018, open. NHTSA opened it in July 2024 after 80 complaints that 2022 Ram 1500s and Jeep Wagoneers with the 5.7-liter eTorque system stall, often at slow speed, with a battery warning light. FCA told NHTSA the cause is probably not the fuel mixture behind recall 23V-265 and may be electrical.
  • Loss of power steering assist 2013–2016RQ23003, open. NHTSA opened a recall query in August 2023 after 380 reports of intermittent or complete loss of power steering assist, to check whether FCA’s 2016 recall 16V-167 (440 trucks) covered the right trucks.
  • Rollaway with the rotary shifter 2013–2016 and laterPE16014, closed. NHTSA closed it in January 2024. It found no design or manufacturing defect behind 43 rollaway incidents, and said FCA’s customer service notifications, which secure the vehicle when the driver exits without reaching Park, reduced them.

What to check before you buy a used Ram 1500

  • Open recalls by VIN.Enter the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. A recall fix is free at a Ram dealer, and the lookup shows which ones are still open on that exact car.
  • Whether it is a Classic.On a 2019 to 2024 truck, ask whether it is the Ram 1500 Classic or the new body. They have different recalls: the Classic has 26V-756 and the new body has the 2025 and 2026 cluster recalls.
  • The 5.7-liter eTorque engine on a 2021 or 2022.Confirm 23V-265 was done on a 2021, and ask a 2022’s seller about stalls, battery warning lights and low-voltage faults. PE24018 is still open.
  • The power steering on a 2016 or 2019.Ask for proof of 16V-167 or 19V-812, and have a shop check the steering on a 2016, since RQ23003 is open on trucks that may fall outside the recall.
  • The air bag and seat belt recalls.On a 2019 or 2020 confirm 19V-407, on a 2019 to 2026 truck 26V-495 (rear seat belt anchors), and on a 2019 or 2021 to 2024 truck 24V-653 (stability control).
  • A 3.0-liter EcoDiesel.Confirm 19V-757 (EGR cooler) and 22V-406 (high-pressure fuel pump) were done, with receipts.
  • An independent inspection.Have a shop that isn’t the seller’s scan for engine and battery codes, and check the air suspension if the truck has it.

Is the 2026 better?

The 2026 brings the Hemi back after the 2025’s Hurricane six, and NHTSA already lists 110 complaints and 9 recalls for it, from headlight wiring to a side air bag connector. We wouldn’t call it more reliable than the 2023 and 2024 until its recall list stops growing.

Common questions

What are the worst Ram 1500 years?

The 2016 draws 322 NHTSA complaints per 100,000 sold, the highest, and the 2019 has 30 recalls, the most of any year. These rates divide by all Ram pickups, heavy duty included, so they understate the 1500’s own rate in every year before 2024.

What is the best used Ram 1500 year?

The 2023 and 2024 draw 47 and 42, and the 2020 and 2021 draw 115 and 122. The 2024 count is only 79 complaints, and the step from 2023 to 2024 is partly a change in the sales figure behind the rate.

Do these Ram 1500 figures include the Classic?

Yes. NHTSA files the Ram 1500 Classic’s complaints and recalls under the same name, so the 2019 to 2024 counts include it, and the sales totals include it too.

Is the 2025 Ram 1500 reliable?

It draws 187 complaints per 100,000, with 203 of 382 naming the electrical system, and recalls for the instrument cluster, headlights and hub encoders. We rate it Check first.

How do I check a used Ram 1500 for recalls?

Enter the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It lists open recalls for that truck; recall repairs are free at a Ram dealer.
