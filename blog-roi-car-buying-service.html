The ROI of a Car Buying Service

By Mason • Financial Strategy

Hiring help with a car purchase once seemed reserved for billionaires managing hedge funds or buying art. Car buying has become more complicated, with more opportunities for dealers to add profit to a deal, so the cost of expert help deserves a closer look.

When clients first contact Drive Right, the most frequent hurdle is a completely logical one: "Why would I pay a $495 fee to buy a car when I can just go to the dealer for free?"

A dealership visit may appear free, but the price, add-ons, financing, and time involved still have a cost. The comparison below shows where a car-buying service fee may fit.

Costs that can be hidden in a deal

MSRP is a starting point, not the full transaction cost. When you buy without comparing written offers, review these common sources of additional cost:

  • Market adjustments: Additional charges on highly desirable models.
  • Dealer add-ons: Nitrogen-filled tires ($299), "Protection Packages" ($1,495), and VIN etching ($399) installed before you see the car.
  • Financing markup: The dealer secures a loan at 5%, marks it up to 7%, and keeps the spread over 60 months.

When you account for these elements, the average unrepresented buyer may leave between $2,500 and $5,000 on the table. A dealership transaction has no service fee, but that does not make the overall purchase cost zero.

What the service fee covers

Our flat fee pays for research, negotiation, and review. The examples below show how a $495 fee can be compared with a potential $4,000 net benefit:

Parts of the comparison:
  • Price comparison: We negotiate using invoice pricing, factory holdbacks, and volume targets rather than only the sticker price. (Average savings: $2,100)
  • Add-on review: We identify unauthorized dealer add-ons and prep fees. (Average savings: $1,300)
  • Rate comparison: We ask dealers to match or beat outside lender rates and review the finance terms. (Average savings: $800 over loan term)

The time involved

A car search also takes time. Clients may spend 10 or more hours reviewing inventory, calling dealers, and sitting in a finance office. A service can take on some of that research and communication.

"The $495 fee paid for itself three times over in the vehicle discount alone, and a fourth time in the fact that I spent my weekend with my family instead of a car salesman."
