No, not for the fuel. The PowerBoost adds up to $9,635 to an XLT (Cars.com’s figure, which includes 4WD) and is rated 23 mpg combined against 20 for the 4WD 2.7L EcoBoost and 19 for the 3.5L. At the national average of $3.805 a gallon that is about $372 less gas a year against the 2.7L at 15,000 miles, so earning back the full premium takes up to 26 years, and up to 12 years against the 3.5L.

What the money buys is a different truck. Ford rates the PowerBoost at 420 hp and 570 lb-ft, more torque than any F-150 engine short of the Raptor R’s 640 lb-ft, and Pro Power Onboard (outlets in the bed and cab that run tools and appliances) can deliver 7.2 kW with it, the most Ford offers. Against that, it tows 11,600 pounds against 13,500 for the 3.5L EcoBoost, carries less (1,740 pounds of payload against 2,440), and loses value faster by iSeeCars’ figures.

Buy the hybrid if…

  • You will use the generator. Ford calls the PowerBoost’s 7.2 kW the highest Pro Power Onboard output available.
  • You want torque. 570 lb-ft is 70 more than the 3.5L EcoBoost and 170 more than the 2.7L.
  • You tow more than the 2.7L allows (8,400 pounds) but never near the 3.5L’s 13,500. The hybrid is rated 11,600.
  • You buy a King Ranch or Platinum anyway. The hybrid premium there is $5,140, the smallest on the trucks, and the payback is about 10 years.

Buy the gas version if…

  • Fuel savings are the reason you are looking. Even at half of Cars.com’s premium, the XLT hybrid takes about 13 years to earn it back at 15,000 miles a year.
  • You tow near the limit or haul heavy loads. The 3.5L EcoBoost is rated 13,500 pounds and 2,440 pounds of payload.
  • You will sell in three years. iSeeCars has a three-year-old hybrid down 27.4% against 18.9% for the gas truck.
  • You want the cheapest truck. A gas XLT starts at $44,695, and the 2.7L EcoBoost is standard.

What the hybrid costs on like-for-like trims

Ford sells the PowerBoost as an option on the STX, XLT, Lariat, King Ranch and Platinum. It is not offered on the XL, Tremor or Raptor. Cars.com lists the added cost over each trim’s base price as $9,145 on an STX, $9,635 on an XLT, $7,810 on a Lariat and $5,140 on a King Ranch or Platinum, and says the hybrid package includes 4WD.

That makes the gap hard to pin to the dollar. A gas XLT 4x4 needs 4WD added as its own charge, which we couldn’t confirm from Ford, so the real premium on a 4x4 is lower than these figures. We use Cars.com’s numbers as a ceiling and show how the payback shrinks if the real gap is smaller.

EPA fuel economy, gas against hybrid

With 4WD, EPA rates the PowerBoost at 23 mpg combined (22 city, 24 highway), the 2.7L EcoBoost at 20 (18 city, 23 highway) and the 3.5L at 19 (17 city, 23 highway). The gain is in town: 22 mpg against 18 and 17. On the highway the hybrid is one mpg ahead, so a truck that lives on the interstate gets little from the hybrid system.

Ford’s offers are listed by trim, and none names the engine. Offers are from the F-150 price guide, read on Ford’s site on November 15, 2026.

Resale: does the hybrid hold its value?

iSeeCars figures the standard F-150 loses 18.9% of its value in three years and 38.6% in five, and the hybrid 27.4% and 44.0%. The hybrid’s faster depreciation comes on top of its premium, so resale doesn’t shorten the payback. The F-150 price guide makes the same point.

What owners on Reddit report about real-world mpg

Owners say the PowerBoost beats the V8s on mileage, though speed and towing pull every F-150 down. We counted 160 opinions about the F-150’s fuel economy in 397 Reddit threads about the car, posted from January 2025 to November 2026: 110 positive, 35 negative and 15 mixed. That is a net score of +47 on the same −100 to +100 scale as the F-150 price guide, and it covers every version of the car, not only the hybrid.

Common questions

How much does the F-150 PowerBoost cost?

Cars.com lists the PowerBoost as an extra $9,145 on an STX, $9,635 on an XLT, $7,810 on a Lariat and $5,140 on a King Ranch or Platinum, with 4WD included. Ford doesn’t offer it on the XL, Tremor or Raptor. Confirm the current charge in Ford’s configurator.

How much better is the PowerBoost’s mpg?

With 4WD, EPA rates it 23 mpg combined against 20 for the 2.7L EcoBoost and 19 for the 3.5L and the 5.0L V8. That is about 100 to 140 fewer gallons a year at 15,000 miles.

Does the F-150 hybrid tow less than the gas truck?

Than the 3.5L EcoBoost, yes: 11,600 pounds against 13,500. Than the 2.7L, no: that engine is rated 8,400 pounds. Ratings are maximums for a well-equipped truck.

What is Pro Power Onboard?

Ford’s built-in generator: outlets in the bed and cab that run tools and appliances. Ford says the PowerBoost can deliver 7.2 kW, the highest output available.
