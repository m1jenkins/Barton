The best used Tundras are the older trucks, the 2016 to 2021 model years, which draw 29 NHTSA owner complaints per 100,000 sold for the 2021 up to 105 for the 2016. Skip the 2022, 2023 and 2024: they draw 418, 339 and 229, with the engine named in 207, 224 and 186 of their complaints, and Toyota has recalled them for engine bearing debris (24V-381, 25V-767, 26V-320) and a camshaft that can crack (26V-750). That last recall, filed November 12, 2026, also reaches 2025 and 2026 trucks, so we rate the 2025 Check first even though its rate is 99.

The rates divide each model year’s NHTSA complaints, counted across the Tundra and Tundra Hybrid listings, by U.S. sales. They compare years with each other; they don’t predict one truck. The Tundra was redesigned for 2022, so the older trucks and the newer ones carry different engines. The 2026 is too new to rate; our Tundra price guide covers what it costs new.

Tundra generations: what changed

Second generation (XK50) 2007–2021

Toyota built it from late 2006 to October 2021. From 2016 to 2021 the 5.7-liter V8 (381 hp) was the main engine, with a six-speed automatic; the 4.6-liter V8 left the lineup for 2020, and Toyota Safety Sense became standard for 2018 (Wikipedia). The 2016 to 2021 rows on this page are this truck.

Third generation (XK70) 2022–

Production began in December 2021 in San Antonio. Toyota dropped the V8 for a 3.4-liter twin-turbo V6 (the V35A, 348 to 389 hp), added an i-FORCE MAX hybrid rated at 437 hp, and fitted a 10-speed automatic (Wikipedia). Every engine recall on this page is on this truck.

Common Tundra problems by component

NHTSA files each complaint under the parts the owner names, and one complaint can name more than one. Across all 1,823 Tundra complaints for the 2016 to 2026 model years, these parts come up most:

  • Engine 2022–2025 model years666 complaints naming engine, 124 per 100,000 sold. Recalls 24V-381, 25V-767 and 26V-320 cover manufacturing debris in the 3.4-liter V6 that can fail the main bearings and stall the engine; dealers replace the engine or, under the later two, inspect the number-one main bearing and replace the engine if needed, free. Recall 26V-750 covers a camshaft that may crack and snap on 2022–2023 and 2025–2026 trucks, and dealers inspect and replace it as necessary, free. Engine is the top complaint part on every year from 2022 to 2025.
  • Transmission and drivetrain 2022–2024 model years278 complaints naming transmission and drivetrain, 71 per 100,000 sold. Power train complaints run 104, 88 and 86 on the 2022 to 2024 trucks. Recall 22V-445 covers rear axle shaft flange nuts that can loosen on 2022 trucks, and 24V-125 (2022–2024) covers unexpected movement in neutral, fixed with a software update. NHTSA’s label spans the transmission, axles and driveline, so the count doesn’t say which part.
  • Fuel and hybrid system 2022–2024 model years230 complaints naming fuel and hybrid system, 59 per 100,000 sold. Recall 23V-566 (2022–2023) covers a plastic fuel tube that can chafe against brake lines and leak; dealers add protection and a clamp, free. NHTSA files hybrid-system complaints under the same label, so this count is wider than the fuel tube alone.
  • Electrical system 2016–2018 model years79 complaints naming electrical system, 23 per 100,000 sold. Electrical leads the 2017 and 2018 counts, but on 79 and 100 complaints in total they are small, and NHTSA’s label names a part, not a failure.

What owners report on Reddit

Reliability is the topic Reddit raises most about the Tundra: 793 mentions in 373 threads, 340 positive and 334 negative, a net of +3 on a scale from −100 to +100. Our Reddit Sentiment Index for the Tundra is 46 out of 100 (50 is even). The threads run from January 2025 to November 2026 and come from r/Tundra and r/ToyotaTundra and the rest of Reddit, so they say little about a car that owners stopped discussing before 2025. The Toyota Tundra price guide has the full topic breakdown.

Reddit’s Tundra owners split by generation. The older 5.7 V8 trucks are widely reported to last 250,000 miles or more, which fits the low 2016 to 2021 rates, with timing chain tensioner wear on some years the main gripe. The third-generation threads center on the twin-turbo V6’s engine failures and the recalls, while some owners report trouble-free miles and call failures a small share. The threads rarely give a model year, so they agree with the table without confirming any one year.

What owners praise about reliability

  • Older 5.7 V8 trucks are widely reported to last 250,000 miles or more
  • First-generation 4.7 V8 trucks reach high mileage if the timing belt is changed
  • Some third-generation owners report trouble-free miles and say failures are a small share

What owners report going wrong

  • Owners report third-generation twin-turbo V6 engine failures and a large engine recall
  • Rust on first-generation frames is a serious concern for used buyers
  • Owners report 5.7 V8 timing chain tensioner wear on some years

Other complaints that come up

  • Fuel economy. Owners report real-world mileage in the low to mid teens
  • Noise and refinement. The 5.7 V8 can rattle on cold start from timing chain tensioners
  • Interior and tech. Interior plastics and design feel dated next to American full-size trucks

Recalls worth knowing about

  • Engine main bearings 2022–2023Recall 24V-381. Debris from manufacturing can contaminate the V35A engine and fail the main bearings, which can stall the engine and cut drive power. Dealers replace the engine assembly, free.
  • Engine main bearings, wider scope 2022–2024Recall 25V-767. The same debris problem, extended to 2022 to 2024 Tundras. Dealers inspect the number-one main bearing with vehicle software and drive data and replace the engine if necessary, free.
  • Engine main bearing 2024Recall 26V-320. A third V35A debris campaign, for certain 2024 trucks, with the same inspection and engine replacement as needed, free.
  • Camshaft cracks 2022–2023 and 2025–2026Recall 26V-750. A camshaft may have been made incorrectly, which lets cracks develop and can snap it, stalling the engine and cutting drive power. Filed November 12, 2026 and covering 62,490 trucks; dealers inspect and replace the camshaft as necessary, free. Owner letters begin December 28, 2026.
  • Rear axle shaft nuts 2022Recall 22V-445. Flange nuts on the axle shaft can loosen over time and let an axle shaft separate from the axle housing. Dealers inspect and tighten them and replace damaged parts, free.
  • Movement in neutral 2022–2024Recall 24V-125. The vehicle can move unexpectedly when it is in neutral with the brakes off. Dealers update the transmission control software, free.
  • Fuel tube chafing 2022–2023Recall 23V-566. A plastic fuel tube near metal brake lines can chafe and leak fuel. Dealers install protective material and a clamp, free.
  • Power steering gear oil leak 2016–2021Recall 21V-920 and 21E-103. A power steering gear assembly may have been built incorrectly and leak oil. Dealers inspect and replace the assembly as needed, free.

Open and recent NHTSA investigations

NHTSA’s public investigation list showed no investigation naming the Tundra from 2016 to November 21, 2026. The engine problems have gone through Toyota’s own recalls: three for the V35A’s main bearings and one for the camshaft.

What to check before you buy a used Tundra

  • Open recalls by VIN.Enter the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. A recall fix is free at a Toyota dealer, and the lookup shows which ones are still open on that exact car.
  • The engine recalls on a 2022 to 2026.Ask for the dealer’s service record showing 24V-381, 25V-767 and 26V-320 closed, whichever apply to the truck’s build, and for any engine replacement paperwork. A VIN lookup lists what is open.
  • The camshaft recall on a 2022, 2023, 2025 or 2026.Recall 26V-750 was filed November 12, 2026 and its letters start December 28, 2026, so a truck may not have had the inspection yet. Ask the seller to schedule it, or price the risk in.
  • The power steering gear on a 2016 to 2021.Confirm 21V-920 or 21E-103 was done: the fix replaces a gear assembly that can leak oil.
  • The fuel tube on a 2022 or 2023.Recall 23V-566 adds a clamp and protection to a tube that can chafe; the VIN lookup shows whether it is open.
  • The frame on an older truck.Owners on Reddit raise frame rust as a worry for used Tundra buyers, mostly on first-generation trucks. Have a shop look at the frame of any older truck from a salt-belt state.
  • An independent inspection.Have a shop that isn’t the seller’s check the engine for codes, leaks and recall history before you sign.

Is the 2026 better?

NHTSA had no 2026 Tundra complaints on its list yet, and the truck already has 3 recalls, including the camshaft recall 26V-750. The newer trucks share the 2022 to 2024 engine design, so we wouldn’t call the 2026 more reliable until the engine recalls stop reaching new model years.

Common questions

What are the worst Toyota Tundra years?

The 2022, 2023 and 2024 model years. They draw 418, 339 and 229 NHTSA complaints per 100,000 sold against 29 for the 2021, and they carry the engine bearing recalls 24V-381, 25V-767 and 26V-320.

What is the best used Tundra year?

The 2016 to 2021 trucks have the lowest rates, from 29 for the 2021 to 105 for the 2016. They are the older truck with the 5.7-liter V8, so the newer engine’s recalls don’t apply to them.

Does the camshaft recall 26V-750 cover my Tundra?

It lists 2022–2023 and 2025–2026 trucks. The 2024 isn’t on that list but has its own bearing recalls, so check the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for what is open on your truck.

Is a 2025 Tundra a good buy?

It draws 99 complaints per 100,000, which is low, but the camshaft recall reaches it and the owner letters start December 28, 2026. We rate it Check first until the inspection is done on the truck you are buying.

How do I check a used Tundra for recalls?

Enter the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It lists open recalls for that truck; recall repairs are free at a Toyota dealer.
