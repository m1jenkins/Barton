# Drive Right: national competitor SEO findings and implementation plan

Prepared October 1, 2026. Research and planning only. Source baseline: Barton `1acc809`; live homepage and Resources inspected today. Companion: [detailed competitor pages, copy and article inventory](2026-10-01-national-competitor-research.md). All proposed copy below is original draft copy for review, not a publication decision.

## Recommended direction

Make Drive Right the useful, approachable expert for buyers who want someone to research vehicles, compare written offers and negotiate for them. Build that position through the existing national service pages, a small library of practical guides, the already-built quote worksheet and documented customer work.

The first investment should be **finishing and releasing the three prepared guides and quote worksheet, making the Resources page useful to buyers, and showing what the service actually produces**. Follow with service-value education, used-car due diligence and a consented case study. Expand geography after those assets work and the existing local release gates are met.

The inspected national companies use several acquisition models. CarEdge connects educational content with tools and its own dealer-quote data; Car Pal covers service questions and buying decisions; Authority Auto separates service options and uses media proof; Delivrd emphasizes founder/video credibility and buyer fit; Automatch relies partly on offsite expert publishing. None of that establishes their Google rankings or proves a tactic caused growth. The recommendations here are business-fit judgments grounded in observed pages, current Drive Right assets and documented search queries.

### 2. Service explainer — make the service understandable and tangible

**File:** `car-buying-service.html`. **Priority:** P1; about 1–2 days including sample preparation.

Section order: direct answer → who it suits → work performed → annotated written recommendation → buyer responsibilities → choose a plan → remote coverage/limits → questions → CTA. Answer the difference between a listing site, dealer referral and an advisor who helps with the search/negotiation; present different compensation models without a universal claim about brokers.

Suggested opening draft: **“Drive Right helps you research vehicles, compare available offers and negotiate with participating dealers. One advisor handles the search and the back-and-forth; you decide what to buy.”** Follow with current plan prices and separate costs already listed in approved service records. Do not promise a fixed number of responses, inspection certification or a guaranteed outcome.

Sample fields: buyer requirements, vehicle/trim/condition, offer date, vehicle price, quoted charges, conditional incentives, unanswered questions and advisor reasoning. Make the sample's limits clear. Acceptance: reader can understand what they receive, what they still do and how a plan differs without reading every page; visible copy/metadata/schema agree.

### 3. Pricing and process — improve choice, reuse existing disclosure work

**Files:** `schedule.html`, `how-it-works.html`, relevant approved policy/service records. **Priority:** P1; about 1–2 days.

Concierge additions: auctions/forums/niche sources, priority coordination and delivery coordination **where available**. Put excluded vehicle/transaction costs and the policy link directly beneath it.

Explain suitable buyer situations with the existing “most buyers” and “hard-to-find” guidance. Keep brief → choose plan → pay → detailed intake accurate; do not introduce a free consultation or standalone AI plan. New timing statements need actual operational support.

Verify and integrate the current owner-approved compensation disclosure work in [PR90](https://github.com/m1jenkins/Barton/pull/90) before writing comparative independence language. Acceptance: fee, scope, payment order, compensation and refund/change conditions agree across visible text, schema and actual approved records. Read checkout amounts without making a live payment; test purchases belong in the established test-mode release workflow.

### 4. Resources — turn a status directory into a useful reading path

**File:** `blog.html`. **Priority:** P1 alongside the first guide release; about 1–1.5 days.

Keep `/blog.html`. Proposed H1: **“Car buying guides for the decisions that matter.”** Proposed introduction: **“Compare written offers, understand dealer add-ons and know what to check before you choose a used car.”** Only list these as published resources after their individual review/release.

### 5. About and proof — substantiate expertise with actual work

**File:** `about.html`; proposed case-study URL only after proof is ready. **Priority:** P1/P2; about 1–2 editorial days per case plus document/review time.

Use Mason's genuine experience, existing photography and a clear explanation of how guides are prepared and reviewed. Link guide bylines to a stable author section on About; name an actual qualified reviewer only after that person has accepted the exact article. Do not invent credentials, years, vehicles bought or client counts.

First case-study template: buyer goal → dated vehicle/market context → comparable original and final offer → advisor actions → outcome after charges/service fee → remaining limitations. Support it with redacted quotes and customer permission. Separate price reduction, removed products, changed financing and service fee; a changed configuration or conditional incentive is not automatically negotiated savings. A hypothetical example can teach the method while genuine proof is being collected, but cannot be presented as a client story.

Reuse the approved Michael R quote now; a richer case still needs its own documentary support and consent. No self-serving AggregateRating or fabricated review schema. Acceptance: every result can be traced to an allowed record, and the published story stays within the evidence.

### Standard for each guide

Begin with the buyer's question and a direct answer. Follow with a short list of decisions, a worked example or reusable template, specific steps, important limits, source links beside consequential facts, and an appropriate next action. End with a few genuinely relevant reading links. Do not target arbitrary word counts; length follows the task. Google emphasizes original usefulness and visible authorship rather than a preferred length. [Helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).

Use a real byline linked to About, truthful original/revised/reviewed dates and the named reviewer's accepted role. Recheck September draft sources; link FTC/CFPB, regulators, manufacturer program terms and NHTSA/NMVTIS where relevant, rather than citing competitors as authorities for consumer facts. Create an original example for every first-wave guide. Optional founder videos should answer the exact topic, have captions and an adjacent transcript, and load without blocking the useful text.

Implementation starts from existing private guide content, not obsolete public archive bodies. On promotion, match the current design (`buying/daisy.css`, `buying/drive-right.css`, `.dr`) and retain the article URL. Keep a reproducible private source/generation path. Restyling or editing must refresh the exact artifact hash and source/date records before final acceptance; old private hashes do not approve changed publication bytes. Extend existing governance records rather than adding a parallel approval system.

## Measurement and maintenance

Primary business measures: qualified organic inquiries, verified paid engagements and revenue once attribution/reconciliation supports them. Supporting measures: nonbrand impressions/clicks for approved intent clusters, article-to-service journeys, worksheet use and approved-page index state. Pageviews, all-form events and success-page loads are not proof of a paid customer. Use the established governed event model and server-confirmed purchase ledger; do not transmit customer quotes or checkout session IDs into analytics.

At start, store the current 28-day window plus the previous non-overlapping window by page/query/device. At days 30/60/90, compare equal windows and release cohorts, while recording price changes, paid campaigns and release dates. Do not claim causality from small samples. Decision rules:

- A new page not discovered after several weeks: check actual crawl access, internal links, sitemap/canonical and URL Inspection before changing its topic or requesting repeated indexing.
- Relevant impressions but weak engagement: inspect query fit, result snippets and page usefulness; do not assume the title alone caused low CTR.
- Visits without qualified interest: inspect offer fit and reader next steps; traffic growth alone is not a success.
- Topic repeatedly earns suitable inquiries: deepen its examples and related checks before expanding dozens of new URLs.
- No distinctive evidence for a city or deal series: defer it rather than fill a publishing calendar.

Review current prices, scope and competitor comparisons quarterly and after a change. Refresh dated finance/tax/safety facts when sources or programs change. Any future incentive series needs eligibility, region, source, expiration and an accountable refresh owner. Improve Google Business Profile/Bing/Apple facts only after checking existing account state; use real service-area information and a public profile URL when verified. No fake local offices.

Large sets of nearly identical city pages are a poor fit for both this business and Google's doorway/scaled-content policies. Genuine local utility is the criterion, not the competitor's page count. [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies).
