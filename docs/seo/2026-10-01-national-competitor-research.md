## How to read the evidence

- Commercial page tables show actual observed URLs, visible headings where established, the message/section structure, trust evidence and CTA.
- Article tables link exact titles to their individual source pages and record displayed dates/access limits. Dates in a headline do not establish that source facts were recently reviewed.
- Authority's hosted media articles and Automatch's offsite advice are distinguished from onsite blogs. CarBargains is historical; Costco's current blog inventory is unverified.
- Observed page features are evidence. Their SEO purpose and proposed benefit for Drive Right are explicitly interpretations.
- No signup, competitor purchase, lead inquiry or outreach was performed.

### Four article structures inspected in detail

- **Article 08, category guide:** Named author, short read-time, summary bullets, definition, service components, four-stage process, suitability, benefits and FAQs. Contextual product links appear while discussing market data and service outsourcing; later service promotion follows education. Generic template also includes insurance sponsorship disclosure, download CTA, related guides and newsletter. Implementation lesson: answer category confusion on the service page or one guide; avoid duplicate pages with the same purpose.
- **Article 17, email templates:** Expert byline, video embeds, distinction between new/used negotiation, who to contact, message anatomy, likely replies, lowering price and a copyable email. Contextual links explain OTD pricing and used-car markup; a free download captures intent. Implementation lesson: an original email template plus an annotated sample quote is more useful than generic car-buying tips.
- **Article 03, monthly APR analysis:** Up-front date/snapshot and takeaway bullets, changes since prior edition, offer tables, alternatives to cash, explicit financing assumptions, inventory context, FAQs and methodology. Links to previous editions, manufacturer/model pages, depreciation and the paid negotiation service. Expiry is stated repeatedly. Implementation lesson: only publish recurring deals if a named owner can refresh source/eligibility/expiry; start with evergreen rebate-versus-rate math instead.
- **Article 16, dealer-fee guide:** Definition and fee categories are supported by redacted real worksheets, itemized examples, comparison tables and links to dealer details. References flow to the calculator, state fee guide, coaching and free worksheets. Implementation lesson: one consented Drive Right deal breakdown can support an explainer, service proof and sales follow-up. Avoid adopting competitor fee/legal statements as facts.

Additional observation on article 01: it discloses weighting and quote counts, links each dealer to its profile, uses a comparison table and closes with shopping actions. Some listed dealer names span unrelated franchises despite the Ford-focused heading. Treat it as evidence of a content structure; manually validate any similar generated content rather than copying the template at scale.

### Four inspected article patterns

Short social video supports the topic. Hire and consultation links sit at top; related articles and contact links follow. Lesson: concrete buyer decisions work better than broad tips, but derivative model/reliability claims must be independently sourced and dated.

- **End-of-month article:** A single misconception is answered with practitioner experience, a short explanation and takeaway. Lesson: a focused question can support a short helpful guide, but general experience alone is weaker than an example or source.

The body retains an unfinished video-link placeholder; the same minivan embed caption appears. Lesson: use the accessible question structure; source credit/finance facts to authoritative guidance and finish every link/embed before publishing. No new finance advice is adopted here.

## Scope corrections

- **Authority Auto:** explicitly says it represents clients nationwide on its [homepage](https://www.authorityauto.com/), while its [FAQ](https://www.authorityauto.com/faqs) distinguishes Southern California pickup/delivery capability. National representation does not mean identical physical service everywhere.
- **Car Pal:** [FAQ](https://car-pal.com/faqs/) explicitly says it is based in Chapel Hill, North Carolina, serves locally and nationwide, and has clients including Alaska. New, used, CPO, leasing, trade-ins and out-of-state coordination appear in its service descriptions.
- **Automatch Consulting:** [homepage](https://automatchconsulting.com/) offers new, pre-owned, leasing and specialty vehicles; [reviews](https://automatchconsulting.com/reviews/) show clients in NJ, Chicago, Las Vegas, Phoenix, Boston, Minnesota and Tucson, including a nationwide search. The inspected pages support geographically distributed service, but did not expose an explicit all-state coverage policy. Treat national scope as qualified, not an all-state guarantee.
- **CarBargains/LeaseWise:** no longer active services. Consumers' Checkbook [current overview](https://www.checkbook.org/boston-area/car-buying-strategies-for-a-good-deal) states both ceased operations on **September 15, 2023** after 32 years. Treat Checkbook as a historical service and ongoing editorial reference, not a current direct competitor. Its broader publication has regional editions; the inspected Boston URLs are published edition paths, not proof that historical CarBargains was Boston-only.
