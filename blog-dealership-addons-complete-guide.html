Dealership Add-Ons: What to Ask Before You Agree

Original article: MasonDraft updated September 5, 2026AI-assisted revision; author acceptance and qualified consumer-finance, insurance, and warranty review pending

Start here: Ask for a vehicle quote with each add-on listed separately. Decide on each product using its written terms, your existing coverage, and the full cost. A product name or a small monthly payment increase is not enough information to choose it.

Itemize the quote · Review each product · Compare costs · Before signing

Separate products from other charges

Make three lists: the vehicle and installed equipment; optional products you are considering; and the taxes and fees shown in the quote. Ask who receives each charge and what it pays for. Do not assume a documentation fee, a government charge, and an optional protection product are interchangeable.

The CFPB explains that extended warranties, GAP, and credit insurance are generally optional. If a seller says a product is required for your loan, ask the lender to identify the requirement in the contract before agreeing.

“Please send an itemized quote without optional products, plus a separate price and sample contract for each product you recommend. If a charge cannot be removed, please explain why in writing.”

This is a sample request, not a claim that a seller must accept your proposed price. If a dealer insists on selling an installed package, compare the complete offer with another vehicle or seller.

Questions for the common product categories

GAP protection

GAP is optional protection that may cover some or all of the difference between a vehicle’s cash value and the remaining loan or lease balance after a covered theft or total loss. It does not promise payment of every dollar owed. See the CFPB’s explanation of vehicle insurance options.

  • What is the maximum benefit, and what balances or charges are excluded?
  • How are a deductible and any prior vehicle’s unpaid balance treated?
  • Who provides the protection and handles the claim?
  • Can my lender or insurer quote an alternative? Compare the actual terms; do not assume products are identical.

Vehicle service contracts

A separately purchased service contract is often marketed as an extended warranty. Coverage can overlap with an existing vehicle warranty. The FTC distinguishes these products and advises reviewing the contract’s limits before buying.

  • When does coverage start and end, by both date and mileage?
  • Which failures, parts, labor charges, and diagnostic work are excluded?
  • What deductible applies, and is it charged per visit or repair?
  • Which shops can perform work, and who must authorize it?
  • Who administers claims? What are the cancellation and transfer terms?

A brand name or a “bumper-to-bumper” description cannot answer those questions. Keep the offered contract beside your current warranty and mark overlapping dates and components.

Paint, fabric, wheel, tire, and appearance packages

Ask for a written description of the actual product, covered surfaces or parts, installer, exclusions, and service procedure. Separate a physical installation from a promise to repair future damage. A coating, film, and damage-protection contract are different offers to evaluate.

For VIN etching, nitrogen, pinstriping, or a bundled appearance package, ask whether the work is already installed, what the quoted amount covers, and whether the seller will offer the vehicle without that charge. Request a comparable independent quote if you want the work. This guide makes no claim about typical markups or universal product value.

Prepaid maintenance

List the exact services and intervals included, then compare them with your intended ownership period and the vehicle’s maintenance schedule. Ask where the plan can be used, whether unused visits expire, and what happens if you move or sell the car.

Compare the dollar cost, not just the payment

When an add-on is financed, its cost can include interest and payments can continue after its benefits expire. The CFPB describes this financing mismatch. Ask for loan disclosures both with and without the products, using the same loan term and down payment.

Hypothetical arithmetic: A $900 product spread over 60 payments accounts for $15 per payment before any interest: $900 ÷ 60 = $15. That figure is not a financing quote. Compare the lender’s actual total payments, fees, and APR before deciding.

Build one comparison row per product: provider / cash price / financed total / coverage period / exclusions / deductible / cancellation terms / accept or decline. Leave a field blank until the provider supplies it; do not fill gaps with a salesperson’s verbal summary.

Before you sign or seek a cancellation

  1. Match each selected product to its contract and price.
  2. Check that declined products are absent from the final paperwork.
  3. Save the completed agreements and the provider’s contact route.
  4. If you later want to cancel, request the applicable process, calculation, and destination of any refund in writing. Do not assume a refund amount or automatic reduction in monthly payments.
