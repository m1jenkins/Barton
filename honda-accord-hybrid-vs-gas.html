The EX-L Hybrid, the trim we’d buy, costs $4,400 more than a gas SE and is rated 48 mpg combined against 31, which is 171 fewer gallons a year at 15,000 miles. At the national average of $3.805 a gallon that is about $652 a year, and the premium is back in 6.7 years (8.4 years at 12,000 miles). The cheaper Sport Hybrid is $3,100 over the SE at 44 mpg and gets there in 5.7.

That is a longer wait than on the Civic or CR-V hybrids, because the Accord’s premium is bigger and its gas car already gets 31 mpg. What the hybrid adds is power, 204 hp against 192, and Edmunds’ 7.0 seconds to 60 mph in a Sport-L Hybrid against 8.5 for a gas SE in U.S. News’ test (two publications, so a rough comparison). The catch is resale: iSeeCars has an Accord Hybrid losing 22.1% in three years against 15.5% for the Accord overall.

Buy the hybrid if…

  • You keep a car six or seven years, or drive about 15,000 miles a year. The EX-L Hybrid’s $4,400 premium is back in 6.7 years at that pace.
  • You want the quicker Accord: 204 hp against 192, and Edmunds timed a Sport-L Hybrid at 7.0 seconds to 60 mph.
  • Your driving is mostly around town. EPA rates the EX-L Hybrid at 51 mpg in the city against 28 for the gas SE.
  • You want leather, a power passenger seat, driver’s seat memory and parking sensors with the hybrid, which is what the EX-L Hybrid adds.

Buy the gas version if…

  • You want the cheapest Accord. A gas LX starts at $28,395, $6,700 under the EX-L Hybrid, though it has no heated seats, no moonroof and no blind-spot warning.
  • You sell at three years. iSeeCars has an Accord Hybrid down 22.1% after three years against 15.5% for the Accord overall.
  • You drive fewer than about 10,000 miles a year. At that pace the EX-L Hybrid’s premium takes about 10 years to earn back.
  • You can’t find an EX-L Hybrid. Edmunds says its stock is tighter than the other trims’, and a gas SE is easy to find.

What the hybrid costs on like-for-like trims

Honda sells the gas Accord as an LX and an SE, and every hybrid starts a step higher, at the Sport Hybrid, so there is no same-trim pair. The SE (heated front seats, moonroof, blind-spot warning, dual-zone climate control, power driver’s seat, 19-inch wheels) is the nearest gas car. The EX-L Hybrid adds leather, a power passenger seat, driver’s seat memory and parking sensors that the SE doesn’t have, so its gap overstates the cost of the hybrid system alone.

EPA fuel economy, gas against hybrid

EPA rates the EX-L Hybrid at 48 mpg combined (51 city, 44 highway) and the gas SE at 31 (28 city, 36 highway). The Sport Hybrid and the other 19-inch hybrids are rated 44 (46 city, 41 highway), so the EX-L’s 17-inch wheels are worth 4 mpg. The hybrid leads on the highway too, by 8 mpg for the EX-L and 5 for the Sport, so an interstate commute still pays. Honda doesn’t offer all-wheel drive on either version.

How many years until the hybrid pays for itself?

The EX-L Hybrid’s $4,400 premium is back in 6.4 to 8.4 years and the Sport Hybrid’s $3,100 in 5.4 to 7.1, depending on mileage and gas price. That is slower than the Civic’s or CR-V’s payback, which is why the verdict carries a condition: this is a car to keep.

Do this month’s incentives favor one version?

Honda treats the two versions almost alike this month. Its financing runs to January 4, 2027: 3.99% for 24 to 60 months on the gas Accord in all four regions we checked, and on the Hybrid 3.49% in Chicago and Dallas and 3.99% in Los Angeles and New York. On $35,000 over 60 months, 3.49% costs about $470 less interest than 3.99% (our arithmetic), which trims the hybrid’s gap a little where it applies. Honda lists no cash back on any Accord. The leases differ because the trims do: $229 a month for an LX against $299 for a Sport Hybrid, both for 36 months. The $750 owner offer and the $500 military and graduate offers cover every Accord and Accord Hybrid. Offers are from the Accord price guide, read at Honda’s offers pages on November 17, 2026.

Resale: does the hybrid hold its value?

iSeeCars counts three-year depreciation at 15.5% for the Accord overall and 22.1% for the Accord Hybrid, against 24.8% for the average vehicle. So the hybrid gives back 6.6 points more than the Accord average, which is the main case for the gas car. Both hold their value better than the average new vehicle, and the figures come from iSeeCars’ sample prices, not Honda’s.

What owners on Reddit report about real-world mpg

Hybrid owners report 46 to 50 mpg in mixed driving, near the 44-to-48 rating, though some say real mileage falls short of EPA’s figure. We counted 54 opinions about the Accord’s fuel economy in 143 Reddit threads about the car, posted from January 2025 to November 2026: 30 positive, 16 negative and 8 mixed. That is a net score of +19 on the same −100 to +100 scale as the Accord price guide, and it covers every version of the car, not only the hybrid.

Common questions

How much more does the Accord Hybrid cost than the gas Accord?

$4,400 for an EX-L Hybrid over a gas SE ($35,095 against $30,695) and $3,100 for a Sport Hybrid over the SE ($33,795). Honda sells no hybrid below the Sport, so the gas LX ($28,395) leaves a $5,400 gap to the cheapest hybrid with less equipment. Prices are 2026 starting MSRPs without destination, from the Accord price guide.

How much gas does the Accord Hybrid save?

About 171 gallons a year at 15,000 miles for the EX-L Hybrid (313 against 484), which is $652 at the November 16 national average of $3.805 and $685 at EPA’s $4.00. At 12,000 miles it is about 137 gallons.

Is the Accord Hybrid faster than the gas Accord?

Yes. It makes 204 hp against 192, and Edmunds timed a Sport-L Hybrid at 7.0 seconds to 60 mph where U.S. News took 8.5 seconds in a gas SE.

Does the Accord come with all-wheel drive?

No. Every 2026 Accord, gas or hybrid, is front-wheel drive. If you need AWD, the Toyota Camry offers it on its hybrid.
