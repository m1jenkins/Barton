The Ranger is the midsize pickup to price first if ride quality and tech matter to you. Edmunds ranks it first of seven midsize trucks and timed a 4x4 Lariat to 60 mph in 6.7 seconds, a second quicker than a comparable Tacoma. The catch is the sticker: Edmunds says it costs a bit more than the Colorado, and U.S. News ranks it seventh of seven, citing low-speed transmission jitters and cheap cabin materials. Ford is carrying plenty of stock (112 days of supply across the brand in October, against 87 for the industry) and adds $2,000 cash plus $500 Black Friday bonus cash through November 30. Price a 2026 XLT, aim for 3–4% under MSRP before Ford’s cash, and get your bank’s rate before you accept Ford’s 4.9%. Prices and offers were checked on November 18, 2026.

What to pay: how far under MSRP

Rangers sell under sticker, and Ford’s cash comes on top. Edmunds’ November tips put the average buyer about $1,362 under MSRP before Ford’s $2,000 cash, and a Ranger sells in a median 22 days against 34 for the average new vehicle. Ford is long on trucks, though: Cox counted 112 days of supply for the brand in October against 87 for the industry. CarWhere’s 14 verified buyers paid a median 6.2% under MSRP, in a sample too small to lean on. The Raptor is the exception, with about $800 off and no general cash.

The trim we’d buy

A 2026 XLT, 4x4 if you leave pavement, with the standard 2.3L four-cylinder. At $36,075 it costs $2,525 more than the XL and adds wireless phone charging and rear parking sensors, which Edmunds credits when it names the XLT its Editors’ Pick. The 4x4 adds $3,500. Ford’s lease deals are aimed at the XLT too.

If you tow, every trim with the Trailer Tow Package is rated at 7,500 pounds, so the V6 buys no extra capacity. Edmunds tested both engines and found the 2.7L V6 only a little quicker (6.2 seconds to 60 mph against 6.7 for the four-cylinder Lariat) and rated at 20 mpg combined against 21 with 4x4, so we’d skip it. The Raptor is a different truck: $57,270, 17 mpg combined and a 5,510-pound tow rating. Ford’s cash does not apply to it, and Edmunds says it sells about $800 under sticker.

What to watch for at the dealer

  • Engine recalls. NHTSA lists two recalls for loss of drive power on 2025 and 2026 Rangers. 26V343 (2025–2026) is fixed with a new engine long block; 26V548 (2026) is an inspection, and Ford’s letter says the remedy is expected in December 2026. Check any truck’s VIN before you sign.
  • Rearview camera recall. 26V605 covers 2026 Rangers with the 10.1-inch screen, which can lose power and drop the camera image. NHTSA lists the remedy as under development.
  • Dealer-installed extras. Ford’s own price list shows a hard roll-up tonneau cover at $1,470 and cargo management rails at $600. If a dealer’s quote includes extras you didn’t ask for, ask for them to come off.
  • The Raptor and the offers. The $500 bonus cash excludes the Raptor, and Edmunds says it carries no general cash. Don’t expect the Ranger deals to apply to it.
  • The model year. A 2026 and a 2027 can sit on the same lot. Ford’s current offers are for the 2026.
  • Financing terms. The 4.9% needs Ford Credit approval, and Ford’s pages do not say whether it combines with the cash. Compare the total cost with your bank’s rate.

Common questions

Is the Ford Ranger a good truck?

Most reviewers rate it well, with one outlier. Edmunds scores the 2026 a 7.1 out of 10 and ranks it first of seven midsize trucks, and Kelley Blue Book gives it a 4.7 out of 5. U.S. News scores it 8.4 and ranks it seventh of seven, and faults low-speed transmission jitters and cheap cabin materials. Consumer Reports expects about average reliability.

How much under MSRP can you get a Ford Ranger?

On an XL, XLT or Lariat, 3–4% under MSRP is a fair deal and 6–7% a strong one, before Ford’s $2,500 cash. The Raptor has less room, around 1–2%. Those ranges are our read of Edmunds’ tips, iSeeCars’ invoice figures and CarWhere’s 14 verified buyers, so a given dealer may land a point either side.

What is the destination charge on a Ranger?

$1,895 on the 2026. Ford’s advertised prices leave it out, so a $33,550 XL is $35,445 on the window sticker.

Does Ford offer cash back on the Ranger?

Yes, until November 30, 2026: $2,000 customer cash plus $500 Black Friday bonus cash on a 2026 Ranger, in Chicago, Dallas, Los Angeles and New York. The bonus cash is not available on the Raptor, and Edmunds says the Raptor has no general cash. Ford’s pages do not say whether the cash combines with its 4.9% financing, so ask the dealer.

Should I buy the 2.7L V6?

Probably not. Edmunds clocked a V6 Ranger at 6.2 seconds to 60 mph against 6.7 for a four-cylinder Lariat 4x4, found no towing gain, and the EPA rates the V6 at 20 mpg combined against 21 for the 2.3L 4x4. Both engines tow 7,500 pounds with the Trailer Tow Package.

Is the Ranger Raptor worth it?

Only if you will use it off pavement. It starts at $57,270 against $43,955 for a Lariat, is rated 17 mpg combined, and tows 5,510 pounds against 7,500. It makes 405 hp from a 3.0L twin-turbo V6, and gets none of Ford’s November cash.

Is a used Ranger a better deal than new?

Rarely. CarWhere’s median asking price for a used 2024 is $37,075 and for a 2025 $39,089, while a new XLT starts at $36,075 before Ford’s $2,500 cash. Used loans averaged 11.19% in the second quarter of 2026, against Ford’s 4.9% on a new one. A Gold Certified 2024 or 2025 with its recalls done is the used route we’d take.
