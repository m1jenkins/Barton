The San Francisco car market right now

The Census Bureau counted 229 new-car dealer locations in the San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area in 2023, and 91 more in the separate San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area. S&P Global Mobility ranked the San Francisco-Oakland-San Jose market tenth in the country for new-vehicle registrations in the first half of 2025. Its average monthly payment was $718, one of six above $700 among the ten markets with the most registrations, and its 74 days’ supply tied Chicago’s, behind only Boston. Miami and San Francisco were the only two of the ten where electric-vehicle registrations outnumbered hybrids.

Buying a car around San Francisco

Check which bridge you’ll cross

The Bay Area’s bridges charge a toll in one direction only. The Bay Bridge, the San Mateo-Hayward Bridge, the Dumbarton Bridge and the Richmond-San Rafael Bridge charge westbound, the Carquinez and Benicia-Martinez bridges charge northbound, the Antioch Bridge charges eastbound, and the Golden Gate Bridge charges southbound into San Francisco. A dealer across the water can put a toll on your trip to pick up the car or on your drive home, depending on which way you cross. Tell us which side of the bay you live on, and we weigh each dealer’s bridge against its price.

Tell us where you’ll charge

Electric vehicle registrations outnumber hybrids in this market, and most electric car owners charge overnight at home, on a standard outlet or a faster dedicated charger. If you own a house, an electrician can tell you whether it has the capacity for a charger. If you rent or live in a building with shared parking, the landlord or building manager decides whether you can charge where you park. Ask before you shop. Tell us whether you can charge at home, and we search electric, plug-in hybrid, hybrid or gas models to match.

Tell us how steep your street is

San Francisco law requires you to turn your wheels toward or against the curb when you park on a steep street, and a driver who doesn’t can be fined. The city’s Public Works office publishes a street grade map you can look your block up on. On a test drive, stop on a slope and check that the parking brake holds the car. Tell us your street and the longest and widest car you can park, and we check your model against those limits before we ask any dealer for a price.

How it works from San Francisco

  1. Tell us the car. Model, trims and colors you’d accept, budget, and how far you’d go to pick it up. Mason replies within one business day to confirm the search and when it starts.
  2. We find the cars. Your advisor searches new, used and CPO dealer inventory for the model.
  3. We get written offers and negotiate. Each dealer is asked for an out-the-door price in writing. Offers go against each other, and add-ons and padded fees get questioned before you see a contract.
  4. You pick. We send written recommendations and our pick of the best deal. You make the final call, sign the paperwork and pick up the car from the dealer.

Most searches take one to two weeks from payment to a signed deal. Rare specs and factory orders take longer, and we tell you that up front. We can’t promise a specific discount: the market for a given car sets what a dealer will do. The full process is on how it works.

Common questions

Do you have an office in San Francisco?

No. We’re based in Austin, Texas, and we work with Bay Area dealers by phone and email. You deal with one advisor the whole way.

Will I pay a bridge toll to pick up the car?

Maybe, depending on the bridge and the way you cross. The state-owned bridges each charge in one direction only, and the Golden Gate Bridge charges southbound. Tell us which side of the bay you live on and which bridges you’d cross, and we ask the dealers you can reach with the fewest crossings.

What if I can’t charge an electric car at home?

Ask your landlord or building manager whether you can charge where you park before you pick a car. If the answer is no, tell us and we search plug-in hybrid, hybrid and gas models instead.

Will a car I find fit and park on my San Francisco street?

Yes, if you give us your limits. Tell us the longest and widest car you can park and whether your street is steep, and we check your model against those limits before we ask any dealer for a price. On a test drive, stop on a slope and check that the parking brake holds the car.
