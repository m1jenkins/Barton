Why the same new car costs different amounts at different dealers

A new car has a sticker price, the MSRP, and people reasonably assume that settles it. It doesn’t. One dealer discounts the car, the next adds a “market adjustment” on top of sticker, and a third advertises a low number and then installs a paint sealant and a nitrogen fill you never asked for. Fees vary from store to store too.

The only way to compare is to get each dealer’s whole price in writing, the out-the-door figure with every line showing. Our guide to comparing complete new-car quotes shows how to do that yourself, and the dealership add-ons guide covers what to ask about each extra.

What we do on a new-car search

  1. Find the cars. Your advisor searches dealer inventory for the model, in the trims and colors you’d accept.
  2. Ask for written offers. Each dealer that has the car is asked for an out-the-door price in writing, so nothing gets “re-figured” when you arrive.
  3. Negotiate. We negotiate for a price below MSRP and play the written offers against each other. Add-ons, protection packages and padded fees get questioned before you see a contract.
  4. Recommend. You get written recommendations: what we found and what we’d do. You make the final call.

How far below sticker a dealer will go depends on the car. A model that sits on lots has room in it. A car with a waiting list may not, and for those the win is often getting it at sticker without the add-ons.

In-transit cars and factory orders

If the exact spec isn’t on a lot, it may be on a truck. Dealers list cars that are in transit, and those can be negotiated like any other. When nothing matching is built yet, a factory order is the remaining route. Most searches take one to two weeks from payment to a signed deal. Rare specs and factory orders take longer, and we tell you that up front.

Torn between two or three new models? That is Steer Me Right Pro: we price up to three models side by side and give you our pick, judged on out-the-door price plus resale and running costs, not just the sticker.

What stays with you

You decide which car, if any, to buy. You pick the financing and insurance, read the contract and sign it. Check with the dealer that any manufacturer rebate in the offer still applies on the day you sign, since those programs change. Our fee does not include the car, taxes, registration, financing, insurance or delivery.

Common questions

Can you get me a new car below MSRP?

We negotiate for a price below MSRP on new cars. We can’t promise a specific discount: the market for a given car sets what a dealer will do.

How much does a new car buying service cost?

Steer Me Right charges a flat $395 for one model or $695 for up to three. Most services where a person negotiates for you charge $999 or more, by their own published prices; see car buying services compared.

Can you handle a car that has to be ordered?

Yes. Rare specs and factory orders take longer than the usual one to two weeks, and we tell you that up front.

Will I still have to go to the dealership?

You sign the paperwork and pick up the car from the dealer. The price and fees are already agreed in writing by then. On Steer Me Right Pro we handle the paperwork remotely where we can.

Do dealers pay you?

No. Dealers and lenders pay us nothing. You pay the flat fee, and you’re the only one we work for.

What if I change my mind?
