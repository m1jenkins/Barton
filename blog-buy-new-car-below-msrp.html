Buying a New Car Below MSRP: Compare the Whole Quote

Original article: MasonDraft updated September 5, 2026AI-assisted revision; author acceptance and qualified consumer-finance and advertising review pending

Start here: To pursue a price below MSRP, request comparable written offers, check which discounts you qualify for, and make a specific counteroffer. A discount is not guaranteed. Judge the complete quote and your financing separately before choosing a seller.

Understand MSRP · Request a quote · Compare offers · Make a counteroffer

What does “below MSRP” actually mean?

MSRP is the manufacturer’s suggested retail price for the vehicle with its specified features. It is different from the total purchase cost, which includes other charges. The CFPB explains the components of a vehicle’s price.

Ask for the window sticker for the exact VIN. Write down the comparison amount and label whether it includes destination and factory options. Keep dealer-installed products separate. This avoids comparing a base-model advertisement against an equipped vehicle’s total sticker figure.

Do not choose a target from an unsupported claim about dealer profit, holdback, or the discount “everyone” receives. This guide uses your actual quotes, not a purported statewide average or a forecast of month-end discounts.

Request the same information from each seller

The FTC recommends confirming availability and obtaining a written out-the-door price before visiting. That total should include all charges and fees. Promotions can have eligibility restrictions, so check the conditions attached to every advertised discount.

“I’m comparing a [model year, model, trim, drivetrain] with [required options]. Please confirm the VIN, mileage, availability, and quote expiration. Send the window sticker and itemized out-the-door quote showing vehicle price, dealer discount, each rebate and its conditions, installed products, fees, taxes, registration, and any delivery charge. Please identify any financing or trade-in requirement.”

This is a reusable request template. Choose a manageable set of sellers with relevant inventory. Use the same requirements and budget so their replies answer the same question. Do not imply that you have another offer unless you do.

Check incentives without assuming they combine

  • Save the manufacturer’s current offer terms for your ZIP code and vehicle.
  • Ask what documents establish your eligibility and when the offer expires.
  • Ask whether the quote assumes loyalty, military, graduate, membership, or other qualifications.
  • Request separate scenarios if a cash rebate and promotional financing cannot be combined.
  • Have the seller identify any discount that changes if you use another lender or have no trade-in.

Compare each line before comparing the headline discount

Use these fields for each seller: VIN and equipment / sticker comparison amount / selling price / dealer discount / eligible rebates / optional products / taxes and fees / delivery / out-the-door total / quote expiry.

Hypothetical comparison: Two otherwise equivalent cars each have a $35,000 sticker comparison amount. Seller A quotes $33,000 plus a $1,500 package; Seller B quotes $33,800 with no package. Before taxes, fees, and financing, the subtotals are $34,500 and $33,800. B is $700 lower despite the smaller advertised vehicle discount. These are invented figures to illustrate comparison, not market prices or a customer result.

Then add the actual taxes, fees, and delivery quotes for each offer. Do not copy one seller’s estimate into the other column. If a field is missing, mark the quote incomplete and ask for it. Use the dealer add-on draft guide for products that need a separate decision.

Compare financing and trade-in on their own terms

Review APR, amount financed, loan length, and total payments together; a lower monthly payment can mask a longer or more expensive loan. Track trade-in value and outstanding payoff separately. The FTC financing guide explains these comparisons. This article concerns purchases; do not compare a lease payment directly with a purchase quote.

Make a precise, supportable counteroffer

Choose the vehicle and terms you would actually accept. Refer to a real competing quote only when the specification and conditions are comparable. A useful counteroffer names the requested change and asks for an updated written total.

“Your quote includes [specific product or charge]. I would like to proceed without that product at an out-the-door total of [your proposed amount], subject to confirming the vehicle and final documents. Can you send a revised itemized offer?”

The seller may decline. Decide beforehand whether another trim, timing, travel distance, or vehicle would suit you. Include travel and delivery in your own comparison; a distant advertised discount is not automatically a better choice.

Reconcile the final documents

Compare the contract with the latest quote, line by line. Recheck the VIN, selected products, rebate conditions, and any financing-dependent price. Resolve differences before signing; the FTC’s pre-signing guidance emphasizes matching the documents to the earlier written terms.
