The Dallas–Fort Worth car market right now

The Census Bureau counted 381 new-car dealer locations across the Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington metro area in 2023. S&P Global Mobility ranked the Dallas–Fort Worth market third in the country for new-vehicle registrations in the first half of 2025, behind New York and Los Angeles. The average monthly payment was $808. Dallas–Fort Worth and Houston had the highest payments among the ten markets with the most registrations, and S&P ties that to their large numbers of full-size pickups. Its 62 days’ supply was the second-lowest of the ten, after Detroit’s.

Buying a car around Dallas–Fort Worth

Ask whether the car has been hailed on

Large hail falls somewhere in the Dallas–Fort Worth area nearly every year, mostly in spring, and it leaves dents in a car’s roof and hood. Ask the dealer to say in writing whether the car has hail damage or has had hail repair. Ask for photos of the roof and hood taken outside in daylight, from a low angle, because dents show in reflections. Then have a mechanic you choose look for mismatched paint and filler. Tell us if you want to avoid hail history altogether, and we ask each dealer for that answer before we ask for a price.

Test a used car for summer heat

North Texas summers are hot enough to wear on a car. Look at the dashboard top for cracks, the headlight lenses for yellowing, the tire sidewalls for cracks and the roof paint for fading. Ask how old the battery is, and ask for an afternoon test drive with the air conditioning at full blast to see how fast the cabin cools. On a used search we can ask the dealer for photos of those spots and of the battery label. Tell us if you want us to ask for an afternoon drive.

Count the toll roads between you and the dealer

Dallas and Fort Worth sit on opposite sides of the metroplex, and the route to a dealer on the far side can run on a toll road. The North Texas Tollway Authority runs the Dallas North Tollway, the President George Bush Turnpike, the Sam Rayburn Tollway and the Chisholm Trail Parkway. Drivers without a TollTag get a ZipCash bill by mail at the higher rate. Tell us which side you live on and whether you have a TollTag, and we weigh each dealer’s price against the drive to reach it.

How it works from Dallas–Fort Worth

  1. You pay the flat fee and tell us the car. Model, trims and colors you’d accept, budget, and how far you’d go to pick it up. Mason replies within one business day to confirm the search and when it starts.
  2. We find the cars. Your advisor searches new, used and CPO dealer inventory for the model.
  3. We get written offers and negotiate. Each dealer is asked for an out-the-door price in writing. Offers go against each other, and add-ons and padded fees get questioned before you see a contract.
  4. You pick. We send written recommendations and our pick of the best deal. You make the final call, sign the paperwork and pick up the car from the dealer.

Most searches take one to two weeks from payment to a signed deal. Rare specs and factory orders take longer, and we tell you that up front. We can’t promise a specific discount: the market for a given car sets what a dealer will do. The full process is on how it works.

Common questions

Do you have an office in Dallas–Fort Worth?

No. We’re based in Austin, Texas, and we work with DFW-area dealers by phone and email. You deal with one advisor the whole way, and you pick up the car from the dealer.

Can you tell if a used car has hail damage?

Only from what the dealer sends us. We can ask for a written answer on hail damage and repair, and for daylight photos of the roof and hood, and pass them to you. Photos miss dents, so have a mechanic you choose look the car over before you sign.

What should I check on a test drive in the summer?

Run the air conditioning at full blast for the whole drive and see how fast the cabin cools. Ask how old the battery is. We can ask a dealer to schedule an afternoon drive, and a mechanic you choose can look the car over before you sign.

Can you pick dealers on my side of the metroplex?

Yes. Tell us where you live, the toll roads you are willing to use and the longest drive you would make, and we ask the dealers inside that window. Some dealers will deliver the car to you; whether they do, and what they charge, is up to them.
