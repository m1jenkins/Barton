Dealers and lenders pay us nothing.

The 2026 Silverado 1500 is the outgoing truck, and Chevrolet is paying you to take one. The trim we’d buy

A 2026 LT crew cab with the 5.3L V8, with 4WD if you need it. At $48,200 it starts $3,100 below an RST, and it has the 13.4-inch touchscreen and 12.3-inch digital gauges. The 5.3L is the engine Chevrolet puts $6,000 on this month; an LT with the TurboMax four gets $2,250. TrueCar’s averages show LTs selling about 5% under MSRP, so there’s room to ask for more, and the RST’s black-wheel look is the one to haggle on, since those average nearly 7% under.

If you tow often or drive long highway miles, get the 3.0-liter Duramax diesel. It’s rated up to 25 mpg combined (23 to 24 with 4WD) against 17 for the 5.3L, and a 2026 can tow up to 13,300 pounds. Chevrolet puts no cash on it this month, so expect to pay more for it. Skip the 6.2L V8 for now. NHTSA has an open investigation into failures of GM’s 6.2-liter that covers 2021 to 2026 trucks, including ones already fixed under a 2025 recall. The High Country is the step up for a nicer cabin; the ZR2 is for serious off-roading and costs $23,600 more than an LT.

What to watch for at the dealer

  • Which engine. Chevrolet’s $6,000 applies only to the 5.3L V8 and the $2,250 only to an LT with the TurboMax. Make sure a quote names the engine before you compare it with an ad.
  • Cash or 0%. You get one or the other. Work out the total cost of each with your bank’s rate before you choose.
  • Dealer add-ons. Chevrolet dealers put add-ons on 32.4% of the quotes CarWhere studied, averaging $1,443. Paint protection, nitrogen and etched VINs are the dealer’s products. Refuse or negotiate them.
  • Fees. Documentation fees run from about $85 in California to $999 in Florida, by CarEdge’s count. Compare out-the-door prices.
  • The 6.2L V8. On a 2021 to 2024 truck, ask for proof that recall 25V274’s inspection was done. NHTSA’s open investigation also covers 2025 and 2026 engines.
  • The transfer case recall. A few 2026 4WD trucks are under recall 26V289, and GM tells owners not to drive them until fixed. Have the dealer check the VIN before delivery.

Common questions

Is the Chevrolet Silverado 1500 a good truck?

Reviewers are lukewarm on the 2026 and warmer on the 2027. U.S. News scores the 2026 an 8.8 out of 10, fifth of six full-size pickups, Kelley Blue Book gives it 4.3 out of 5, and J.D. Power gives it 82 out of 100, tied for third among large light-duty pickups. Edmunds is cooler on the 2026, at 6.1, but rates the redesigned 2027 a 7.7 and second of five large trucks. Consumer Reports expects the 2026 to be less reliable than the average new car, and iSeeCars finds it loses 30.4% of its value in three years, against 23.7% for full-size pickups.

How much under MSRP can you get a Chevrolet Silverado 1500?

On a 2026 LT, RST, Custom Trail Boss, LTZ or LT Trail Boss, 5–7% under MSRP is a fair deal and 8–10% a strong one. High Country and ZR2 trucks sit at 5–7%, and the WT and Custom have less room, about 2–3%. Those ranges are our read of TrueCar’s market averages and iSeeCars’ invoice figures, and Chevrolet’s cash comes on top.

Should I buy a 2026 or a 2027 Silverado 1500?

The 2026 if price matters most. It costs less (the High Country starts $8,500 lower), the RST and LTZ exist only as 2026s, and Chevrolet’s cash and 0% offers are on the 2026. The 2027 brings 5.7L and 6.6L V8s, a 10-speed automatic on every engine and a redesigned cabin, and Edmunds scores it 7.7 against 6.1 for the 2026, but we found no Chevrolet offer on it yet.

What is the destination charge on a Silverado 1500?

$2,795 on the 2026 and the 2027. Chevrolet’s advertised starting prices leave it out.

Does Chevrolet offer cash back on the Silverado 1500?

Yes, on the V8. On November 16, 2026, Chevrolet offered $6,000 ($4,250 plus $1,750 bonus cash) on a 2026 Silverado 1500 with the 5.3L V8 and $2,250 on an LT with the TurboMax, or 0% for 60 months, in Chicago, Dallas, Los Angeles and New York. The cash can’t be combined with special financing or a lease, and all of it ends with delivery by November 30.

Is the Silverado’s 6.2L V8 reliable?

It has a record. GM recalled nearly 600,000 2021 to 2024 trucks and SUVs with the 6.2L in 2025 for crankshaft and connecting-rod defects that can cause engine failure, and NHTSA has since opened a wider investigation covering 2021 to 2026 engines, including ones already fixed. Until that closes, we’d take the 5.3L V8 or the diesel.

How much can a Silverado 1500 tow?

Chevrolet lists up to 13,300 pounds on the 2026 and up to 13,700 on the 2027 with the new 5.7L V8. The 2026 diesel is the one it advertises for the 13,300 figure; what a specific truck can tow depends on its engine, cab, axle ratio and hitch, so check the trailering rating on the truck’s own label.

Is the Silverado 1500 the same truck as the Sierra?

They’re closely related and offer the same four engines. The Silverado costs less (the 2026 WT starts at $37,200, against $38,600 for a Sierra Pro) and is finished a step lower inside. Chevrolet puts its November cash on the V8, so price both and let the better deal decide.
