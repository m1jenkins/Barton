New 2026

Best when: Most buyers. Toyota’s financing, the $1,000 cash and the leases apply to the 2026, it costs $640 to $4,530 less than a 2027, and its ToyotaCare covers two years or 25,000 miles.

Check: That the truck is a 2026 and not a 2027, and that the offer runs in your region.

New 2027

Best when: The 2026 you want is gone, or you want the 14-inch touchscreen and built-in dash camera Toyota makes standard for 2027.

Check: The price rise, that its 3.99% and $1,000 cash run only in Southern California and the New York area, and that ToyotaCare drops to one year or 10,000 miles.

CPO (Toyota Certified Gold)

Best when: You want a 2024 or 2025 with a warranty, and you’re paying cash.

Check: That it’s Gold Certified (7 years or 100,000 miles of powertrain cover from purchase), not Silver (12 months or 12,000 miles), and that every engine recall is done. A hybrid battery stays covered for 10 years or 150,000 miles from first sale.

Used, not certified

Best when: Your budget is under about $40,000; a 2022 asks a median $39,079.

Check: Open recalls by VIN, above all the engine campaigns (main bearings on 2022 to 2024 trucks, a camshaft on 2022, 2023, 2025 and 2026 trucks), and an independent inspection.

If you buy a 2022 to 2026 Tundra, get the engine recall work in writing. Main-bearing recalls cover certain 2022 to 2024 trucks, and camshaft recall 26V750 covers 2022 to 2023 and 2025 to 2026 trucks. Both fixes are free.

What to watch for at the dealer

  • Engine recalls. Four NHTSA campaigns cover 2022 to 2026 trucks: three for main bearings and 26V750 for a camshaft. Ask the dealer to show each applicable one is done.
  • Toyota financing. The rates and leases need Toyota Financial Services, and the $1,000 cash doesn’t combine with them. Compare the total cost with your bank’s rate.
  • The model year. A 2026 and a 2027 look alike on the lot. In Chicago and Dallas only the 2026 has offers.
  • Fuel economy. Edmunds measured 15.2 mpg on its test route against the EPA’s 19 for a 4WD Tundra. Budget for the real figure if you tow or drive in town.
  • Towing and payload. Toyota’s top figures are 12,000 pounds and 1,850 pounds. Check the rating for your exact cab, bed and engine on the door sticker.

Is the Toyota Tundra a good truck?

Mostly yes, with caveats. U.S. News gives the 2026 a 9.0, third of six full-size pickups, and J.D. Power’s owner score is 82. Edmunds is cooler at 7.0, fourth of five large trucks, and faults its fuel economy, thick front pillars and mid-pack towing.

How much under MSRP can you get a Toyota Tundra?

On a 2026 SR through 1794 Edition, 5–7% under MSRP is fair and 8–9% strong. The TRD Pro sells near sticker, around 3–4%. That’s our read of TrueCar, Edmunds and CarWhere figures. Toyota’s $1,000 cash comes on top, but not with its low rates.

Should I buy a 2026 or a 2027 Tundra?

The 2026, for most buyers. It costs $640 to $4,530 less, nearly all of Toyota’s offers are on it, and its ToyotaCare runs two years against one. The 2027 adds a standard 14-inch touchscreen and a built-in dash camera.

What is the destination charge on a Tundra?

$2,195. Toyota’s advertised starting prices leave it out, so a $41,260 SR is $43,455 on the window sticker.

Does Toyota offer cash back on the Tundra?

Yes: $1,000 customer cash on 2026 gas and i-FORCE MAX trims, except the TRD Pro, in all four regions we checked on November 18, 2026. It doesn’t combine with Toyota’s 1.99% or 2.99% financing or its leases.

Is the Tundra i-FORCE MAX hybrid worth it?

Only if you want its power. It makes 437 hp and 583 lb-ft against 389 hp and 479 lb-ft, adds $3,700 to a Limited and is rated 20 mpg combined against 19 in 4x4. Toyota’s 1.99% reaches it in all four regions we checked.

Is a used Tundra a better deal than new?

Rarely. A used 2024 asks a median $44,714, while TrueCar’s average paid for a new 2026 SR5 is $45,885. Used loans averaged 11.19% against 1.99% on a new hybrid. A Gold Certified 2024 or 2025 with engine recalls done is the used route we’d take.
