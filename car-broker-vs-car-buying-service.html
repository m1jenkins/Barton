Car broker or car buying service? Ask who pays before you hire one

By Mason · Updated November 15, 2026

Short answer: “Car broker” and “auto broker” get used for anyone who finds and negotiates a car for you, but the people behind the name are paid in different ways. Many brokers are paid by the dealer that sells the car. A car buying service like Steer Me Right is paid only by you: a flat $395 for one model (the Steer Me Right plan) or $695 for up to three (Steer Me Right Pro). Dealers and lenders pay us nothing. Before you hire either one, ask who pays them.

What a car broker does

A car broker is a go-between. You say what you want, the broker finds a car through the dealers it knows, and it may handle the price talk and the paperwork too. Chase’s guide to car brokers describes them as often “former dealers who have shifted to working more directly with buyers.”

The catch is the pay. The same Chase guide says brokers may “have a financial affiliation with certain sellers and may receive a commission on sales.” Experian puts it this way: some brokers charge a small fee or none, “but they may receive a commission for steering business to certain car dealers.” Car Pal, a buyer-paid service, goes further in its FAQ: “A car broker is someone who gets paid by a dealership to help facilitate a vehicle purchase.”

None of that makes a broker dishonest. It does mean a free or cheap broker has a second customer, and that customer is the dealer.

Chase’s guide makes the same split. It calls the buyer-only kind a car-buying concierge and says such services are “unlikely to have any potential conflicts of interest that a car broker receiving commissions from a dealer might have.”

Five questions to ask a car broker or a car buying service

  1. Who pays you? If any money comes from a dealer or lender, you are not the only customer.
  2. Is your fee flat? Chase notes that broker fees can be “hourly, flat fee, or commission based on the value of the vehicle,” and suggests looking for flat fees. A fee that rises with the car’s price rewards a higher price.
  3. Which dealers will you contact? A short list of partner dealers means fewer offers to compare.
  4. Will I see the offers in writing? You should be able to read each dealer’s out-the-door number yourself, with the fees listed.
  5. What happens if I cancel? Get the refund rule before you pay.

Common questions

What is an auto broker?

An auto broker, or car broker, is a go-between who finds a car for a buyer and may negotiate the deal. Many are paid by the dealer that sells the car, some by the buyer, and some by both. Ask which before you agree to anything.

Is a car broker the same as a car buying service?

People use the words for the same job, but the pay differs. A buyer-paid car buying service takes a flat fee from you and nothing from dealers. A broker is often paid by the dealer. Steer Me Right is a buyer-paid service, not a broker.

Are car brokers free?

Some are free to the buyer because a dealer pays them. That can be a fair trade for the convenience. It also means the person finding your car earns money from the seller.

How much does a car broker charge?

It depends on who pays. A dealer-paid broker may charge you little or nothing. Buyer-paid services charge flat fees; Steer Me Right is $395 for one model or $695 for up to three. We list other published fees in what a car buying service costs.

Is it worth using a car broker or buying service?

It can be if you dislike haggling or don’t have the hours for dealer calls and follow-ups. Nobody can honestly promise a set discount. What you are buying is time back and someone checking the out-the-door number before you sign.
